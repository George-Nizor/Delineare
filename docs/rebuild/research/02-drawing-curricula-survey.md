## 1. The consensus progression, and where curricula disagree

**What nearly everyone agrees on**, as six stages:
1. **Mark control.**
   - Drawabox Lesson 1 (https://drawabox.com/lessons).
   - Proko Drawing Basics opens with "Getting Started", then "Lines" (https://www.proko.com/course/drawing-basics/lessons).
   - Robertson's chapter 1 (https://www.parkablogs.com/comment/13340).
   - Peter Han's Dynamic Sketching week 1 (https://cgmasteracademy.com/courses/66-dynamic-sketching-1.html).
2. **Seeing: contour, measuring, negative space.**
   - Weeks 1–3 in every public syllabus found: Youngstown State (https://catalog.ysu.edu/syllabi/2026-fall/art-1521-01-40519/syllabi-202640-art-1521-01-40519.pdf), Georgia (https://bulletin.uga.edu/Course/Details/5301), Lamar (https://www2.lamarpa.edu/syllabus/Fall%202024/Wilbur%20Mary%20Catherine/ARTS-1316-01%20-%20Drawing%20I%20Syllabus.html), Clark (https://www.clark.edu/cce/class-resources/supply-lists/beginning-drawing-syllabus-spring2025.pdf).
   - Edwards' five skills (https://www.drawright.com/theory).
   - Dodson chapters 1–3 (https://elib.lasalle.edu.sg/cgi-bin/spydus.exe/FULL/WPAC/BIBENQ/104042811/51316).
   - Ctrl+Paint's Traditional Drawing order: measuring, contour, block-in, negative space (https://www.ctrlpaint.com/library/).
3. **Simple forms and construction.**
   - Vilppu weeks 2–5: sphere, box, cylinder (https://vilppuacademy.com/products/figure-drawing-1).
   - Watts Phase I basic shapes (https://www.wattsatelier.com/course-previews/drawing/).
   - Loomis, *Successful Drawing*: cube, then round forms, then perspective (https://archive.org/details/andrew-loomis-successful-drawing).
4. **Perspective.**
   - Weeks 4–5 at Youngstown, Lamar and Clark.
   - Week 2 at BYU-Idaho (https://courses.byui.edu/art110_new/art110/).
   - Robertson chapters 2–5.
5. **Value and light.**
   - Weeks 5–7 in the syllabi.
   - Proko places it after perspective.
   - Vilppu weeks 8–10.
6. **Figure.** The order is gesture, then structure or mannequin, then landmarks and proportion, then shading:
   - Proko Figure Drawing Fundamentals (https://www.proko.com/course/figure-drawing-fundamentals/lessons).
   - Loomis, *Figure Drawing for All It's Worth* (https://archive.org/details/loomis_FIGURE_draw).
   - Hampton: gesture → landmarks → forms → head → anatomy → limbs → drapery (https://library-catalog.jbu.edu/bib/116466, seen through search only).

After that comes **application**: plants, animals, objects, environments (Drawabox Lessons 3–7; Robertson chapters 7–9).

**Where they genuinely disagree:**

- **Observation first or construction first.**
  - *Observation first:* ateliers. Florence Academy starts with Bargue copies and sight-size, then cast drawing (https://www.florenceacademyofart.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Certificate-Programs-Course-Catalogue-2024.pdf). Grand Central Atelier's order is Bargue, then tipped casts, then figure (https://grandcentralatelier.org/?p=89). Edwards and the university courses also start here.
  - *Construction first:* Drawabox, Robertson, Han, and Concept Design Academy, whose "everyone should start here" core is perspective plus Dynamic Sketching (http://www.conceptdesignacad.com/curriculum).
  - Drawabox's position: observational drawing without construction is "fundamentally incorrect… you may eventually become an exceptional photocopier" (https://drawabox.com/lesson/2/3).
  - The evidence supports doing both:
    - Misperceiving the subject is the main source of drawing error (Cohen and Bennett 1997, https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-1523.23.3.609), so observation needs explicit looking and measuring drills.
    - Drawing from imagination needs spatial construction.
  - **I run both strands from lesson 1.**
- **Gesture early or late.**
  - *Early:* Nicolaides puts it on day two (https://learningtodrawlearningtosee.blogspot.com/p/the-natural-way-to-draw-example-schedule.html). Proko Figure Drawing Fundamentals and Vilppu both start with it. Art Prof spends its first two levels on timed gestures (https://artprof.org/learn/tracks/drawing-basics-track/).
  - *Late:* Austin CC leaves it to weeks 12–15 (https://lighthouse.apps.austincc.edu/faculty/syllabus?id=551822). Watts puts Head before Figure (https://www.wattsatelier.com/online/drawing-program/).
  - **I put it early, as a short daily drill.**
- **When perspective comes.**
  - Drawabox teaches it on day one, while saying it "is NOT a perspective course… training… your instincts" (https://drawabox.com/lesson/1/6).
  - Georgia leaves it to weeks 10–11.
  - Edwards treats it as sighting a box on day 3 (https://www.drawright.com/online-drawing-workshop-overview).
- **Value before or after form.**
  - *Value first:* Austin CC (week 1), NMA Drawing Foundation I (value scales before 3D forms, https://nma.art/courses/a-beginners-guide-to-drawing/), Watts (values before basic shapes).
  - *Value after form:* Proko, Vilppu, BYU-Idaho.
  - *No value at all:* Drawabox, which says "we do not touch upon any lighting, rendering, or shading" (https://drawabox.com/lesson/0/1).
  - **My split:** seeing value can come any time; the logic of light on form needs form first.
- **Volume of practice.**
  - *High:* Drawabox's 250 boxes take "weeks, or even months" (https://drawabox.com/lesson/250boxes). Nicolaides aims at about 4,000 gestures (https://starkickfall.com/2021/01/15/im-40-through-kimon-nicolaides-the-natural-way-to-draw/).
  - *Against grinding:* Drawabox's own "Do not grind" (https://drawabox.com/lesson/1/4).
- **When imagination starts.**
  - *From the start:* Drawabox's 50% rule (https://drawabox.com/lesson/0/2) and Lecoq's memory training, which begins with a nose outline (https://archive.org/details/TheTrainingOfTheMemoryInArtAndTheEducationOfTheArtist).
  - *After years:* the atelier route.

## 2 and 3. Proposed sequence: 37 lessons in 6 parts, with resources

Each lesson below gives what the learner can do after it, its prerequisites, the best free resources, and a paid option only where one is clearly better. Free resources are video unless marked. Before Lesson 1 comes an untimed baseline and placement set (see section 5); it is not counted as a lesson.

**Part 1: Line and looking**

1. **Confident lines.**
   - After it: draws ghosted straight lines and arcs from the shoulder that land on both target dots.
   - Needs: nothing.
   - Free: Drawabox "Ghosted Lines" (page plus 10:32 video): https://drawabox.com/lesson/1/ghostedlines and https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LkJG6pKTuRc. Also "Drawing From Your Wrist and Shoulder" (9:35): https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0_AdsK8x9Lw.
2. **Freehand ellipses.**
   - After it: draws ellipses of a chosen degree and axis that fit a given space.
   - Needs: 1.
   - Free: Drawabox "Ellipses" (12:48), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tHJ3rzk6kno, with the page https://drawabox.com/lesson/1/tablesofellipses. Robertson "page 018: freehand ellipses" (9:14): https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_xFEV2uOp1M.
3. **Contour.**
   - After it: draws an object's real edges by following them with the eye rather than drawing a symbol.
   - Needs: nothing.
   - Free: Ctrl+Paint "Drawing Shape: Contour" (3:58), https://www.ctrlpaint.com/videos/drawing-shape-contour. Art Prof blind contour (1:36), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d_aMCHRyELQ.
4. **Measuring angles and proportions.**
   - After it: sights angles and ratios at arm's length to within about 10%.
   - Needs: 3.
   - Free: Proko "How to Draw Accurate Proportions" (12:04), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1J7RQvKnWf4, and "Drawing Measuring Techniques" (13:23), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tZtnKp4fAPk. Ctrl+Paint "Visual Measuring" (6:01), https://www.ctrlpaint.com/videos/visual-measuring.
5. **Block-in and negative space.**
   - After it: starts any observed subject with a straight-line envelope, big shapes and negative shapes, then subdivides.
   - Needs: 4.
   - Free: Ctrl+Paint "Linear Block-in" (4:05), https://www.ctrlpaint.com/videos/drawing-shape-linear-block-in, and "Negative Space" (4:37), https://www.ctrlpaint.com/videos/drawing-shape-negative-space. Robertson "Blocking-In Proportions" (19:54), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WXj9R49EwlI.
   - Paid: Eviston's *The Drawing Laboratory* ($279, 23 h). Its Part 1 is exactly this skill: angles, enveloping, block-in. https://www.proko.com/course/the-drawing-laboratory
6. **Gesture.**
   - After it: catches a person's or animal's action in 30 seconds to 2 minutes with a few C, S and straight lines.
   - Needs: 1.
   - Free: Proko "How to Draw Gesture – Step by Step" (14:55), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8j39NqwL7s4. The main lecture is age-gated on YouTube (sign-in required); its notes are at https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/how-to-draw-gesture. Hampton "The 16 Line Gesture Method" (19:30), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tSyGOZjTs5A.
   - Paid: Proko Figure Drawing Fundamentals ($150).
7. **Checking and correcting.**
   - After it: finds errors with a mirror or flipped image, distance, plumb lines, a photo overlay and a fixed checklist, and restates lines rather than erasing them.
   - Needs: 4 and 5.
   - Free: Florent Farges "How to Evaluate Yourself as a Self-Taught Artist" (10:02), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SGJ_uBtK__M. Ctrl+Paint "Critique", https://www.ctrlpaint.com/videos/critique.
8. **Visual memory.**
   - After it: studies a simple subject for 1–2 minutes, draws it from memory, compares, then corrects from memory.
   - Needs: 3.
   - Free: Proko "How to Improve Your Visual Memory" (6:26), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XDLJAfDR4fM. Drawabox "Observation and Memory" (6:26), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QW-YPV3SDn0. Lecoq's 1911 book (public domain, text): https://archive.org/details/TheTrainingOfTheMemoryInArtAndTheEducationOfTheArtist.

**Part 2: Form and space**

9. **Simple forms and overlap.**
   - After it: draws spheres, boxes, cylinders and cones that sit in depth through overlap, size and placement.
   - Needs: 1 and 2.
   - Free: Proko "Structure Basics" (11:11), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3uEtdDvK6Xo. Proko/Vandruff "The Secret of Simple Forms" (9:08), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qGs8-xBForM.
10. **Contour lines on organic forms.**
    - After it: wraps contour lines around sausages and arrows, with the degree shift, so they read as 3D.
    - Needs: 2 and 9.
    - Free: Drawabox "Organic Arrows" (9:50), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B_iaMu-crZk, and "Sausages with Contour Lines" (12:17), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y5By0Q_XFBM, with the page https://drawabox.com/lesson/2/contourlines. Ctrl+Paint "Form, Not Shape", https://www.ctrlpaint.com/videos/form-not-shape.
11. **Horizon and one-point perspective.**
    - After it: sets eye level and a vanishing point, and places boxes above, below and across the horizon.
    - Needs: 9.
    - Free: Proko "Perspective for Beginners" (13:56), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BYlW8XC0MlI. Ctrl+Paint "Horizon Line and Viewpoint", https://www.ctrlpaint.com/videos/horizon-line-and-viewpoint.
    - Paid, and clearly the deepest option: Vandruff's *The Perspective Course*, Part 1 ($139, 114 lessons), https://www.proko.com/course/the-perspective-course/lessons.
12. **Two- and three-point boxes, plotted with a ruler.**
    - After it: constructs boxes and understands foreshortening and vanishing-point spacing.
    - Needs: 11.
    - Free: Drawabox's two Part 3 videos (5:42 and 6:09), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XhDWiPARouY and https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tH6kpY6lYUw, with the page https://drawabox.com/lesson/1/plottedperspective. Proko × Vandruff free playlist (23 videos): https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLtG4P3lq8RHFoefyASDnGxFtsjxpelkTK.
13. **Freehand boxes.**
    - After it: draws boxes freehand at any rotation, draws through them, and checks convergence with line extensions.
    - Needs: 12.
    - Free: Drawabox "Rotated Boxes" (17:55), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Oz98L4Fyxoo. The 250 Box Challenge, https://drawabox.com/lesson/250boxes, with its video (12:14), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ltbHkgPiQZo.
14. **Ellipses in perspective and cylinders.**
    - After it: draws cylinders at any angle, with the minor axis on the cylinder's axis.
    - Needs: 2 and 13.
    - Free: Proko "How to Draw Cylinders and Ellipses" (14:35), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PdtDtGBu-Dc. Drawabox's 250 Cylinder Challenge, https://drawabox.com/lesson/250cylinders.
15. **Constructing objects.**
    - After it: builds an object from primitives, drawn through, with additions, cuts and form intersections.
    - Needs: 10, 13 and 14.
    - Free: Drawabox "Form Intersections" (29:00), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VuorfzSk33Y. Lesson 6 intro (34:55), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TN8ZxKA-6jw. Ctrl+Paint "Constructive Form pt. 1", https://www.ctrlpaint.com/videos/constructive-form-pt-1.
    - Paid: Robertson's *How to Draw*, https://designstudiopress.com/product/how-to-draw.
16. **Rotating an object in the mind.**
    - After it: draws an observed object from three views it has not seen, using a plan and elevation.
    - Needs: 15.
    - Free: Dan Beardshaw "Rotating Objects in Perspective" (10:14), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ja4W5P7K5PE. Ctrl+Paint "Real Object, Imaginary Details", https://www.ctrlpaint.com/videos/real-object-imaginary-details. Proko "Learning to Sketch from Imagination" (26:28), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-Ww0BXehQ5E.

**Part 3: Light and value**

17. **Seeing value.**
    - After it: makes a 9-step scale, and squints any subject down to 2–3 value shapes (notan).
    - Needs: 5.
    - Free: Proko "Intro to Value Drawing" (8:40), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a0uaqmY04OI. "Train Your Eyes to See Values" (8:26), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RFQtsXktc9g. Notan studies (7:47), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ACJqXFyFVQw.
18. **Light on form.**

    - Needs: 9 and 17.
    - Free: Proko "Shading Light and Form – Basics" (7:15), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V3WmrWUEIJo, and "How to Shade a Drawing" (12:01), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-dqGkHWC5IU. Sycra's light and shadow playlist (17 videos, 2.5 h): https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLV2X3tgajVlHkH3FHxm3rLZWqScFTRhtv.
19. **Cast shadows.**
    - After it: constructs the shadows of boxes and cylinders from both sun and lamp positions.
    - Needs: 12 and 18.
    - Free: simply design sketching "How to sketch cast shadows" (8:56), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tcvp2zcScn8. Proko "Shadow Mapping Basics" (8:26), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QNDJZSpyRLI. BYU-Idaho text page, https://courses.byui.edu/art110_new/art110/week02/cast_shadows_2pt.html (its videos sit behind a login).

    - Needs: 5, 7, 18 and 20.
    - Free: Ctrl+Paint "Simple Still Life", https://www.ctrlpaint.com/videos/drawing-shape-simple-still-life. Oakton College demo (7:49), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FDiPyDMVWVo. Fine Art Academy (72 min), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VZec6WoA7wg.

**What I cut, and why:**
- **Colour, painting and digital:** out of scope, as briefed.
- **Media survey** (charcoal, wet media, pastel), which universities spend weeks on: graphite and pen carry every skill above.
- **Bargue copying and sight-size as lessons.**
  - Florence Academy needs two quarters of 15-hour weeks for three plates.
  - Sight-size needs a fixed spot about six feet back (https://www.artac.org/post/the-sight-size-method-a-critical-overview-by-semyon-bilmes).
  - Both become optional ongoing master copies, using the public-domain plates (https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Bargue-G%C3%A9r%C3%B4me_Drawing_Course_I).
- **Specialised subjects** (insects, vehicles, the wheel and texture challenges): construction transfers to them, and Lesson 15 covers it.
- **Muscle-by-muscle anatomy** (Proko: 371 lessons, 77 h): reduced to Lesson 29.
- **Advanced perspective** (curvilinear, inclined planes, reflections): only 3-point survives, inside Lesson 12.
- **Edges as their own topic:** folded into Lesson 21.
- **Caricature, comics, style and the character-design process:** beyond "drawing convincingly".
- **Upside-down drawing as an accuracy method, and the hemisphere theory:** dropped. No person-level left- or right-brain phenotype exists (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3743825/), and observational skill went with left-cerebellum grey matter (https://research.gold.ac.uk/id/eprint/20007/). Cohen and Earls (2010) found inversion did not help accuracy; I could not link-verify that paper before the search budget ran out.

## 4. Techniques that work, and where they come from

- **Ghosting: plan, ghost, execute.** It applies to "every single mark… that includes ellipses". Drawabox credits it to Robertson (https://drawabox.com/lesson/1/ghostedlines).
- **Shoulder, then confidence before accuracy.** Drawabox's rules are continuous, smooth, consistent (https://drawabox.com/lesson/1/2, https://drawabox.com/lesson/1/3). Superimposed lines and tables of ellipses serve as warm-ups.
- **Drawing through, plus line extensions.** All 12 edges of each box are drawn, and the edges are extended to check convergence after every page (https://drawabox.com/lesson/250boxes).
- **The 50% rule.** At most half your time on study and at least half on drawing for its own sake. "No timer needed" (https://drawabox.com/lesson/0/2). Ignoring it is the main cause of burnout in Drawabox's own forums (https://drawabox.com/community/submission/CQHXYMSX).
- **Warm-ups.** Drawabox: 2–3 earlier exercises, 10–15 minutes (https://drawabox.com/lesson/0/3). Proko: a warm-up for each module.
- **Fixed quotas, no grinding** (https://drawabox.com/lesson/1/4).
- **Timed gesture ladders.**
  - Nicolaides 1B alternates 30-minute gesture blocks with contour work.
  - Line of Action's 30-minute class runs 10×30 s, 5×1 min, 2×5 min, 1×10 min (https://line-of-action.com/practice-tools).
  - Vilppu: "Why Quick Poses Shouldn't Be Fast!" (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GGgWHiORNfk).
- **Contour versus cross-contour.**
  - Pure or blind contour: eyes on the edge, slow, never looking at the paper (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blind_contour_drawing). Dodson's variant is short look-and-draw bursts (https://midnakit.wordpress.com/2016/06/28/keys-to-drawing-chapter-1/). Use it as a looking drill.
  - Cross-contour describes volume (https://drawabox.com/lesson/2/contourlines).
  - Frequent glances go with accuracy: "Look little, look often" (https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03193626).
- **Comparative measurement.** Take a unit from the subject and keep the arm straight. Errors accumulate (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparative_measurement).
- **Block-in.** Envelope first, then subdivide: Ctrl+Paint, and Grand Central's tipped casts.
- **Sight-size.** One-to-one scale from a fixed viewpoint, taught on Bargue copies at Florence Academy. Bilmes argues it "breeds a dependence on the model"; note that he runs a rival school.
- **Memory then check (Lecoq).**
  1. Learn a simple drawing.
  2. Draw it from memory.
  3. The differences are pointed out.
  4. Correct it from memory only.
  5. Grade the difficulty upward ("exactly equal to, but never beyond" the pupil's strength).
  - Kim Jung Gi says 60–70% of an image is "already stored" and must be refreshed (https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/lifestyle/arts-theater/20210513/interview-creative-spontaneity-realized-through-memory-in-kim-jung-gis-live-drawings).
- **Master copies.**
  - Bargue plates.
  - Huston ends each chapter with an Old Master analysis (https://www.parkablogs.com/content/book-review-figure-drawing-artists-making-every-mark-count).
  - Vandruff's "Collecting Masters" assignment (https://www.proko.com/course/the-perspective-course/lessons).
- **Overlay critique.**
  - Proko publishes free critique videos, for example "Critique – Simplify Pear from Observation", 1h25m (https://www.proko.com/course/drawing-basics/lessons).
  - Schoolism LIVE gives personalised paint-overs (https://schoolism.com/pricing).
  - For a solo learner, the photo overlay is the substitute. Show it after drawing, not during.
- **Self-critique checklists.**
  - Loomis's five Ps (proportion, placement, perspective, planes, pattern) and five Cs (https://archive.org/details/andrew-loomis-successful-drawing; copyright disputed, so cite the Titan edition).
  - Dodson's 8 self-critiques (https://parkablogs.com/content/book-review-keys-drawing).
  - Florence Academy's pass test on a Bargue: outline, shadow line, proportion, shadow shape.
  - Huston's chunking: proportion, then shadow shapes, then value, then edges (https://www.proko.com/lesson/simplified-gesture-and-structure-with-steve-huston).
- **Technique beats volume.** "Strategic" learners outdrew "surface" learners who simply logged more hours (https://doi.org/10.1037/aca0000011).

## 5. Handling mixed experience levels

**What the schools actually do:**
- **Drawabox: no skipping.** "No matter how skilled or experienced you are, start at Lesson 0" (https://drawabox.com/lessons). Two softer rules exist:
  - Self-study students may use lessons "in whatever order" (https://drawabox.com/r/artfundamentals/user/uncomfortable/comments/106).
  - A returning student may redo Lesson 1 and then only 50 boxes (https://drawabox.com/r/artfundamentals/post/rr7iu2).
- **Proko: no placement test.** Projects come in Level 1 and Level 2 versions, and "you can go through the course twice" (https://www.proko.com/course/drawing-basics/lessons).
- **Concept Design Academy:** "Everyone should start here" and "take foundational classes multiple times".
- **Florence Academy:** advanced standing needs prior atelier training on the same exercises, capped at 37.5 credits (https://www.florenceacademyofart.com/certificate-programs/application-procedures/).
- **Universities:** credit by AP score or portfolio (https://art.uga.edu/academics/advanced-placement-credit).
- **NMA:** a "MasterPlan" intake form (search snippet only, unverified).
- **r/learnart's difficulty rule:** below 50% success, scale back; above 75%, ramp up (http://web.archive.org/web/20260527165814/https://old.reddit.com/r/learnart/wiki/faq).

**Recommendation:** placement by drawing, not by self-report.
- Ask for Edwards-style pre-instruction drawings (https://www.drawright.com/before-after) plus a 6-task test. Each task takes 10–15 minutes and has a pass check:
  1. Ten ghosted lines landing on their dots.
  2. A measured contour drawing of a mug, overlay error under 10%.
  3. Five freehand boxes whose line extensions converge.
  4. A shaded sphere with its cast shadow.
  5. Three 2-minute gestures and one mannequin.
  6. A Loomis head at three-quarter view.
- Pass a task and that part's lessons are tested out, but their drills stay in the warm-up rotation (Drawabox's reason for not skipping).
- Give every assignment two levels (Proko).
- Repeat the baseline at the end of each part.

## 6. Practice material each source provides, and 10–30 minute formats

**Practice material, by source:**
- **Drawabox:** fixed homework quotas, for example Lesson 1 totals 17 pages. Five challenges: 250 boxes, 250 cylinders, 25 textures, 25 wheels, 100 chests. Warm-ups drawn from earlier lessons.
- **Proko:** warm-ups, two-level projects, free critique videos.
- **Ctrl+Paint:** light homework, for example "Draw 100" (https://www.ctrlpaint.com/videos/draw-100) and overlaying form lines on bug photos.
- **Art Prof:** a daily 10-minute sketchbook and 2/5/10-minute animal and human gesture sets.
- **Nicolaides:** 15-hour schedules.
- **Tulp:** 63 daily workouts of about 30 minutes (https://schoolism.com/courses/drawing/dimensional-drawing-workout-wouter-tulp).
- **Marc Brunet:** 30 days of daily form exercises (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K5AV42mgAe0).
- **Reference tools:**
  - Line of Action: figures, animals, hands, faces, environments; class mode.
  - SketchDaily: the same class ladders (https://www.sketchdaily.net/).
  - Quickposes: 20-image challenges (https://quickposes.com/en/gestures/timed).
  - Inktober prompts (https://inktober.com/rules).
- **Avoid Croquis Cafe:** its channel is now unrelated and the domain redirects elsewhere.

**Formats that fit 10–30 minutes, any time:**
- A 10-minute warm-up of two earlier drills, for example a ghosted-line page plus five rotated boxes.
- The 30-minute gesture ladder.
- Memory loop: study 2 minutes, draw from memory 5, overlay, correct from memory 3.
- One object drawn from life, then from two unseen angles.
- A row of the 25-texture challenge.
- Six 3-value thumbnails from a film still.
- One mannequin pose from imagination, then checked against reference.
- A 15-minute location sketch.
- A Bargue plate or master figure copied in 30-minute sessions.
- A 10-minute retest of the lesson's check, done days later.
