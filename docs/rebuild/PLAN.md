# Delineare rebuild plan

A plan to rebuild Delineare from scratch, written 2026-10-14 for the owner's review. Only the pencil
mark carries over from the first build unchanged. Everything else here is a proposal until the owner
agrees to it.

Read with:
- `COURSE.md`: the 34 lessons.
- `WRITING.md`: how every word gets written.
- `sample-lesson-01.md`: the first lesson in the new format.
- `research/`: the three reports this plan rests on.

## What Delineare is for

Delineare teaches people to draw, whatever their level, from one place. For each skill it shows
what to do and what it looks like done well, and points to the best teacher's lesson on it. Then it
gives practice material to use whenever the learner wants. The course ends when someone can draw convincingly from
observation and from imagination.

It is a course with a practice library beside it. It is not a daily-session planner, and it is not
an encyclopedia of everything about drawing.

## What the research found

1. **Learners get stuck in the same places** (`research/03`, part B):
   - not knowing where to start;
   - too many resources;
   - not being able to tell whether they are improving;
   - the gap between drawing from reference and drawing from imagination;
   - burning out on huge quotas such as the 250 Box Challenge.
2. **Most drawing errors come from misjudging the subject, not from the hand** (Cohen and Bennett
   1997, `research/03` A5). Lessons have to teach where to look and how to check a drawing, as well
   as how to make marks.
3. **A page teaches best when it is short and puts the picture beside the words** (Mayer's
   principles, `research/03` A4 and C):
   - one skill per page;
   - an example before the rule;
   - each step beside its image;
   - a wrong-and-right pair;
   - an exercise, then a short check.
4. **Beginners need every step; experienced learners need the goal and the check** (worked examples
   and expertise reversal, `research/03` A3).
5. **Drawing from memory is the best-supported route to drawing from imagination** (`research/03`
   A5, `research/02` section 4): study a subject, hide it, draw it, compare, then correct from memory.
6. **Practise one skill on its own at first, then mix and space it** (`research/03` A2). Streaks
   and broken-streak warnings make people quit (A7). Seeing your own pages side by side over time is
   the progress signal that works (A6, part B).
7. **Serious curricula agree on an order** (`research/02` section 1): mark control, seeing, simple
   forms, perspective, value, the figure, then application. They disagree on observation versus
   construction first. The course runs both from the start.
8. **The first build is mostly unusable, with some good raw material** (`research/01`):
   - Worth keeping: 594 concrete practice items, about 150 verified resources and two working 3D
     tools.
   - Not worth keeping: the 96-module structure, its invented hours, its five overlapping
     classifications and the research caveats printed on every page.

## The product

Four places, each with one job. Settings and the credits page sit behind a menu.

### Home

One question answered: what do I do now.

- The main card says "Continue: 4. Measuring" with the outcome and the exercise length, and one
  button, Start.
- Below it, "Practise" offers three suggestions (see Practice).
- Your last page sits beside them.

Nothing else is on the page.

### Course

The six parts as a list.

- The part you are in is open, showing each lesson as done, current or not started.
- The other parts are folded, with a count ("8 lessons, 3 done").
- There are no hours, tiers, filters or legends.

Every lesson can be opened at any time. A lesson lists what it needs, but nothing is locked.

### Lesson

The fixed format below, ending in one button: "Start the exercise".

### Exercise

The screen you draw beside.

- **The reference area** sits on neutral grey. It holds one of: a reference image; our 3D form; a
  study image that hides itself for memory work; or only the prompt for a drawing from life or
  imagination.
- **Next to it:** the timer, the step list and one control to move on.
- **When the time is up:** add a photo of your page, then go through the checks (see Feedback and
  progress).

### Practice

The library.

- Every lesson contributes 2 to 5 practice items, organised by part and lesson.
- You can filter by time (5, 10, 20, 30 minutes) and by kind: warm-up, drill, timed reference,
  memory, from life, imagination, master copy.
- Suggest picks three:
  1. something from the lesson you are on;
  2. something from an earlier lesson that is due for review;
  3. something to draw for its own sake.

  The third is Drawabox's 50% rule, built in.

### Drawings

Every photographed page, by date or by lesson.

- A lesson shows your first and latest pages side by side.
- The baseline sets sit side by side too.
- Opening a page shows the reference, the overlay and the checks you ticked.

## The lesson format

Every lesson has the same parts, in the same order, within the word budgets in `WRITING.md`:

1. **Title and outcome:** "Confident lines. After this you can draw a straight line between two
   points in one smooth stroke."
2. **The example:** what the result looks like.
3. **Words to know:** at most three terms.
4. **How:** 3 to 6 numbered steps, each beside its diagram.
5. **The usual mistake:** one wrong-and-right pair and the reason.
6. **Do this:** one exercise, with time, quantity and materials.
7. **Check:** 3 to 5 things you can see on the page.
8. **Watch:** the one or two best lessons elsewhere, one line each.
9. **Practise later:** the items this lesson adds to the library.

Two things change with the learner:

- **"I know this"** on any lesson folds the steps away and leaves the goal, the exercise and the
  check. A setting makes that the default for experienced learners.
- **A lesson's state** is not started, tried (exercise done once) or done (checks ticked). A state
  never locks anything.

## Where to start

The first time Delineare opens, it asks one question with three answers:

- **I'm new to drawing.** Do the baseline (ten subjects from imagination, 5 to 7 minutes each,
  skippable and doable later), then lesson 1.
- **I can draw a bit.** Take the placement set: six tasks of 10 to 15 minutes, each with a pass
  check (`research/02` section 5).
  1. Ghosted lines landing on their dots.
  2. A measured mug.
  3. Freehand boxes that converge.
  4. A shaded sphere with its shadow.
  5. Gestures and a mannequin.
  6. A three-quarter head.

  A passed task marks its lessons as known, and their drills stay in the practice library.
- **Show me the course.** Go straight to the Course page.

The baseline comes back after parts 2, 4 and 6, and Drawings puts the sets side by side.

## Practice

**Formats** (from `research/02` section 6 and the first build's best practice items):

| Format | What you do | Length |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Warm-up | Two drills from lessons you have done | 10 min |
| Drill | One exercise from a lesson | 5 to 15 min |
| Timed reference | A ladder of poses or objects, 30 seconds up to 5 minutes | 10 to 30 min |
| Memory loop | Study 2 minutes, draw 5, compare, correct 3 | 10 min |
| From life | Draw something in the room from a prompt | 10 to 20 min |
| Imagination | A generated prompt: a thing, a quality, a view | 10 to 20 min |
| On location | A quick sketch of a real place with people in it | 15 min |
| Master copy | Copy a public-domain drawing | 30 min |
| Retest | A lesson's check again, days later | 10 min |

**Review timing:** gentle and never overdue. A lesson's drills come up for review about 2 days,
1 week, 3 weeks and 2 months after you last did them. Mixing starts once a lesson is done: one skill
at a time first, mixed later (`research/03` A2). Suggestions never pile up, and nothing shows as
missed.

**Reference sources:**
- Built in: our 3D forms, mannequin poses and heads; open-access museum images from the Art
  Institute of Chicago and the Cleveland Museum of Art, each credited.
- The Met's search moved to a new API on 2026-10-01. Either move to it or drop the Met.
- Photo pose reference for people stays on the sites that own it (Line of Action, Quickposes,
  SketchDaily), opened in the browser with the timer kept in Delineare.

**Tools:**
- Keep and rework: the form viewer and the light-on-form viewer.
- New:
  - a posable mannequin, built from simple shapes in our own code (the existing open library is
    GPL);
  - a head-planes viewer;
  - a photo grid and value checker.

## Feedback and progress

**After an exercise:**
- Add a photo of the page by choosing a file or dragging it in. The launcher does not allow camera
  access.
- Go through the lesson's checks.
- For observation exercises, mark where you think the drawing is off before Delineare shows the
  overlay. Then align your photo to the reference by two points. Estimating your own error first is
  what makes the feedback stick (`research/03` A6).

**A lesson is done** when its checks hold. A retest a few days later then appears in Practice.

**Progress is your pages over time:**
- Shown as first and latest per lesson, and the baseline sets side by side.
- Counts are plain ("23 pages this month").
- No streaks, badges, scores, levels or praise.

## Examples and demonstrations

This is the largest content job. Every lesson needs pictures that teach. In order of preference:

1. **Our own diagrams beside every step.** Diagrams of lines, ellipses, boxes, perspective and
   shadows are generated in code from real geometry, so they are exact.
2. **Live 3D, where turning a thing helps:** forms, light, the mannequin, the head.
3. **Public-domain plates and open-access museum drawings** for figure, head and hand examples,
   credited. Bridgman, Vanderpoel and Hatton are public domain. Loomis is not, so we link to Proko's
   Loomis lessons instead.
4. **The teacher's video**, played inside the lesson if the launcher allows it (decision 1),
   otherwise opened in the browser.
5. **Wrong-and-right pairs:** drawn as diagrams for the geometric lessons. For the figure lessons,
   see decision 2.

No AI-generated images. A drawing course cannot teach from pictures whose construction is wrong.

## Resources

- **One registry,** `content/resources.yaml`, with title, teacher, URL, kind, length, cost and the
  date it was last checked.
- **What ships:** only a resource someone has opened, that teaches the lesson, and that is either
  free or plainly marked paid. At most one paid resource per lesson, and only when it is clearly
  better.
- **In a lesson:** one or two Watch lines. The credits page lists every teacher and source.
- **Link checks:** a weekly job checks every link and opens an issue on failure. Nothing about
  checking appears in the app.
- **Starting point:** the 148 resources `research/01` kept, with the per-lesson picks in
  `COURSE.md`.

## Writing

`WRITING.md` covers who we write for, ten rules, the word budgets, banned patterns, before-and-after
examples from the first build, and the six steps every lesson goes through. Two things enforce it:

- The `humanizer` and `stop-slop` skills in `.claude/skills/`.
- A copy lint in CI.

The owner reads every lesson in the app before it ships.

## Look and feel

- **The pencil mark stays.** Everything else follows the Instrumenta brand: Fraunces for headings,
  Commissioner for interface and lesson text, Spline Sans Mono for timers and numbers.
- **The chartreuse accent** marks the one primary action on each screen.
- **Neutral grey** sits under references and anywhere tone is judged, in both themes.
- **Lesson layout:** text is one readable column. Diagrams sit beside their steps on a wide window
  and below them on a narrow one. Light and dark themes.

## Technical setup

**The codebase:**
- A fresh one on a `rebuild` branch. The old `src/`, `curriculum/` and first-build docs are removed;
  Git history keeps them, and the useful research is already in `research/`.
- React 19, TypeScript, Vite and Dexie.
- A new database name. Nothing migrates, because the only existing data is test use.

**Content lives in files a person can read and edit without the app:**

```text
content/
  course.yaml                         parts and lesson order
  resources.yaml                      the resource registry
  lessons/_template.md
  lessons/01-confident-lines/
    lesson.md                         front matter plus the fixed sections
    diagrams/                         SVG files or the scripts that draw them
  practice/                           practice items, one file per lesson
  references/                         bundled public-domain and open-access images, with credits
```

**The build step** validates all of it and fails on any problem:
- the schema;
- the word budgets;
- the copy lint;
- every resource, diagram and image exists;
- every lesson's "needs" exist and form no loop.

It writes one `content.json`.

**Tests:**
- Unit tests for review timing and content validation.
- Browser tests, served by Instrumenta's own static server under the real content policy, at 1440
  and 390 px, in light and dark.
- Screenshots of every screen, for review.

**CI:**
- `ci.yml`: typecheck, tests, copy lint, build and the launcher's audit.
- `linkcheck.yml`: weekly.
- `release.yml`: unchanged.

**Changes in Instrumenta:**
- The build audit checks for `content.json` instead of `curriculum.json`.
- The Met fix or removal.
- The content policy for any new image host.
- If decision 1 is yes, a narrow rule that lets Delineare's window embed `youtube-nocookie.com`
  players and nothing else, with tests.

**`CLAUDE.md` gets rewritten.** The current one requires every resource to show labels such as
"unverified" and every lesson to say we wrote it, which is where much of the clutter came from.

## What carries over and what goes

**Keep:**

- the museum reference code, with the Met fixed or removed;

## Build phases

Every phase ends with a working app. Every lesson goes through the six writing steps.

**Phase 0. Foundations.**
- Work:
  - the `rebuild` branch;
  - the content pipeline and validation;
  - the copy lint;
  - the new `CLAUDE.md`;
  - the design tokens;
  - empty versions of the four places.
- In Instrumenta: a test of playing a YouTube video inside a lesson.
- Done when CI runs the lint and validation, and the video question has an answer.

**Phase 1. Pilot: lessons 1 to 3, end to end.**
- Work:
  - Home, Course, the lesson page, the exercise screen, photo and checks;
  - practice for those three lessons;
  - all their diagrams.
- Released as 0.2.0, so it installs and updates through the launcher like any app. It replaces
  0.1.0, which has nothing worth keeping, and needs Instrumenta 0.14.0.
- **Gate:** you draw with it for a week. Nothing else gets written until you are happy with the
  format, the voice and the flow.

**Phase 2. Part 1 complete.**
- Work:
  - lessons 4 to 8;
  - Where to start, with the placement set;
  - the baseline;
  - the memory loop;
  - the overlay comparison.
- Release 0.3.0.

**Phase 3. Parts 2 and 3.** Lessons 9 to 21, with the form and light tools reworked. Release 0.4.0.

**Phase 4. Part 4.** Lessons 22 to 28, the mannequin and head tools, and the figure examples.
Release 0.5.0.

**Phase 5. Parts 5 and 6.** Lessons 29 to 34, the baseline comparisons and a polish pass. Release
1.0.0.

## Decisions for the owner

1. **Video inside the app, or in the browser?** Playing the teacher's video inside the lesson keeps
   the learner on one page. It needs a narrow change to the launcher's security rules (YouTube's
   privacy-mode player, for Delineare only). Some videos refuse embedding, and Proko's gesture
   lecture is age-gated, so those open in the browser either way.
   - Recommendation: embed, if the Phase 0 test works.
2. **Who draws the figure examples?** Geometric lessons get exact diagrams from code. Figure, head
   and hand lessons need real drawings, including wrong-and-right pairs. Options:
   - public-domain plates and museum drawings, annotated with our own overlays;
   - drawings you make, or commission.
   - Recommendation: plates and museum drawings first; commission only where they leave a gap.
3. **The pilot gate.** Build Phase 0 and Phase 1, then stop for your week of use before writing the
   other 31 lessons. Recommendation: yes.
