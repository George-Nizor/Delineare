# Confident lines

After this you can draw a straight line between two points in one smooth stroke.

[A page of lines between dots. Most hit both dots, a few overshoot, none wobble.]

## Words to know

- **Ghosting:** moving your pen along the line a few millimetres above the paper before you draw it.
- **Drawing from the shoulder:** moving your whole arm for a long line, with the wrist held still.

## How

1. **Plan.** Put two dots on the page, 5 to 20 cm apart. Turn the page so the line runs in the
   direction that feels easiest. [Two dots; the page turned a quarter so the line runs diagonally
   away from the drawing hand.]
2. **Ghost.** Hold the pen a few millimetres above the first dot and move it to the second with your whole arm.
   Lift it back and repeat until the motion feels familiar. [The path between the dots with three
   curved arrows above it.]
3. **Draw.** Put the pen down and make one stroke at the speed you ghosted. Lift the pen off as it
   reaches the second dot instead of slowing down. [One clean line; a small lift mark past the dot.]
4. **Leave it.** If the line misses, leave it and plan the next one. Never go over a line to fix it.
   [A line that misses its dot, left alone, beside a fresh pair of dots.]

Drawabox calls this the ghosting method, and its author learned it from Scott Robertson. You will
use it for every freehand line and ellipse from here on.

## The usual mistake

[Left: a slow line that wobbles and stops short of the dot. Right: a fast line that overshoots it.]

The line on the right is better. Drawing at speed gives you a smooth line now, and your aim improves
with practice. Slowing down to hit the dot makes the line wobble, and a wobbly line is hard to use.

## Do this · 15 minutes

Fill one page with dots, then join pairs of them with ghosted lines. Use a ballpoint pen or
fineliner, so you cannot erase. The lines can cross and the page can get crowded.

## Check

Look over the page three times, one level at a time:

1. Every line is smooth, with no wobble, even where it misses.
2. Each line heads in the right direction, even if it stops short or overshoots.
3. Lines start on one dot and end on the other.

Work on level 1 until most lines pass it, then aim for level 2.

A wobble means you slowed down or drew from the wrist. A line that bows often comes from the wrist
too, so draw from the shoulder. If it still bows, aim a slight curve the other way.

## Practise later

- **Ghosted lines** · 10 min · a page of dots and lines, as above. A good warm-up before any lesson.
- **Superimposed lines** · 5 min · Rule one line, then draw over it freehand 8 times. Start with a
  line 5 cm long and work up to the width of the page. Fraying at the far end is normal; fraying at
  both ends means you did not start exactly on the line.
