# The course

34 lessons in 6 parts, from a first confident line to a scene drawn from imagination. Colour,
painting and digital tools are out of scope for now.

The order follows what most serious drawing courses agree on (`research/02`, section 1): mark
control, then seeing, then simple forms, perspective, value and the figure, then applying it all.
Two strands run from the first part, because the research supports both:

- **Observation:** contour, measuring and checking. Most drawing errors come from misjudging the
  subject, not from the hand.
- **Construction:** forms in space. Drawing from imagination needs it.

Gesture comes early as a short drill. Memory drawing starts in lesson 8 and continues in the
practice library, because it is the best-supported bridge to drawing from imagination.

Each lesson below gives:

- the outcome;
- the lessons it needs;
- a sketch of its main exercise;
- the resource it leans on.

When a lesson is written, its steps, numbers and checks come from those resources (`WRITING.md`,
"How a lesson gets written"), and every link is checked again. More resources per lesson are in
`research/02-drawing-curricula-survey.md`, section 2.

Not counted as lessons:

- **Before lesson 1:** the baseline drawings and an optional placement set (`PLAN.md`, "Where to
  start").
- **The baseline again:** after parts 2, 4 and 6.

## Part 1. Lines and looking

**1. Confident lines.** You can draw a straight line between two points in one smooth stroke.
- Needs: nothing.
- Exercise: two pages of lines between dots, in ink, ghosted first.
- Watch: Drawabox, Ghosted Lines (https://drawabox.com/lesson/1/ghostedlines; video
  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LkJG6pKTuRc, 11 min).

**2. Ellipses.** You can draw a smooth ellipse of a chosen width that fits a given space.
- Needs: 1.
- Exercise: a table of ellipses.
- Watch: Drawabox, Ellipses (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tHJ3rzk6kno, 13 min).

**3. Contour.** You can follow an object's real edge with your eye and pen instead of drawing a
symbol for it.
- Needs: nothing.
- Exercise: your hand and a shoe, slow contour, three times each.
- Watch: Ctrl+Paint, Drawing Shape: Contour (https://www.ctrlpaint.com/videos/drawing-shape-contour,
  4 min).

**4. Measuring.** You can sight angles and proportions at arm's length to within about a tenth.
- Needs: 3.
- Exercise: a mug and a chair, measured, then checked against a photo.
- Watch: Proko, How to Draw Accurate Proportions (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1J7RQvKnWf4,
  12 min).

**5. Block-in and negative space.** You can start any observed subject with its big straight-line
shape and the shapes around it.
- Needs: 4.
- Exercise: a chair drawn through its gaps.
- Watch: Ctrl+Paint, Linear Block-in (https://www.ctrlpaint.com/videos/drawing-shape-linear-block-in)
  and Negative Space (https://www.ctrlpaint.com/videos/drawing-shape-negative-space).

**6. Gesture.** You can catch a figure's action in 30 seconds to 2 minutes with a few long lines.
- Needs: 1.
- Exercise: a timed ladder of poses.
- Watch: Proko, How to Draw Gesture, Step by Step (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8j39NqwL7s4,
  15 min).

**7. Checking your drawing.** You can find your own errors by flipping the drawing, using plumb
lines, stepping back and comparing with a photo.
- Needs: 4, 5.
- Exercise: check a drawing from lesson 4, list three errors, redraw.
- Watch: Florent Farges, How to Evaluate Yourself as a Self-Taught Artist
  (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SGJ_uBtK__M, 10 min).

**8. Drawing from memory.** You can study a simple subject, draw it without looking, compare, and
correct it from memory.
- Needs: 3.
- Exercise: the memory loop (study 2 minutes, draw 5, compare, correct 3).
- Watch: Proko, How to Improve Your Visual Memory (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XDLJAfDR4fM,
  6 min).

## Part 2. Form and space

**9. Simple forms.** You can draw spheres, boxes, cylinders and cones that sit in depth through
overlap and size.
- Needs: 1, 2.
- Watch: Proko, Structure Basics (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3uEtdDvK6Xo, 11 min).

**10. Contour lines on round forms.** You can wrap contour lines around a curved form so it reads as
solid.
- Needs: 2, 9.
- Watch: Drawabox, Sausages with Contour Lines (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y5By0Q_XFBM, 12 min).

**11. Horizon and one-point perspective.** You can set an eye level and a vanishing point, and place
boxes above, below and across the horizon.
- Needs: 9.
- Watch: Proko, Perspective for Beginners (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BYlW8XC0MlI, 14 min).

**12. Two- and three-point boxes.** You can construct boxes with a ruler in two- and three-point
perspective.
- Needs: 11.
- Watch: Drawabox, Plotted Perspective (https://drawabox.com/lesson/1/plottedperspective).

**13. Freehand boxes.** You can draw a box freehand at any angle, draw through it, and check it by
extending its edges.
- Needs: 12.
- Watch: Drawabox, Rotated Boxes (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Oz98L4Fyxoo, 18 min).
- Later: the 250 Box Challenge (https://drawabox.com/lesson/250boxes) becomes a practice track,
  spread over months.

**14. Cylinders.** You can draw a cylinder at any angle with its ellipses lined up on its axis.
- Needs: 2, 13.
- Watch: Proko, How to Draw Cylinders and Ellipses (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PdtDtGBu-Dc,
  15 min).

**15. Building objects.** You can build a real object from boxes and cylinders, drawn through, with
cuts and joins.
- Needs: 10, 13, 14.
- Watch: Drawabox, Form Intersections (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VuorfzSk33Y, 29 min).
- Paid, and the one book worth buying: Scott Robertson, How to Draw.

**16. Turning an object in your head.** You can draw an object you have looked at from views you
have not seen.
- Needs: 15.
- Watch: Dan Beardshaw, Rotating Objects in Perspective (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ja4W5P7K5PE,
  10 min).

## Part 3. Light and value

**17. Seeing value.** You can make a value scale and squint a subject down to two or three value
shapes.
- Needs: 5.
- Watch: Proko, Intro to Value Drawing (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a0uaqmY04OI, 9 min).

**18. Light on form.** You can shade a simple form under one light, with a clear split between
light and shadow.
- Needs: 9, 17.
- Watch: Proko, Shading Light and Form, Basics (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V3WmrWUEIJo, 7 min).
- Tool: the light-on-form viewer.

**19. Cast shadows.** You can construct the shadow of a box or cylinder from a sun or a lamp.
- Needs: 12, 18.
- Watch: Proko, Shadow Mapping Basics (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QNDJZSpyRLI, 8 min).

**20. Tone with line.** You can build value with hatching and cross-hatching.
- Needs: 10, 18.
- Watch: Alphonso Dunn, Pen and Ink Crosshatching (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rIIpEuLxsiI).

**21. Still life.** You can take a drawing from life through block-in, shadow shapes and values in
60 to 90 minutes.
- Needs: 5, 7, 18, 20.
- Watch: Ctrl+Paint, Simple Still Life
  (https://www.ctrlpaint.com/videos/drawing-shape-simple-still-life).

## Part 4. People

**22. Proportions and landmarks.** You can lay out a standing figure about eight heads tall, with
its three main masses and bony landmarks.
- Needs: 4, 6.
- Watch: Proko on proportions (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OyWHmxFGVK4, 7 min) and landmarks
  (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Qwt_wggUFHg, 8 min).

**23. The mannequin.** You can turn a gesture into a simple 3D body that twists, tilts and points
toward you.
- Needs: 6, 9, 10, 15.
- Watch: Proko, The Bean
  (https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/how-to-simplify-the-motion-of-the-torso-the-bean); Michael
  Hampton, Step by Step Figure Construction (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DIjooolQ6xs, 14 min).
- Tool: the posable mannequin.

**24. Figures from imagination.** You can invent a pose, draw it as a mannequin from three views,
and check it against reference.
- Needs: 22, 23.
- Watch: Peter Han, Training Yourself to Draw From Imagination
  (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6-8ED4DW6A0, 28 min).

**25. The head.** You can construct a head at any angle from a ball and a plane.
- Needs: 9, 14.
- Watch: Proko, the Loomis method, parts 1 and 2 (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wAOldLWIDSM,
  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JC2ZppKHCqU).

**26. Features and likeness.** You can draw the features as forms and measure a likeness from a
photo.
- Needs: 5, 25.
- Watch: Love Life Drawing, How to Get a Likeness in a Portrait
  (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F_vMtxyq9_U, 23 min).

**27. Hands and feet.** You can draw a hand from imagination as a box palm with jointed fingers.
- Needs: 23.
- Watch: Proko, How to Draw Hands from Imagination (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kV2e_v5Pqho,
  13 min).

**28. Clothing folds.** You can dress a mannequin from where the cloth hangs and pulls.
- Needs: 23.
- Watch: Marco Bucci, Understanding Every Type of Clothing Fold
  (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d07NanfYT8U, 14 min).

## Part 5. Living things and places

**29. Plants and trees.** You can construct leaves, branches and flowers, and simplify a tree into
masses.
- Needs: 10, 15.
- Watch: Drawabox, Lesson 3 (https://drawabox.com/lesson/3/1).

**30. Animals.** You can build a four-legged animal from masses and leg structure.
- Needs: 6, 10, 15, 23.
- Watch: Drawabox, Lesson 5 (https://drawabox.com/lesson/5/1).

**31. Rooms and streets.** You can set up a two-point space and keep people in it at the right
scale.
- Needs: 13, 22.
- Watch: Scott Robertson, Transferring Scale in Perspective
  (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C2xn9HiHmT0, 11 min).

**32. Composition and thumbnails.** You can make six small three-value thumbnails and choose the
clearest.
- Needs: 17.
- Watch: Proko, Composition Sketching (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VSRLe6ewKIc, 9 min).

## Part 6. Invention

**33. Designing from parts.** You can design a creature or object from studied references, with a
readable silhouette, in three views.
- Needs: 16, 24, 30.
- Watch: Ctrl+Paint, Designing with Reference
  (https://www.ctrlpaint.com/videos/designing-with-reference).

**34. A scene from imagination.** You can take an invented scene from thumbnail through perspective,
figures in scale and cast shadows.
- Needs: 19, 24, 31, 32.
- Watch: Dan Beardshaw, Environments From Imagination (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aa9hfOOVpUo,
  9 min).
