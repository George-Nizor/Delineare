# Light on form

A ball under one light has a lit side, a shadow side and a shadow on the table, and those three areas make it look round. In this lesson you'll shade a simple form under one light, starting with a clear split between the light and the shadow.

![A sphere under one lamp on the left: a bright lit side that darkens gradually, a dark band at the edge of the shadow, and a long shadow on the table.](example)

## Steps

1. **Set the light.** Put a smooth stone or an egg on white paper with one lamp on the side away from your drawing hand. Ruskin does this so the pencil's shadow stays off the page. Keep direct sun off it.

   ![A ball with a lamp on its left and a dashed outline of the shadow it throws to the right.](setup)

2. **Draw the layout.** Draw the form lightly as a circle or ellipse. Mark the terminator, where it turns from light to shadow, and sketch the outline of the cast shadow.

   ![A circle with the terminator marked across it and the outline of the cast shadow beside it.](layout)

3. **Map the shadows.** Fill the form shadow and the cast shadow with one even dark tone, dark enough to contrast with the light but not black. Leave the lit side bare.

   ![The sphere with its shadow side and cast shadow in one flat dark tone and the lit side bare.](shadows)

4. **Refine the shadow.** Darken the core shadow along the terminator. Leave the reflected light nearer the edge lighter, though still darker than any halftone.

   ![The sphere with a darker band along the terminator and a lighter shadow nearer its edge.](core)

5. **Add the halftones.** Halftones are the lit tones between the shadow and the part facing the lamp. Use three steps, with the darkest beside the terminator. A shiny form gets its highlight last.

   ![The lit side of the sphere in three halftone steps, darkest at the terminator, lightest at the centre.](halftones)

6. **Ground it.** Darken the occlusion shadow where the form touches the table. It is the darkest tone, and it makes the form sit.

   ![The finished sphere with a dark patch where it meets the table.](finish)

Proko teaches this order. Ruskin's stone and Speed's notes give the light and shade.

## Common mistake

![Left, a sphere whose shadow side is as light as its halftones, so it looks flat. Right, a sphere whose shadow is darker than every halftone.](mistake)

The most common mistake is lightening the shadow side until it is as bright as the halftones. Proko's rule is that reflected light stays darker than any halftone. Keep the shadow dark and shade the halftones inside the light.

## Checking your page

Hold the page at arm's length and check these:

1. The whole shadow side is darker than the whole lit side.
2. The reflected light is darker than any halftone.
3. The halftones step gradually, darkest beside the terminator.
4. The cast shadow falls away from the light.
5. The cast shadow is darkest where it touches the form.
