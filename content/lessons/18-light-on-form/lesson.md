# Light on form

A ball under one light has a lit side, a shadow side and a shadow on the table, and those three areas are what make it look round. In this lesson you'll shade a simple form under one light, with a clear split between the light and the shadow.

## Steps

1. **Set the light.** Put an egg, a ball or a smooth stone on white paper with one lamp or window to one side. Ruskin puts the light on the left, away from his pencil hand. Keep direct sun off it.

2. **Outline.** Draw the form lightly, as an ellipse or circle. Mark where the cast shadow, the dark shape on the table, meets the form, and sketch the cast shadow's outline.

3. **Map the shadows.** Proko's advice is to find shadow shapes before halftones, the in-between tones. Fill the shadow side and the cast shadow with one even dark tone. Make the cast shadow darkest where it touches the form.

4. **Add the halftones.** Ruskin sees the light passing into the dark gradually. Add two or three lighter tones across the lit side, each a step lighter than the last, and leave the highlight as paper.

## Common mistake

Starting with the halftones. Proko says drawings look flat when you chase them too soon. The soft middle tones are easy to see, so you shade them first and lose the split between light and shadow. Fill the shadow shape first.

## Checking your page

Hold the page at arm's length and check these:

1. The whole shadow side is darker than the whole lit side.
2. The change from light to shadow is gradual, not striped.
3. The cast shadow falls away from the light.
4. The cast shadow is darkest where it touches the form.
