# Designing from parts

A made-up creature looks believable when its parts come from real animals. In this lesson you'll collect references, borrow parts from them, test the silhouette, and draw your design from three views.

![One invented creature drawn from the front, three-quarters and the side, all the same height on one ground line.](example)

## Steps

1. **Collect.** Gather three pictures of real animals that differ, such as a bird, a beetle and a horse. Ctrl+Paint starts a design by gathering reference, and Drawabox's hybrid page does the same.

   ![Three quick sketches side by side: a bird, a beetle seen from above and a horse.](collect)

2. **Study.** Draw one small part from each picture, such as a leg, a shell or a body. Dow has you copy first, then make original designs. Cuyer shows how one limb plan varies between animals.

   ![Three parts drawn small: a bird's neck and leg, a beetle's shell and a horse's body and tail.](study)

3. **Try silhouettes.** Draw six small solid black shapes that mix your parts in different amounts. Drawabox notes viewers read a silhouette fastest. Pick the one you could still name with no detail.

   ![Six solid black creature shapes with different neck, leg, body and shell sizes.](silhouettes)

4. **Build it.** Redraw your choice with an oval for the body and tubes for the neck and legs, as in lesson 30. Draw through every form and mark each joint with a dot.

   ![The chosen creature rebuilt from a dome, an oval and tubes, with a dot at each joint.](build)

5. **Turn it.** Draw the build from the front, three-quarters and the side, the same size on one ground line. Model sheets show a character at several angles so its proportions stay the same.

   ![The built creature from the front, three-quarters and the side, with dashed lines at the head and the back showing the same height in all three.](turn)

## Common mistake

![Left, a body with a second neck, extra shells and extra legs added at random. Right, one body with a borrowed neck, shell and legs.](mistake)

Combining parts at random so the creature has no clear plan. Pick one animal as the base for the body, and borrow one or two parts from the others.

## Checking your page

Compare your three views and check these:

1. The silhouette reads clearly in solid black.
2. Every part comes from one of the three references.
3. The body is built from forms you can see through.
4. The three views are the same height, on one ground line.
