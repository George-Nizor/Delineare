# Designing from parts

A made-up creature looks believable when its parts come from real ones. In this lesson you'll collect references, borrow parts from them, test the silhouette, and draw your design from three views.

## Steps

1. **Collect.** Gather three photos of real animals or objects. Ctrl+Paint begins a design by gathering reference. Choose three that differ: a bird, a beetle and a horse, say.

2. **Study.** Draw one part from each photo, small, such as a wing, a leg or a head. Dow copies first, then makes original designs of similar motives. Cuyer shows how one limb plan varies from animal to animal.

3. **Try silhouettes.** Proko starts from the silhouette. Draw six small solid black shapes that combine your parts in different ways. Pick the one whose outline you could still name with no detail.

4. **Build it.** Redraw your choice with boxes, cylinders and ovals as in lesson 15. Draw through every form, and put the joints where the legs bend.

5. **Turn it.** Draw the build from the front, the side and a three-quarter view, the same size. Use lesson 16 to turn the forms. Check each against the silhouette.

## Common mistake

Combining parts at random so the creature has no clear plan. Pick one animal as the base for the body, and borrow one or two parts from the others.

## Checking your page

Compare your three views and check these:

1. The silhouette reads clearly in solid black.
2. Every part comes from one of the three references.
3. The body is built from forms you can see through.
4. The three views are the same size, with the same proportions.
