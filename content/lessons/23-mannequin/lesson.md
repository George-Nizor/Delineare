# The mannequin

A mannequin is a figure built from simple forms, so you can turn it, tilt it and point it toward the viewer. In this lesson you'll draw a pose as a ribcage and a pelvis joined by a spine, then add the limbs, so the body reads as solid.

![Three mannequins in different poses, each a head, a ribcage, a pelvis and limbs, with a centre line down the torso.](example)

## Steps

1. **Gesture.** Draw the pose in 30 seconds with one or two long lines, as in lesson 6. Vanderpoel puts the big swing of the pose into one line first. Loomis leans the line of balance the way the figure moves.

   ![A throwing figure drawn faintly with one long line running from the head through the spine to the foot.](gesture)

2. **Bean.** Draw the ribcage and the pelvis as two overlapping ovals with a centerline through both. Proko calls this the bean. Bridgman treats them as blocks: they overlap at the waist and one spine joins them.

   ![A ribcage oval and a pelvis oval overlapping at the waist, with a centre line through both.](bean)

3. **Exaggerate.** Push the pose further than the photo, as Proko does. A side tilt makes the centerline a C curve. A twist makes it an S curve, and the lines nearest you overlap the far ones.

   ![Two beans, one tilted with a C-shaped centre line, one twisted with an S-shaped centre line.](exaggerate)

4. **Foreshorten.** When the torso points toward you, make the ovals shorter and overlap them more.

   ![An upright bean beside one leaning toward the viewer, whose ovals are shorter and overlap more.](foreshorten)

5. **Attach the limbs.** Add a neck, upper arms and thighs as cylinders on the ovals, then lower arms and legs. Draw through the forms where they overlap, as in lesson 15. Loomis builds his mannikin from solid forms like these.

   ![A mannequin reaching up, with cylinders for each limb joined to the ribcage and pelvis ovals.](limbs)

## Common mistake

![Left, a ribcage and pelvis stacked flat. Right, the same two ovals tilted and turned against each other, with a centre line.](mistake)

Stacking the ribcage and the pelvis one on top of the other. Proko calls this a common mistake. In a real pose the two forms tilt and twist against each other, as Bridgman says. Find the angle of each oval first, then draw the centerline.

## Checking your page

Look at the finished mannequin:

1. The centerline runs through both ovals as a C or an S.
2. The ribcage and pelvis tilt differently from each other.
3. Each limb joins an oval at a clear point.
4. Overlapping forms are drawn through, so you can see both edges.

Compare the angle of the shoulders with the angle of the hips in your photo.
