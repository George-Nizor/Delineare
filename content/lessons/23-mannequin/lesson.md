# The mannequin

A mannequin is a figure built from simple forms, so you can turn it, tilt it and point it toward the viewer. In this lesson you'll draw a pose as a ribcage and a pelvis joined by a spine, then add the limbs, so the body reads as solid.

## Steps

1. **Gesture.** Draw the pose in 30 seconds with one or two long lines, as in lesson 6. Hampton starts figure construction with gesture, before any shapes.
2. **Bean.** Draw the ribcage and the pelvis as two overlapping ovals with a centerline through both. Proko calls this the bean. The ribcage oval and pelvis oval overlap at the waist.
3. **Exaggerate.** Push the pose further than the photo. A side tilt makes the centerline a C curve. A twist makes it an S curve, and the near-side lines overlap the far-side ones.
4. **Foreshorten.** When the torso points toward you, make the ovals shorter and overlap them more.
5. **Attach the limbs.** Add a neck, upper arms and thighs as cylinders joined to the ovals, then lower arms and legs. Draw through the forms where they overlap, as in lesson 15.

Proko teaches the bean in its figure drawing course. Hampton's order is gesture, tilts and shapes, connections, landmarks and perspective.

## Common mistake

Drawing the ovals as flat shapes facing the viewer. Then the torso can't twist or lean. Draw a centerline through both ovals first, and use it to tell which way each oval faces.

## Checking your page

Look at the finished mannequin:

1. The centerline runs through both ovals as a C or an S.
2. The ribcage and pelvis tilt differently from each other.
3. Each limb joins an oval at a clear point.
4. Overlapping forms are drawn through, so you can see both edges.

Compare the angle of the shoulders with the angle of the hips in your photo.
