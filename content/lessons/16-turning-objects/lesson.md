# Turning objects in your head

Drawing from life shows you one side. In this lesson you'll build an object inside a box, then redraw it turned to a view you haven't seen by working out where each corner moves. Then you'll turn the real object to check.

## Steps

1. **Draw it as you see it.** Look at a plain object for a minute. Build it from forms, as in the last lesson, with the box around it drawn through.

2. **Choose a turn.** Pick a direction and roughly how far to turn it around a vertical axis. When a box turns this way, its vanishing points slide along the horizon.

3. **Move the front corners.** Move each front corner sideways by less than the original edge length, and a little nearer the horizon. The turned face is shorter, because it slants away.

4. **Place the back corners.** Judge each back corner against the old back corners and the new front ones. Draw the box with ghosted lines.

5. **Add the forms.** Rebuild the object's forms inside the turned box. Then turn the real object that way and compare.

Drawabox teaches this corner-by-corner estimate. Dan Beardshaw's video shows rotating objects in perspective.

## Common mistake

The most common mistake is not rotating. The new box's edges still converge to the same vanishing points, so it has only moved or stretched. Drawabox points out that if the vanishing points haven't moved, the box hasn't turned.

## Checking your page

Compare your two drawings and the object, and check these:

1. The turned box's edges meet at different vanishing points from the first.
2. The turned face is shorter than the original.
3. The turned box is still a box, with twelve edges.
4. The forms inside it match the real object turned.

Your first turns will be off. Look at which corner was wrong.
