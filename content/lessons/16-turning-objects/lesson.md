# Turning objects in your head

Drawing from life shows you one side of an object. In this lesson you'll draw an object, work out how it looks after a turn, and draw that view without looking at it. Then you'll turn the real object to see how close you were.

![The same object drawn twice, once at 45 degrees and once turned 15 degrees, on one eye level with the nearest vertical edge the same in both.](example)

## Steps

1. **Draw it as you see it.** Set a plain object so its two front faces turn away equally. Build it from forms with its box drawn through, and mark your eye level.

   ![A box with a smaller box on top, drawn through, below a dashed eye level line.](start)

2. **Choose a turn.** Turn it about its vertical axis. Norton's second cube position is 15 degrees past the first, so one face makes 60 degrees with the picture plane, the flat plane you face.

   ![A plan view from above: the object's footprint at 45 degrees to the picture plane, then turned to 60.](choose)

3. **Keep what stays.** Use the same eye level, and draw the nearest vertical edge at the same height and length. Norton does this so both drawings show the object at one size.

   ![Two views of the object on one eye level, the nearest vertical edge in each marked.](keep)

4. **Move the corners.** The face turning away gets shorter and its edges slope more steeply. The other face widens and flattens. Place each new corner against its neighbours, as Drawabox does.

   ![The turned object in black over the first view in grey, with both vanishing points sliding left along the eye level.](move)

5. **Add the forms.** Rebuild the object's forms inside the turned box. Extend each set of edges: they should reach the eye level at their own points. Then turn the real object by the same amount and compare.

   ![The turned box with the smaller form on top placed against its corners.](add)

Norton draws a cube in two positions, first from the object and then from memory. Drawabox's Rotated Boxes gives the corner-by-corner estimate.

## Common mistake

![Left, two boxes whose edges run to one vanishing point. Right, two boxes whose edges run to two different points on the eye level.](mistake)

The most common mistake is not rotating. The new box's edges still run to the same vanishing points, so it has only moved or stretched. Drawabox says that if the vanishing points haven't moved, the box hasn't turned.

## Checking your page

Compare your two drawings and the object, and check these:

1. The turned box's edges meet the eye level at different points from the first.
2. The nearest vertical edge has the same height and length in both.
3. The face turning away is shorter, and the other is wider.
4. The turned box is still a box, with twelve edges drawn through.
5. The forms inside it match the real object turned by the same amount.

Your first turns will be off. Look at which corner was wrong.
