# Proportions and landmarks

In this lesson you'll lay out a standing adult using the head as a unit of measure. You'll block in the body's three main masses and mark the bony points that show where things sit.

![A figure drawn on a grid of seven and a half heads, with the body lines and landmark dots, and a second figure beside it drawn the same way without the grid.](example)

## Steps

1. **Divide the height.** Draw a light vertical line and divide it into 7.5 equal parts. Each part is one head. Proko takes this from Dr. Paul Richer, who measured an average adult man.

   ![A vertical line cut into seven equal parts and a half, with one part marked as one head.](grid)

2. **Mark the body lines.** Counting down from the chin, put the nipples 1 head down, the navel 2 heads down and the crotch 3 heads down. The knees sit about 5.5 heads from the top.

   ![A standing figure with dashed lines across it at the chin, nipples, navel, crotch and knees.](lines)

3. **Block in the masses.** Draw an oval for the head, one for the ribcage and one for the pelvis, joined by a curved line for the spine. Proko calls these the body's three major masses.

   ![Three ovals for head, ribcage and pelvis joined by a curved spine line.](masses)

4. **Mark landmarks.** A **landmark** is a place where bone shows at the surface. Dot the collarbones, the tips of the shoulders, the points at the front of the hips, the kneecaps and the ankle bones.

   ![A standing figure with dots on the collarbone ends, shoulder tips, front hip points, kneecaps and ankle bones.](landmarks)

5. **Compare.** Hold a pencil at arm's length to a standing photo, as Speed measures. Mark the head on it with your thumb and count how many heads tall the person is. Compare your count with 7.5.

   ![A pencil with a thumb marking one head, and that length stepped down a standing figure to count the heads.](compare)

Real people vary, so treat 7.5 as an average to measure against. Proko, quoting Bridgman, warns that the system suits an upright figure and fails when the body bends toward you.

## Common mistake

![Left, a large head with the body squeezed into the space left. Right, the grid marked first and the figure fitted to it.](mistake)

Drawing the head first, at whatever size feels right, then squeezing the legs into the space that's left. Vanderpoel asks for the place and size of each part against the whole figure before any part is drawn. Mark all 7.5 divisions first.

## Checking your page

Check your figure against the grid you drew:

1. The figure fills 7.5 equal head units from top to bottom.
2. The nipple line sits 1 head below the chin.
3. The navel sits 2 heads below the chin, and the crotch 3.
4. The ribcage and pelvis are two separate ovals, joined by one spine line.

Press on your own collarbone and kneecap to confirm where those landmarks sit.
