# Proportions and landmarks

In this lesson you'll lay out a standing adult using the head as a unit of measure. You'll block in the body's three main masses and mark the bony points that show where things sit.

## Steps

1. **Divide the height.** Draw a light vertical line and divide it into 7.5 equal parts. Each part is one head. Proko takes this from Dr. Paul Richer, who measured an average adult man.
2. **Mark the body lines.** Counting down from the chin, put the nipples 1 head down, the navel 2 heads down and the crotch 3 heads down. The knees sit about 5.5 heads from the top.
3. **Block in the masses.** Draw an oval for the head, one for the ribcage and one for the pelvis, joined by a curved line for the spine. These are the body's three major masses.
4. **Mark landmarks.** A **landmark** is a place where bone sits at the surface. Dot the collarbones, the tips of the shoulders, the points at the front of the hips, the kneecaps and the ankle bones.
5. **Compare.** Hold a pencil up to a standing photo, measure the head, and count how many heads tall the person is. Lay your grid over your measurement.

Proko teaches this with Richer's numbers. Real people vary, so treat 7.5 as an average to measure against.

## Common mistake

Drawing the head first, at whatever size feels right, then squeezing the legs into the space that's left. Mark all 7.5 divisions before you draw anything, so every part has its space.

## Checking your page

Check your figure against the grid you drew:

1. The figure fills 7.5 equal head units from top to bottom.
2. The nipple line sits 1 head below the chin.
3. The navel sits 2 heads below the chin, and the crotch 3.
4. The ribcage and pelvis are two separate ovals, joined by one spine line.

Press on your own collarbone and kneecap to confirm where those landmarks sit.
