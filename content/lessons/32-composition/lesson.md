# Composition and thumbnails

Before a long drawing it helps to try several arrangements, because the first idea is rarely the clearest. In this lesson you'll draw six small thumbnails in three values on one page, then choose the one that reads best.

## Steps

1. **Rule the page.** Dow notes that Japanese artists divided a page into sections of equal size and put a different design in each. Rule six boxes, each about as big as your palm or smaller.

2. **Choose a subject.** Pick a photo or a scene with a few clear shapes: a figure, a tree and a hill, or a street. Proko starts every composition from the silhouette.

3. **Fill each box.** Use three values: the paper for light, a middle tone and a dark. Keep the shapes big and leave out detail. Proko says to start over if a thumbnail gets complicated.

4. **Change something.** Make each box different. Proko's list: group the values differently, move the shapes, change the background, or crop in closer.

5. **Choose.** Hold the page at arm's length or lean back. Pick the thumbnail with the clearest shapes, the one where you see the main subject first.

## Common mistake

Adding detail to the first thumbnail. Detail hides whether the shapes work. Keep each thumbnail to a few flat shapes and judge it as a pattern.

## Checking your page

Compare your six thumbnails and check these:

1. Each box uses three values and no more.
2. No two boxes have the same arrangement of dark shapes.
3. The chosen thumbnail shows its main subject at a glance.
4. Each box is small enough to see all at once.
