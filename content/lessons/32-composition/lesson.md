# Composition and thumbnails

Before a long drawing it helps to try several arrangements, because the first idea is rarely the clearest. In this lesson you'll draw six small thumbnails in three values on one page, then choose the one that reads best.

![A page of six thumbnails of one lighthouse, each arranged differently in light, middle and dark, with one ringed.](example)

## Steps

1. **Rule the page.** Dow notes that Japanese artists divided a page into sections of equal size and put a different design in each. Rule six equal boxes, each no bigger than your palm. Proko suggests that size.

   ![One page ruled into six equal boxes in two rows of three.](rule)

2. **Choose a subject.** Pick a picture with a few clear shapes, such as a figure, a tree and a hill, or a street. Proko says to capture the main big shapes and leave out detail.

   ![A line drawing of a lighthouse beside the same lighthouse as three flat shapes: tower, rock and sea.](subject)

3. **Fill each box.** Use three values: the paper for the light, one middle tone and one dark. Dow calls these a simple set of three notes. Keep the shapes big. Proko says to start again if a thumbnail gets complicated.

   ![One thumbnail filled with paper white, a middle grey and a dark, beside a key of the three values.](fill)

4. **Change something.** Make each box different. Proko's list: group the values differently, move the shapes, change the background, or crop in closer.

   ![Four thumbnails of the same lighthouse: shapes moved, values swapped, a closer crop and a new background.](change)

5. **Choose.** Pick the thumbnail with a clear hierarchy, where one shape reads first and the rest support it. Ring it.

   ![The six thumbnails with the one whose dark tower reads first ringed.](choose)

## Common mistake

![Left, a thumbnail with water lines and windows drawn in. Right, the same thumbnail as a few flat shapes.](mistake)

Adding detail to the first thumbnail. Detail hides whether the shapes work. Proko says to simplify and try again if it gets complicated, so keep each box to a few flat shapes and judge it as a pattern.

## Checking your page

Compare your six thumbnails and check these:

1. Each box uses three values and no more.
2. No two boxes have the same arrangement of dark shapes.
3. The ringed thumbnail has one shape that reads before the others.
4. Each box is no bigger than your palm.
