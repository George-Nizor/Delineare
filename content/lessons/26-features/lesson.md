# Features and likeness

The eyes, nose and mouth sit on the form of the head, so they're easier to place once the head is built. In this lesson you'll add each feature to a Loomis head as a small form. Then you'll measure the picture you're drawing from to check your likeness.

![A Loomis head turned three-quarters with an eye socket and eyeball on each side, a nose and a mouth built as small forms.](example)

## Steps

1. **Build the head.** Draw a Loomis head to match your picture, with the ball, side plane, centerline and face lines from lesson 25. Loomis says placing the features matters more than drawing them.

   ![A Loomis head with its ball, cross lines, side plane, face lines, jaw and ear.](build)

2. **Eyes.** Treat each eye socket as a trapezoid let into the face. Put an eyeball in each as a sphere, one eyeball's width apart. Wrap the upper lids over the spheres.

   ![The head with a trapezoid socket and an eyeball on each side, an eyeball's width apart.](eyes)

3. **Nose.** Draw the nose's centerline, then a flat trapezoid for its base. Soften it into a bridge, a tip and two nostril wings.

   ![The head with a nose centre line and a flat trapezoid base.](nose)

4. **Mouth.** Wrap the mouth around the front of the jaw. The upper lip usually overlaps the lower. When the head turns, the far corner gets shorter.

   ![The head with a mouth curved round the jaw, the far corner shorter than the near one.](mouth)

5. **Measure.** Pick one distance as your unit, such as an eyeball's width. Hold a pencil at arm's length, as Speed does, and compare eye to eye, nose to mouth and mouth to chin with it. Move what's off.

   ![A front head with dashed lines across it at eyes, nose, mouth and chin, and a bracket of three equal eyeball widths below.](measure)

This construction follows Loomis, as Fine Art Tutorials sets it out.

## Common mistake

![Left, a flat almond-shaped eye. Right, an eyeball drawn as a sphere with the lids wrapped over it.](mistake)

Drawing each feature as a flat symbol you know, such as an almond-shaped eye. Loomis notes the eyes are round, though usually drawn flat like a slit in paper. Draw the sphere and the lids over it, and measure each feature against the picture.

## Checking your page

Compare your drawing with the picture:

1. The eyes sit one eyeball's width apart.
2. The nose and mouth are on the centerline.
3. Eye, nose, mouth and chin heights match the picture, measured with the same unit.
4. The far-side features are narrower than the near-side ones.

Flip your drawing in a mirror, as Proko suggests. Distances that look wrong in the mirror are the ones to fix.
