# Features and likeness

The eyes, nose and mouth sit on the form of the head, so they're easier to place once the head is built. In this lesson you'll add each feature to a Loomis head as a small form. Then you'll measure a photo to check your likeness.

## Steps

1. **Build the head.** Draw a Loomis head to match your photo, with the ball, side plane, centerline and face lines from lesson 25.
2. **Eyes.** Treat each eye socket as a trapezoid set into the face. Place two eyeballs as spheres in the sockets, one eyeball's width apart. Wrap the upper lids around them.
3. **Nose.** Draw the nose's centerline, then a flat trapezoid for its base. Soften it into a bridge, a tip and two nostril wings.
4. **Mouth.** Wrap the mouth around the front of the jaw. The upper lip usually overlaps the lower. When the head turns, the far corner gets shorter.
5. **Measure.** Hold a pencil to the photo and compare distances: eye to eye, nose to mouth, mouth to chin. Mark where your drawing is off, and move the feature.

This construction follows Loomis. Love Life Drawing teaches likeness as measuring how a face differs from the average.

## Common mistake

Drawing each feature as a flat symbol you know, such as an almond-shaped eye. Then every face looks alike. Draw the sphere and the lids over it, and measure each feature against the photo.

## Checking your page

Lay a sheet of tracing paper over the photo, or compare by eye:

1. The eyes sit one eyeball's width apart.
2. The nose and mouth are on the centerline.
3. Eye, nose and mouth heights match the photo's proportions.
4. The far-side features are narrower than the near-side ones.

Hold the drawing next to a mirror. Distances that look wrong in the mirror are the ones to fix.
