# Drawing from memory

Drawing from imagination needs a store of things you've seen. In this lesson you'll learn a loop for building that store: study a simple object, draw it with the object out of sight, then compare and correct. It also shows you which parts you didn't look at closely.

## Steps

1. **Study.** Choose a simple object and look at it for about 3 minutes. Notice its angles and shapes, as in contour.

2. **Draw from memory.** Put the object away, out of sight. Draw it for about 5 minutes. Gaps in your drawing show what you didn't take in.

3. **Compare.** Bring the object back. Put it beside your drawing and note each difference.

4. **Draw again.** Hide the object again and draw it a second time, fixing what you found.

Proko teaches this loop. Later you'll stretch the time between looking and drawing.

## Common mistake

Looking at the object while you draw from memory. It feels like help, but it skips the work of recalling. Look only before you draw and when you compare. Proko says to check the reference only after each drawing.

## Checking your page

Set the object beside your second drawing:

1. Its overall shape matches the object's.
2. You fixed the biggest difference from your first drawing.
3. Parts you missed the first time are now drawn.
4. You looked at the object only before and after drawing.

Aim for recognisable structure, not detail.
