# Seeing value

Value is how light or dark something is, whatever its colour. In this lesson you'll make a value scale and learn to squint at a subject until it breaks into three flat shapes. Drawing those shapes first gives every later shading lesson a plan to follow.

## Steps

1. **Make a scale.** Rule a strip of five boxes. Leave the first white and fill the last with the darkest tone your pencil makes. Fill the three between so each is one even step darker than the last.

2. **Squint.** Set a mug or a shoe in front of you by a window. Squint until the detail blurs. Compare the darkest area with the lightest one, and look for the plain shapes they make.

3. **Draw the borders.** Draw only the edges between light and dark, as straight lines and angles, the way you blocked in a shape in lesson 5. Ignore the object's outline where it has no border.

4. **Fill in three values.** Dow works with three: light, medium and dark. Leave the lightest shapes as paper, fill the medium shapes with one even tone, and the darkest with one even tone.

5. **Compare.** Match each tone to your scale. Squint at the page and at the subject in turn. Proko suggests leaning back, or flipping the drawing, to see it fresh.

## Common mistake

Filling a shape because of what the object is: white mug, so white. Proko calls an object's own lightness its local value, and lists it as only one source of value beside the light falling on it. Fill what you see while squinting.

## Checking your page

Look at the page and at the subject while squinting, and check these:

1. Each box on your scale is darker than the one before.
2. The drawing uses three values, each filled evenly.
3. The lightest shapes are bare paper.
4. Squinting at the page, the dark shapes line up with those in the subject.
