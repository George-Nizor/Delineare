# Still life

A still life brings the course together: you measure, block in, find shadow shapes and set values on one subject. In this lesson you'll draw three objects from life in about an hour, working from the large shape down to the tone.

## Steps

1. **Set up.** Put two or three objects close together on a table with one window or lamp to the side. Ruskin keeps direct sun off the subject. Sit where the group fills about half your page.

2. **Block in.** Draw the envelope, the big straight-sided shape around the whole group, as Ctrl+Paint teaches. Cut it down to angles and place each object in it. Measure as you did in lesson 4.

3. **Check.** Step back, flip the drawing in a mirror or hold it to a window, and compare it with the group. Fix the largest errors first.

4. **Map the shadows.** Squint. Draw the edges of the shadow shapes and cast shadows, and fill them with one flat dark tone, as Proko advises, before any halftones.

5. **Set the values.** Add the middle tone and the lights. Use hatching if you work in pen. Draw the background as you see it, as Ruskin insists, so the values stay honest.

## Common mistake

Drawing the objects one at a time, finishing the first before starting the second. The group then drifts out of proportion. Block in all of them together, then shade all of them together.

## Checking your page

When you finish, step back and check these:

1. The group fills about half the page.
2. Each object's height matches the subject within a finger's width.
3. The shadows all fall away from the light.
4. The darkest tone sits where an object meets its cast shadow.
