# Hands

A hand is easier to draw from imagination when you build it from simple forms. In this lesson you'll draw a hand as a box palm with a wrist, jointed fingers and a thumb, using Proko's process.

## Steps

1. **Decide.** Choose the viewing angle, whether you see the palm or the back, and what each finger and the thumb is doing. Picture it before you mark the page.
2. **Palm.** Draw a box about as wide as it is tall. Curve its front plane, since the little-finger side is shorter. Divide the front plane into 4 parts, one per finger.
3. **Fingers.** Draw a mitten shape or a light line for each finger. Do the pinky and index finger first, and fit the middle two between. Build each of the 12 segments as a cylinder.
4. **Thumb.** Draw a stretched triangular box at its base, a cylinder for the first segment and a wedge for the tip.
5. **Wrist.** Draw the wrist as a narrower box. Place the hand slightly lower than the forearm so there is a small step down.

Proko teaches this process in the third part of his hand series.

## Common mistake

Drawing the fingers as long, even tubes. Real fingers have different lengths and bend at three joints. Draw the middle finger longest, the pinky shortest and a cylinder for each of the 3 segments.

## Checking your page

Check each hand against the steps:

1. The palm is a box, with a curved front plane.
2. Each finger has three segments, and the thumb has two.
3. The middle finger is longest and the pinky shortest.
4. The wrist box is narrower than the palm.

Draw your own hand in the same pose and compare.
