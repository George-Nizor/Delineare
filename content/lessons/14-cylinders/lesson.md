# Cylinders

A cylinder is two ellipses joined by straight sides, and the ellipses must line up. In this lesson you'll draw a cylinder at any angle by building it around a line through its centre. Then you'll check how well the ellipses line up.

## Steps

1. **Draw the axis.** Ghost and draw a straight line longer than the cylinder will be. Drawabox calls it the minor axis. Every ellipse on the cylinder is cut in half by it.

2. **Place the first ellipse.** Draw an ellipse near one end of the line, with the whole line passing through it. Don't put it right on the end point.

3. **Place the second.** Draw an ellipse at the other end. If it's the farther end, make it slightly wider than the first. If it's the nearer end, make it slightly narrower.

4. **Join the sides.** Ghost and draw a straight line from the edge of one ellipse to the matching edge of the other, on each side. These sides share a vanishing point with the axis.

5. **Check.** Take a ruler and a pen of another colour. Find the line that cuts each ellipse in half, then compare it with your axis.

Proko's video shows the same cylinder inside a box.

## Common mistake

The most common mistake is drawing the sides parallel on the page. That forces the vanishing point out to infinity. Unless the cylinder points exactly across your view, its sides must converge, even if only slightly.

## Checking your page

Look at each cylinder and check these:

1. Both ellipses are smooth and drawn through.
2. The axis cuts each ellipse into two equal halves.
3. The farther ellipse is wider than the nearer one.
4. The sides converge slightly.

Being a little off is normal. The check shows you which way.
