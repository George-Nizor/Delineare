# Gesture

A gesture drawing records what a pose is doing rather than what it looks like. In this lesson you'll learn to catch a figure's action in 30 seconds to 2 minutes, using a few long lines. You'll draw from photos of people, so the pose stays still while you work.

## Steps

1. **Look first.** For a 2-minute pose, spend 10 to 15 seconds studying it before you draw. For a 30-second pose, start at once.

2. **Find the line of action.** Draw one long line from the neck down to the foot. Use C and S curves, and think of the limbs as a flowing snake rather than rigid bones.

3. **Tilt the torso.** One side of the torso compresses and the other stretches, so the shoulders and hips tilt in opposite directions. Draw both tilts.

4. **Add rhythm lines.** Use a few curved lines for the arms and legs. In a 30-second pose, that is nearly all you have time for.

5. **Add thickness.** In longer poses, show how each form thickens and tapers. If time runs short, drop the least important details first.

Proko teaches this method.

## Common mistake

Drawing fast is not the same as drawing a gesture. Proko puts it as "instead of drawing fast, draw smart": keep each line clean and accurate. Look for the story of the pose rather than copying its outline.

## Checking your page

Hold each drawing at arm's length and look for these things:

1. One long line of action runs through each figure.
2. Shoulders and hips tilt in opposite directions.
3. Limbs are drawn as curves, not stiff straight bars.
4. Short poses show less detail than long ones.

Proko suggests drawing 10 to 20 figure photos a day when you can.
