# Gesture

A gesture drawing records what a pose is doing rather than what it looks like. In this lesson you'll learn to catch a figure's action in 30 seconds to 2 minutes, using a few long lines. You'll draw from pictures of people in action, so the pose stays still while you work.

![Four 30-second gestures, each a long line with a few curves for the limbs.](example)

## Steps

1. **Look first.** For a 2-minute pose, spend 10 to 15 seconds studying it before you draw. For a 30-second pose, start at once.

   ![Two time bars: a 2-minute pose with a short look before drawing, and a 30-second pose drawn at once.](look)

2. **Find the line of action.** Draw one long line from the neck down to the foot. Use C and S curves, and think of the limbs as a flowing snake rather than rigid bones.

   ![A walking figure with one S-curve from the neck to the foot of the leg that carries the weight.](action)

3. **Tilt the torso.** One side of the torso compresses and the other stretches, so the shoulders and hips tilt in opposite directions. Draw both tilts.

   ![A torso with the shoulder line sloping one way and the hip line the other, pinched on one side and stretched on the other.](tilt)

4. **Add rhythm lines.** Use a few curved lines for the arms and legs. In a 30-second pose, that is nearly all you have time for.

   ![The walking figure with one curve for each arm and leg.](rhythm)

5. **Add thickness.** In longer poses, show how each form thickens and tapers. If time runs short, drop the least important details first.

   ![The figure's limbs, chest and hips drawn as tapering shapes over the rhythm lines.](thickness)

Proko teaches this method.

## Common mistake

![Left, a stiff figure of straight bars with level shoulders and hips. Right, the same pose as one line of action with tilts and curves.](mistake)

The most common mistake is drawing as fast as you can. Proko puts it as "instead of drawing fast, draw smart". Keep each line clean and accurate, and look for the story of the pose instead of copying its outline.

## Checking your page

Hold each drawing at arm's length and look for these things:

1. One long line of action runs through each figure.
2. Shoulders and hips tilt in opposite directions.
3. Limbs are drawn as curves, not stiff straight bars.
4. Short poses show less detail than long ones.

Proko suggests drawing 10 to 20 figure photos a day when you can.
