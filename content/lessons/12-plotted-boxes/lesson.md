# Two- and three-point boxes

When you look at a box from a corner, its edges run to two vanishing points on the horizon. In this lesson you'll build boxes with a ruler between two vanishing points, then see what a third point does. Plotting every line shows you how the vanishing points control each edge.

![A frame with a horizon, a vanishing point at each end and five ruled boxes of different sizes, one above the horizon, some across it and some below.](example)

## Steps

1. **Set up.** Rule your page into three frames. In each one, rule a horizon near the middle and mark a vanishing point near each end of it.

   ![A ruled frame with a horizon line and a vanishing point at each end.](setup)

2. **Draw the first vertical.** Rule a vertical inside the middle two thirds of the frame. Its length is the height of the box. Rule a line from its top and from its bottom to each vanishing point.

   ![A vertical line with four ruled lines running from its ends to the two vanishing points.](vertical)

3. **Close the faces.** Rule a vertical on each side, between the lines that run to the same point. Their positions set the box's length in each direction. Rule lines from their ends to the far vanishing points.

   ![Two more verticals closing the left and right faces, with the box's length marked on each side.](faces)

4. **Join the back.** The new lines cross at the two back corners. Rule the last vertical between them. It will lean a little. Drawabox expects that.

   ![A finished box with its back vertical joining the two crossing points.](back)

5. **Repeat.** Draw five or six boxes in each frame. They are boxes, not cubes, so make their heights and lengths differ.

6. **Add a third point.** Look down at a box and its vertical edges stop being parallel. They meet below the horizon. Rule three boxes this way in the last frame.

   ![A box seen from above, with its vertical edges running down to a point far below the horizon.](three)

Drawabox sets the two-point exercise and gives the rule for vanishing points that sit on and below the horizon.

## Common mistake

![Left, a back vertical redrawn straight so it misses the crossing points. Right, a back vertical joining the crossing points and leaning a little.](mistake)

The most common mistake is redoing a box because its back vertical leans. Every ruler placement is a little off, and the errors add up at the back. Drawabox says to join the corners anyway, note which errors repeat, and watch for those next time.

## Checking your page

Look over each frame and check these:

1. Every slanting edge, extended, reaches one of the two vanishing points.
2. Side verticals are parallel to the frame's sides, apart from the back one.
3. Each box has twelve edges, hidden ones included.
4. Boxes differ in height and length.
5. A box below the horizon shows its top, and one above it shows its underside.

In the three-point version, check that the vertical edges, extended, meet at one point below the horizon.
