# Two- and three-point boxes

When a box turns so you see two of its sides, its edges point to two different vanishing points. In this lesson you'll construct boxes with a ruler in two-point perspective, then in three-point. Plotting the lines by hand shows you how the vanishing points control a box.

## Steps

1. **Set up.** Use a ruler to divide the page into three rectangles. Draw a horizon near the centre of each, then put a vanishing point near each end of it.

2. **Draw the first vertical.** Draw a vertical line in the middle two thirds of the frame. Its length is the height of the box. Rule a line from its top and from its bottom to each vanishing point.

3. **Close the faces.** Draw two more verticals between those ruled lines, one on each side. Their positions set the box's length in each direction. Rule lines from their ends back to the vanishing points.

4. **Join the back.** The new lines cross at the back corners. Draw the last vertical between those two corners.

5. **Repeat.** Draw five or six boxes in each frame, then do the same for the other two frames.

Drawabox sets this exercise. Its author expects the back vertical to lean a little, because small errors add up.

## Common mistake

The most common mistake is redoing a box because the back vertical isn't quite vertical. Drawabox expects it. Every ruler placement is a little off. Note which errors repeat and take more care with those next time.

## Checking your page

Look over each frame and check these:

1. Every non-vertical edge, extended, reaches one of the two vanishing points.
2. The vertical edges are parallel to the frame's sides, apart from the back one.
3. Each box has twelve edges.
4. Boxes differ in height and length.

In the three-point version, the vertical edges lean too, because they point to a third vanishing point below the horizon.
