# Tone with line

With a pen you can't smudge a tone, so you build it from lines. In this lesson you'll make light and dark tones with hatching, a layer of close parallel lines, and cross-hatching, a second layer across the first. Then you'll shade a ball.

## Steps

1. **Hatch.** Draw a row of parallel lines close together, all in one direction, all at the same speed. Wikipedia notes that more lines, thicker lines or closer lines make a darker tone.

2. **Cross.** Lay a second layer of lines over the first at an angle. Maginnis finds a sharp angle works better than a square grid. Add a third layer where you want the tone darker.

3. **Even it out.** Ruskin fills the pale gaps with finer lines until no single line catches the eye. Keep every line the same weight, and keep going until the patch reads as one tone.

4. **Make a gradient.** Rule a strip as long as your hand. Ruskin grades it from white to black over the longest distance he can, so every part of the strip changes visibly. Use fewer layers at the pale end.

5. **Shade a ball.** Draw a circle. Curve your hatching around the form, which Wikipedia calls contour hatching. Leave the lit side as paper and add layers toward the shadow side.

## Common mistake

Drawing each line slowly to keep it neat. Ruskin warns that a slow pen leaves a dot of ink at the end of each line, and the dots spoil the tone. Draw each stroke fast, as if signing your name.

## Checking your page

Hold the page at arm's length and check these:

1. The strip changes in even steps from pale to dark.
2. No single line stands out from its neighbours.
3. Lines are evenly spaced within each layer.
4. The ball's curved lines follow its roundness.
