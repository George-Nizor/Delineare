# Block-in and negative space

In this lesson you'll learn to start a drawing with the big straight-edged shape around the subject. Then you draw the empty spaces around and between its parts. Ctrl+Paint calls those spaces "the best way to see an object".

## Steps

1. **See one shape.** Look at the whole subject, or a group of objects, as a single shape. Squint to lose the detail.

2. **Mark the extremes.** Lightly dot the highest, lowest, widest and narrowest points. Get Realism says accuracy matters more than the number of dots.

3. **Join them with straight lines.** Sight the angle between two dots with your pencil and draw it as a straight line, even where the real edge curves. Call the outline you get the **envelope**.

4. **Check the envelope.** Measure its height against its width on the subject and on your page. If the page is too tall or too wide, move the lines now.

5. **Draw the gaps.** The space around and between parts is **negative space**. Draw the largest gap first, such as the opening under a chair seat, then the smaller ones. Check each gap's angles and proportions.

6. **Cut the corners.** Break the envelope into smaller angles, and round them into curves only once the proportions hold.

Ctrl+Paint teaches the envelope and the negative space as ways to see; Sciandra's chair exercise supplies the gap drawing.

## Common mistake

Starting with a detail, such as one chair leg, leaves nothing to judge it against, so errors pile up as you add parts. Ctrl+Paint works from basic to complex: draw the whole envelope first, then divide it.

## Checking your page

Look at your chair:

1. The envelope's height and width match the chair's.
2. The largest gap is the right shape and in the right place.
3. The gaps between legs are drawn, not the legs.
4. Straight lines come before curves.

Correct the envelope before you add any detail.
