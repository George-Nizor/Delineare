# Rooms and streets

People in a room or street have to get smaller as they go back, and by a known amount. In this lesson you'll set up a space with a horizon and two vanishing points. Then you'll use a height scale to draw people at the right size anywhere in it. The scale is G. A. Storey's.

![A room corner with two vanishing points, a height scale at the left edge and four people standing at different distances, each at the height the scale gives.](example)

## Steps

1. **Set the horizon.** Draw a horizontal line across the page. Storey says its height depends on the height of the eye. Put two vanishing points on it, far apart, and draw a room corner to them.

   ![A horizon line with a vanishing point at each end and a room corner whose floor and ceiling edges run to the two points.](horizon)

2. **Draw a height scale.** Near the page edge, mark a person's height as A at the floor and B above it. Pick a point O on the horizon. Draw lines from A to O and from B to O.

   ![Points A and B on a vertical at the left edge, both joined to a point O on the horizon, with equal verticals drawn between the two lines.](scale)

3. **Place a person.** Pick a floor spot. Draw a horizontal line from it to the line AO, then a vertical up to BO. Storey says that vertical is one person's height at that depth. Draw the person that tall.

   ![A floor spot with a dashed line across to the scale, a vertical between the two scale lines, and a person drawn at the spot with the same height.](place)

4. **Repeat.** Mark three more spots, nearer and farther, and find each height the same way. Storey says all the verticals between the two lines are equal in perspective.

   ![Four floor spots, each with its own line across to the scale and its own height, the nearest person tallest.](repeat)

5. **Put heads on the horizon.** If you stand among the figures, Storey places their heads about at the horizon. Draw your people so their heads sit near it, a little above or below for tall or short ones.

   ![Six people at different distances whose heads all sit close to the horizon line.](heads)

## Common mistake

![Left, four people drawn the same height whatever their distance. Right, the same four with heights that shrink toward the horizon.](mistake)

Giving every person the same height on the page, however far away. Distant people are smaller, and the scale tells you how much. Measure each one from the scale before you draw it.

## Checking your page

Check the space and the people with these:

1. The sides of the room run to the two vanishing points.
2. Nearer people are taller on the page than farther ones.
3. Every person's height matches the scale at their spot.
4. Standing people's heads sit close to the horizon.
