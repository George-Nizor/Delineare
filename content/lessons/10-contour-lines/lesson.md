# Contour lines on round forms

Lines that run across a surface show which way it curves. In this lesson you'll draw sausage shapes and wrap contour lines around them so they look solid. Drawabox uses "contour line" for a line across a surface. It isn't the edge-following contour from lesson 3.

## Steps

1. **Draw a sausage.** Draw two equal round ends joined by a middle of constant width, bent any way you like. Avoid ends of different sizes, flattened ends, or a middle that swells or narrows. Don't draw through it.

2. **Add a spine.** Draw a line through the centre of the sausage. It marks the **minor axis** of each ellipse you add: the line that cuts an ellipse in half across its narrow side.

3. **Draw contour ellipses.** Add a few ellipses around the sausage, each cut in half by the spine. Draw each through twice and keep it snug inside the outline. Make the ones further back wider.

4. **Try contour curves.** On a second page, draw only the visible part of each contour line. Overshoot a little, so the curve hooks round the edge onto the far side.

5. **Cap a tip.** If one end points toward you, draw a small ellipse on it. The wider that ellipse, the further it sits from the edge of the outline.

Drawabox, whose method this is, assigns one page of ellipses and one of curves.

## Common mistake

The most common mistake is contour lines that float. They hang inside the form, all at the same angle, like rings on a flat shape. Pinch each line between the edges of the sausage, and widen the ellipses toward the far end.

## Checking your page

Look at one page at a time and check these:

1. Each contour line touches both edges of the sausage, or stays close inside.
2. The spine cuts every ellipse into two equal halves.
3. Ellipses get wider toward the far end.
4. On the curves page, each curve hooks round the edge.

If your curves look flat, overshoot them more.
