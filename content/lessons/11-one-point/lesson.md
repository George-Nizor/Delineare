# Horizon and one-point perspective

One-point perspective is the simplest way to draw boxes that recede into the distance. In this lesson you'll set an eye level and a vanishing point, then place boxes above, below and across the horizon so they all line up.

![A page with a horizon, one vanishing point and six boxes above, below and across the horizon, all receding to the same point.](example)

## Steps

1. **Draw the horizon.** Rule a horizontal line across the middle of the page. It's your eye level. Drawabox defines the horizon as the far end of the ground, seen with your head level.

   ![A level line across the page with an eye above it, marking eye level.](horizon)

2. **Place the vanishing point.** Mark a dot on the horizon. A **vanishing point** is where a set of parallel edges that run away from you meet.

   ![Four parallel lines running away from the viewer and meeting at a dot on the horizon.](point)

3. **Draw front faces.** Draw three squares or rectangles: one above the horizon, one below it and one across it. Keep their sides horizontal and vertical. In one-point perspective those edges stay parallel on the page.

   ![Three rectangles with horizontal and vertical sides, one above, one below and one across the horizon.](fronts)

4. **Run edges to the point.** From each front corner, rule a line toward the vanishing point. Stop each one where you want the box to end.

   ![Lines ruled from each front corner toward the vanishing point and stopped partway.](edges)

5. **Close the back.** Join the ruled lines with horizontal and vertical edges to make the back face. It comes out smaller than the front, because farther things look smaller.

   ![Three finished boxes with smaller back faces, the hidden edges dashed.](back)

Drawabox supplies the horizon, vanishing point and one-point ideas. Proko's Perspective for Beginners is the video to watch alongside.

## Common mistake

![Left, a box far from the vanishing point with long, stretched edges. Right, boxes close to the vanishing point.](mistake)

The most common mistake is putting boxes far from the vanishing point. Drawabox says one-point works when the box sits roughly in front of you, with the vanishing point inside or close to it. The nearer it drifts to an edge, the more wrong it looks. Keep yours central.

## Checking your page

Hold a ruler along the receding edges and check these:

1. The sides of each front face are exactly horizontal and vertical.
2. Every receding edge, extended, passes through the vanishing point.
3. Each back face is smaller than its front face.
4. Boxes below the horizon have edges sloping up to the point; boxes above, down.
