# Cast shadows

A shadow follows rules, so you can work out its shape instead of guessing. In this lesson you'll build the shadow of a box from the sun and from a lamp, using the method G. A. Storey set out in his book on perspective.

## Steps

1. **Draw the box.** Draw a box standing on the floor with its front face square to you, using the freehand or plotted boxes from earlier lessons. Draw the horizon line and the vanishing point for its sides.

2. **Place the sun.** Storey treats the sun's rays as parallel. At 45 degrees up, a shadow on the floor is as long as the object is tall. Put the sun to one side, level with the picture.

3. **Find the tips.** From the top of each vertical edge, draw a ray down at 45 degrees. From the base of the same edge, draw a floor line sideways. They meet at the tip of that edge's shadow.

4. **Join and fill.** Join the tips with lines that run to the box's own vanishing point. Fill the shape with one flat dark tone.

5. **Use a lamp.** Storey calls the spot on the floor under a lamp its foot. Draw floor lines from the foot through each base corner, and rays from the lamp over each top corner. They cross at each tip.

## Common mistake

Drawing the shadow's edges parallel on the page when the light is a lamp. The rays spread out from a lamp, so the shadow widens as it goes away from the box. The shadow of a sun, by contrast, keeps its edges parallel in space.

## Checking your page

Check each box against these:

1. The sun shadow is as long as the box is tall.
2. Every floor line starts at the base of the edge it belongs to.
3. The lamp shadow widens away from the box.
4. The shadow's edges run to the box's own vanishing point.
