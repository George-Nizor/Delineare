# The head

A head is easier to turn when you build it from a ball and a flat side plane than when you draw an outline. In this lesson you'll build a head with the Loomis method, named after the illustrator Andrew Loomis, so you can draw it facing any direction.

## Steps

1. **Ball.** Draw a circle, then a horizontal line across its middle. This is the brow line. Add a vertical line to show which way the ball faces.
2. **Side plane.** Cut a flat slice off the side of the ball: draw an ellipse inside it, guided by the cross lines. This is the side plane of the head.
3. **Face lines.** Drop a centerline down the middle of the face. Divide the face into thirds: hairline to brow, brow to the base of the nose, nose to chin. Draw a line at each.
4. **Jaw.** From the brow line, draw the far cheek and jaw down to the chin line. Square off the chin, then curve the near jaw back to the side plane.
5. **Turn it.** To change the angle, orient the cross lines first. The far side of the face gets narrower as the head turns.

Proko teaches Loomis's method in a series. Part 1 draws the average head from several angles, and Part 2 adjusts it to other head types.

## Common mistake

Drawing the side plane as a circle. It is a flat slice of the ball, so it's an ellipse, and it narrows as the head turns. Draw the cross lines first, then the ellipse to match them.

## Checking your page

Look at each head on your page:

1. The brow line and the centerline cross at right angles.
2. The three face sections are roughly equal.
3. The side plane is an ellipse, not a circle.
4. The face lines curve around the ball.

Draw one head from the front, one from three-quarters and one from the side.
