# The head

A head is easier to turn when you build it from a ball and a flat side plane than when you draw an outline. In this lesson you'll build a head with the Loomis method, named after the illustrator Andrew Loomis, so you can draw it facing any direction.

![Six Loomis heads, two from the front, two three-quarter and two in profile, each a ball with cross lines, a side plane, face lines and a jaw.](example)

## Steps

1. **Ball.** Draw a circle, then a horizontal line across its middle. This is the brow line. Add a vertical line to show which way the ball faces. Loomis builds the whole head from the point where they cross.

   ![A ball with a brow line across its middle and a centre line down the face, turned three-quarters.](ball)

2. **Side plane.** Cut a flat slice off the side of the ball, as Loomis does. Seen from an angle the slice is an ellipse inside the circle. This is the side plane of the head.

   ![A ball with an ellipse on its near side for the side plane, and the far one faintly behind.](plane)

3. **Face lines.** Drop the centre line down the face. Loomis puts the hairline halfway from the brow line to the top of the ball. Step down the same distance for the base of the nose, and again for the chin.

   ![The ball with a hairline, brow line, nose line and chin line in equal steps down the face.](facelines)

4. **Jaw.** From the side plane, draw the jaw line round to the chin, square off the chin and match it on the far side. Put the ear on the side plane, between the brow line and the nose line.

   ![The head with a jaw line from the side plane to the chin, and an ear on the side plane.](jaw)

5. **Turn it.** To change the angle, set the cross lines first. The side plane nearest you opens up as the head turns toward profile, and the far side of the face gets narrower.

   ![Four balls with cross lines and a side plane: front, three-quarter, profile and looking up.](turn)

Proko teaches Loomis's method in a series. Part 1 draws the average head from several angles, and Part 2 adjusts it to other head types.

## Common mistake

![Left, a full circle for the side plane on a turned head. Right, an ellipse that follows the turn.](mistake)

Drawing the side plane as a full circle at every angle. It is a flat slice of the ball, so it shows as an ellipse that opens as the head turns toward profile. Draw the cross lines first, then the ellipse to match them.

## Checking your page

Look at each head on your page:

1. The brow line and the centre line cross at one point on the face.
2. The hairline, brow, nose and chin sit in equal steps.
3. The side plane is an ellipse that opens toward a circle in profile.
4. The face lines curve around the ball.

Draw one head from the front, one from three-quarters and one from the side.
