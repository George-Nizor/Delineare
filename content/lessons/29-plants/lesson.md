# Plants and trees

A plant is built from the same cylinders and ribbons you have already drawn, and a tree is a mass with a structure inside it. In this lesson you'll draw a branch, a leaf and a spray from life, then simplify a whole tree into a few masses. Ruskin's drawing book, written in 1857, is the source.

## Steps

1. **Draw a fork.** Fix a forked branch about a foot long in place. Draw it full size, with light and shade, and draw whatever is behind it. Ruskin says to master the fork, where the structure shows.

2. **Turn a leaf.** Hold one long leaf with its point toward you, so you see only its thinness, and outline it. Tilt it toward you in three or four steps, and draw it at each, with its midrib.

3. **Draw a spray in profile.** Fix a spray about eight feet away against pale paper, with one eye shut. Draw each leaf mass as a black shape. Let leaves run together into one mass where they overlap.

4. **Step back.** Move the drawing a yard away, then a yard and a half, then two yards. Draw only what you still see. Thin stalks vanish and the leaves become dark patches.

5. **Draw the tree.** Ruskin sees a tree's branches carrying their smaller branches out to one rounded outer curve. Draw that outer curve first, then the trunk and the main branches reaching it.

## Common mistake

Drawing a tree as a flat shape, with branches only on two sides of the trunk. Ruskin notes that branches grow all round, and that the foreshortened ones, growing toward you, are the hardest. Put branches in front of the trunk as well as beside it.

## Checking your page

Look at your drawings next to the plants and check these:

1. The fork shows how the two branches join the trunk.
2. The leaf looks shorter in each position as it tilts toward you.
3. The profile spray reads as dark masses, not outlines.
4. The tree has branches in front of its trunk.
