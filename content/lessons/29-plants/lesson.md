# Plants and trees

A plant is built from the same tubes and ribbons you have already drawn, and a tree is a mass with a structure inside it. In this lesson you'll draw a branch, a leaf and a spray from life, then simplify a whole tree into one outer shape and its main branches.

![A page with a forked branch, one leaf in three positions, a black leaf spray and two trees drawn as an outer curve with a trunk and branches.](example)

## Steps

1. **Draw a fork.** Fix a forked branch about a foot long where it can't move. Draw it full size with light and shade, and draw whatever is behind it. Ruskin says to master the fork, where the structure shows.

   ![A forked branch with short shading strokes on its shadow side and the background drawn behind it.](fork)

2. **Turn a leaf.** Hold a long leaf with its point toward you and outline it, so you see only its thinness. Tilt it down in three or four steps, drawing each with its midrib and a flow arrow along it.

   ![One leaf in four positions, from edge on to full length, with a flow arrow along the midrib of three of them.](leaf)

3. **Draw a spray in profile.** Fix a spray about eight feet away against pale paper and shut one eye. Draw each leaf mass as a black shape. Where leaves overlap, let them run into one mass.

   ![A spray of leaves drawn as solid black shapes on a pale ground, overlapping leaves joined into masses.](spray)

4. **Step back.** Move the drawing a yard away, then a yard and a half, then two yards. Draw only what you still see. Thin stalks vanish first, and the leaves turn into dark patches.

   ![The same spray at one yard, a yard and a half and two yards, with the stalks gone at the second and only dark patches at the third.](stepback)

5. **Draw the tree.** Ruskin sees a tree's branches carrying their smaller branches out to one rounded outer curve. Draw that curve first, then the trunk and the main branches reaching it.

   ![A tree drawn as a dashed rounded outline, a trunk, and main branches that each reach the outline.](tree)

Ruskin's drawing book, written in 1857, is the source for the fork, the leaf, the spray and the tree. The flow arrow is Drawabox's.

## Common mistake

![Left, a tree with branches on only two sides of its trunk. Right, the same tree with short branches also coming toward you across the trunk.](mistake)

Drawing a tree as a flat shape, with branches only on two sides of the trunk. Ruskin notes that branches grow all round, and the ones growing toward you are the hardest. Put branches in front of the trunk as well as beside it.

## Checking your page

Look at your drawings next to the plants and check these:

1. The fork shows how the two branches join the trunk.
2. The leaf is shortest edge on and longest when held flat before you.
3. The profile spray reads as dark masses, not outlines.
4. The tree's main branches all reach one rounded outer curve.
5. The tree has branches in front of its trunk.
