# Building objects

Drawabox teaches constructional drawing: you break an object into simple forms, draw those forms on the page, then build detail on top of them. In this lesson you'll build a real object from boxes and cylinders, drawn through, and join the forms with intersection lines.

## Steps

1. **Pick an object.** Choose something plain, such as a mug or a stapler. Set it in front of you. Ignore its details, and look for the boxes and cylinders in it.

2. **Name its forms.** Look for the geometric forms first, then the rounder ones. Decide how they connect and which sits in front.

3. **Build them.** Draw the main forms, each drawn through. You're drawing a construction, not the object, so leave out details.

4. **Draw the joins.** Where two forms meet, draw the intersection line: the line that runs along the surface of both forms at once. It fixes how they sit together.

5. **Add a pass.** Add smaller forms that attach to what's already there. Before each pass, check that the drawing already looks solid. Thicken lines only where they overlap.

Drawabox gives this process in its Construction lesson.

## Common mistake

The most common mistake is trimming the construction lines to make it neater. The extra lines are the point. They train you to see the object in 3D. Leave them in, and draw every line at full strength.

## Checking your page

Compare your drawing with the object and check these:

1. Every main form is drawn through, including its hidden edges.
2. Each join sits on the surface of both forms.
3. Smaller forms attach to forms that are already there.
4. Thickened lines are only where lines overlap.

The extra lines will look messy, which is normal for a construction.
