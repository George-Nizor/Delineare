# Building objects

Constructional drawing means breaking an object into simple forms, drawing those forms on the page, then building detail on top of them. In this lesson you'll build a real object from boxes and cylinders, drawn through, and join the forms with intersection lines.

![A mug built from a cylinder and a curved handle, every form drawn through, with the joins marked.](example)

## Steps

1. **Pick an object.** Choose something plain, such as a mug, and set it in front of you. Strip its details away in your mind and look at the forms underneath.

   ![A plain mug, drawn as an outline.](pick)

2. **Name its forms.** Look for geometric forms first, such as boxes and cylinders, then rounder ones, then flat ones. Think about where they connect and how they cross.

   ![The mug with its two forms labelled: a cylinder for the body and a curved band for the handle.](forms)

3. **Build them.** Rebuild the forms on the page, each drawn through. You're drawing a construction, not the object, so ignore detail.

   ![The mug's cylinder and handle drawn through, with the cylinder's axis dashed.](build)

4. **Draw the joins.** Where two forms meet, draw the intersection: a line that runs along the surface of both forms at once. It fixes how they sit together.

   ![The same construction with a small oval where each end of the handle meets the body.](joins)

5. **Add a pass.** Add smaller forms that rest on what's already there. Check that the drawing feels solid before each pass. Then thicken lines only where they overlap.

   ![The construction with the rim's thickness and a foot ring added as smaller forms.](pass)

Drawabox gives this process in its Construction lesson and its Form Intersections exercise.

## Common mistake

![Left, only the outline of the mug kept. Right, the mug with its construction lines left in.](mistake)

The most common mistake is trimming or fading the construction lines to make the drawing neat. Drawabox says those extra lines are there to build your sense of 3D space. Leave them in and draw every line with the same confidence.

## Checking your page

Compare your drawing with the object and check these:

1. Every main form is drawn through, including its hidden edges.
2. Each join sits on the surface of both forms.
3. Smaller forms attach to forms that are already there.
4. Thickened lines are only where lines overlap.

The extra lines will look messy. That is normal for a construction.
