# Freehand boxes

Once you can plot a box with a ruler, you can learn to draw one freehand at any angle. In this lesson you'll draw boxes without a ruler, draw through them, and check each one by extending its edges back to see where they head.

![A page of five freehand boxes at different angles, each with a second pen's lines extending its edges back.](example)

## Steps

1. **Mark the vanishing points.** Put three vanishing points on the page, close enough to fit on it. Use a different symbol (an O, an @, a #) for each box's set, so you can tell the sets apart.

   ![Vanishing points on a page, marked as circles, at signs and hash signs, three of each.](vanishing)

2. **Draw a Y.** Draw three lines that meet at one point, with every angle between them greater than 90 degrees. The meeting point is the box's nearest corner. Drawabox calls this the **Y method**.

   ![Three lines meeting at the nearest corner of a box, each angle wider than 90 degrees.](y)

3. **Draw the box.** Ghost and draw the other edges, each aimed at its vanishing point. Draw all twelve, hidden ones too, as if the box were see-through.

   ![A box with all twelve edges drawn, the hidden ones dashed, and its three vanishing points.](box)

4. **Extend the edges.** When the page is done, take a ballpoint pen and start at the centre of the Y. Extend each edge back, away from you, along the edge itself. See how far each line ends from its point.

   ![A box with lines continuing each edge back towards its vanishing point and ending a little off it.](extend)

5. **Fill the page.** Draw five or six boxes on each page, changing the length and angle of the Y's arms every time. Use what the extensions showed on the next page.

Drawabox sets this as the first fifty boxes of its 250 Box Challenge.

## Common mistake

![Left, a box with its back corner ringed and its extended edges wandering. Right, a box whose extended edges meet at their points.](mistake)

The most common mistake is fixing the back corner because it looks wrong. Drawabox calls the corner a distraction, a symptom of edges that don't converge consistently. Look at each set of edges and where its extensions end, not at the corner.

## Checking your page

Check these on each page:

1. The page holds five or six boxes, each with all twelve edges.
2. No set of edges is parallel on the page.
3. Each extension runs back, away from you, along its own edge.
4. Each set of extended edges heads toward its vanishing point.

The extensions will rarely hit the points. They show you which way you drifted.
