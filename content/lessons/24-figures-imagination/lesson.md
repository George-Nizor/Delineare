# Figures from imagination

Drawing a figure from imagination means you decide the pose, instead of copying one. In this lesson you'll invent a pose, build it as a mannequin from the front, then draw the same pose from the side and from a three-quarter view. Then you'll check it against reference.

## Steps

1. **Decide.** Write one sentence naming the action, such as "a person reaching up to a high shelf". Draw a gesture line for it in 30 seconds.
2. **Front view.** Build the mannequin on the gesture: bean first, then limbs, using the head as a unit of height as in lesson 22.
3. **Side view.** Draw the same pose from the side. Keep the head height from the front view and carry each joint across at the same height.
4. **Three-quarter view.** Turn the same pose a third of the way round. Overlap the forms and draw through them.
5. **Check.** Find a photo of someone in a similar pose. Compare the tilt of shoulders and hips, and the number of heads tall.

Hampton builds a figure in this order: gesture, tilts and shapes, connections, landmarks, perspective. Peter Han talks through the thinking behind constructing a character in the Proko video.

## Common mistake

Looking up a reference before drawing anything. You then copy the photo, and the drawing doesn't show what you can invent. Draw all three views first, then check.

## Checking your page

Hold the three drawings side by side:

1. All three mannequins are about the same height in heads.
2. The shoulders and hips tilt the same way in each view.
3. Each joint sits at the same height in all three.
4. The reference photo differs from your drawing in tilt or lean you can name.

Mark the differences on your drawing in a second colour, then redraw one view.
