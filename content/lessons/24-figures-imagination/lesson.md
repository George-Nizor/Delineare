# Figures from imagination

Drawing a figure from imagination means you decide the pose, instead of copying one. In this lesson you'll invent a pose, build it as a mannequin from the front, then draw the same pose from the side and from a three-quarter view. Then you'll check it.

![One invented pose drawn from the front, from three-quarters and from the side, with dashed lines carried across at shoulder, hip and knee height.](example)

## Steps

1. **Decide.** Write one sentence naming the action, such as "a person reaching up to a high shelf". Draw a gesture line for it in 30 seconds. Loomis says most of the action must come from you, not from a model.

   ![A faint mannequin reaching toward a shelf, with one long gesture line through it.](decide)

2. **Front view.** Build the mannequin on the gesture: bean first, then limbs, using the head as a unit of height as in lesson 22. Hampton builds in this order: gesture, tilts and shapes, then connections.

   ![The reaching mannequin from the front, with a scale of head units beside it.](front)

3. **Side view.** Draw the same pose from the side, as Loomis draws his figure from front, side and back. Keep the head size and carry the shoulders, hips and knees across at the same heights.

   ![The same pose from the front and from the side, with dashed lines at shoulder, hip, knee and ankle height running across both.](side)

4. **Three-quarter view.** Turn the pose halfway between front and side. Loomis sets a mannikin in a box in rough perspective, divided by diagonals. Draw that box, build the mannequin in it and draw through the forms.

   ![A tall box turned halfway round, with diagonals on its faces and the mannequin standing inside it.](threequarter)

5. **Check.** Count the heads in each view. Loomis says you must watch balance carefully when you invent a pose, so check that the weight sits over the feet. Then compare with a photo of a similar pose.

   ![The reaching mannequin with a dashed line falling from its middle to the floor between the feet, and a bracket marking 7.5 heads.](check)

## Common mistake

![Left, a detailed face and hand floating on the page. Right, a faint action line through a head, ribcage and pelvis.](mistake)

Starting with a face, hands or clothes. Vanderpoel warns that with a vivid picture in mind it is tempting to draw insignificant detail, when the first lines should carry the movement and the big planes. Draw the gesture line and the three masses first.

## Checking your page

Hold the three drawings side by side:

1. All three mannequins are the same height in heads.
2. Shoulders, hips and knees sit at the same height in every view.
3. In each view the weight sits over the supporting foot or between both feet.
4. The three-quarter view shows both edges of each overlapping form.

Mark the differences on your drawing in a second colour, then redraw one view.
