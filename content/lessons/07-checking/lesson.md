# Checking your drawing

You get used to your own mistakes the longer you look at a drawing. In this lesson you'll learn four ways to see them again: stepping back, flipping the drawing, plumb lines and laying a photo over it. Then you'll list what's wrong and redraw it.

## Steps

1. **Step back.** Lean back or stand up and look at the whole drawing. Take a break first if you can. Proko notes that mistakes often show up straight away after one.

2. **Flip it.** Hold the drawing up to a mirror, or turn it upside down. The reversed image looks unfamiliar, so errors stand out. Ian Walsh suggests doing it often, before you add heavy rendering.

3. **Drop plumb lines.** A **plumb line** is a vertical that shows what lines up with what. Hang a weighted string at arm's length. Check that points aligned on the subject also align in your drawing.

4. **Overlay a photo.** Photograph the subject from where you sat, and photograph or scan the drawing. In an image editor, lay the drawing over the photo as a multiply layer. Proko then spends about ten minutes looking for errors.

5. **Name three errors.** Write down three, each with its place, direction and size, for example "chair back leans too far right". Redraw the subject, fixing them.

## Common mistake

Waiting until you finish the drawing. Walsh points out that an early error, such as one eye drawn higher than the other, throws everything after it off. Check the block-in before you add detail.

## Checking your page

Compare your redraw with your first drawing and with the photo:

1. You fixed each of the three errors.
2. No new error has appeared in the same place.
3. The plumb lines you tested still line up.
4. Overall height and width match the photo.

Where your eye and a measurement disagree, Proko says to trust your eye.
