# Animals

A dog, a horse and a cat share one plan: a horizontal trunk, a head at the front, and four limbs. In this lesson you'll draw that plan from a side view as masses, then add the legs. The method follows Edouard Cuyer's book on animal anatomy for artists.

## Steps

1. **Find the trunk.** Take a photo of a horse or dog from the side. Cuyer notes that in a quadruped the trunk runs horizontally, with the chest at the front and the belly behind. Draw one long, slightly sagging barrel.

2. **Mark the ends.** Draw an oval for the chest at the front of the barrel and a smaller one for the hips at the back. Cuyer's skeleton plates show both. Join them into one shape.

3. **Size the head.** Cuyer says beginners draw the head too small and the body too long. Measure the head in the photo and fit the trunk's length against it. Bourgelat gave a horse's body two and a half head lengths.

4. **Add the legs.** Cuyer compares a front leg with a human arm. Find the shoulder, elbow and wrist in the photo and draw each segment as a cylinder. Do the hind leg the same way from the hip.

5. **Check.** Hold the photo beside your drawing. Compare the head-to-body ratio, the leg lengths and where each leg meets the barrel.

## Common mistake

Starting with the head and drawing outward, which gives a small head on a long body. Cuyer names this as the usual error. Start with the trunk, then size the head against it.

## Checking your page

Compare your drawing with the photo and check these:

1. The trunk is one barrel, with the chest larger than the hips.
2. The head fits the body within a finger's width at arm's length.
3. Every leg joint lines up with the photo.
4. Each leg begins inside the barrel, not at its edge.
