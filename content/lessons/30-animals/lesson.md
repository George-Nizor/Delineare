# Animals

A horse, a dog and a cat share one plan: a horizontal trunk, a head at the front and four legs. In this lesson you'll draw that plan from the side as simple masses, then add the legs joint by joint.

![A horse in side view built from a sagging barrel, two ovals, a head and four cylinder legs.](example)

## Steps

1. **Find the trunk.** Use a picture taken from the side. Cuyer notes that a quadruped's trunk is horizontal. Draw it as one long barrel that sags a little in the middle, as Drawabox does.

   ![A long barrel with a slight sag on a ground line.](trunk)

2. **Mark the ends.** Drawabox puts the ribcage over about half the trunk's length and the pelvis over about a quarter. Draw a big oval at the front and a smaller, slanted one at the back.

   ![The barrel with a large oval over its front half and a smaller slanted oval over its back quarter, each marked with a bracket.](ends)

3. **Size the head.** Cuyer says beginners draw the head too small and the body too long. Measure the head in your picture and fit the trunk against it. A horse's body is about two and a half heads long.

   ![The head drawn above the barrel, with a bracket along the body marked in four ticks that make two and a half head lengths.](head)

4. **Add the legs.** Drawabox notes an animal has the same joints we do. On a front leg find the shoulder, elbow and wrist, and on a hind leg the hip, knee and ankle. Draw each segment as a cylinder.

   ![Four legs as cylinders, with a dot and a name at each joint on the near front and hind legs.](legs)

5. **Check.** Hold your picture next to your drawing. Compare the head with the trunk, the length of each leg segment and where each leg meets the barrel. A horse's body is as long as it is high.

   ![A dashed square from the horse's chest to its buttock and from its back to the ground, showing the body as long as it is high.](check)

Drawabox supplies the masses and the joints. Cuyer supplies the head ratio, the horse's proportions and the comparison of a front leg with an arm.

## Common mistake

![Left, a small head on a long body. Right, a head sized to the trunk.](mistake)

Starting with the head and drawing outward, which gives a small head on a long body. Cuyer names this as the usual error. Start with the trunk, then size the head against it.

## Checking your page

Compare your drawing with the picture and check these:

1. The trunk is one barrel, with the front oval larger than the back one.
2. The head is the same fraction of the trunk as in the picture.
3. Every leg joint sits where it does in the picture.
4. Each leg begins inside the barrel, not at its edge.
