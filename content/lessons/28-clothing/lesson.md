# Clothing folds

Cloth falls into a few fold shapes, and each one starts where the body holds, pushes or pulls it. In this lesson you'll dress a mannequin by finding those places first, then drawing the folds that run out from them.

![A mannequin dressed in a shirt and trousers, with a dot at each tension point and folds running out from the dots.](example)

## Steps

1. **Pose.** Draw a mannequin standing with one arm raised and one knee bent, in about 5 minutes. Use ovals for the ribcage and pelvis and tapered tubes for the limbs.

   ![A standing mannequin with its left arm raised and its right knee bent, built from ovals and tapered tubes.](pose)

2. **Wrap.** Draw the clothes as tubes round the limbs and one block round the torso, a little wider than the forms. Leonardo says cloth must fit the body and not look like an empty bundle.

   ![Clothing tubes wrapped round the mannequin, with the mannequin's forms showing in grey through them.](wrap)

3. **Find the tension points.** A **tension point** is a place where the body holds, pushes or pulls the cloth, such as a shoulder, an elbow, a belt or a knee. Mark each one with a dot.

   ![The clothed mannequin with a dot at each shoulder, each elbow, the belt and each knee.](tension)

4. **Draw the folds.** Run folds out from each dot. Leonardo notes that cloth far from a gathered place goes back toward flat, so make folds broader and softer as they move away. Curve them round the limb.

   ![Folds fanning out from each dot, short and tight near it and longer and softer farther away.](folds)

5. **Name them.** Label each group with one of seven names. Pipe is a tube of bunched cloth, drop a cone from one point, and diaper a bridge between two points. The picture shows zigzag, spiral, half-lock and inert.

   ![Seven small panels, one for each fold name, each showing the shape that fold makes.](names)

The fold names and what causes them are from jozlixart's Clip Studio TIPS article, and Doug Swinton's page uses the same seven. Proko lists compression and flag folds instead of zigzag and drop. Leonardo's Treatise on Painting is the source for fitting cloth to the body and spreading the folds.

## Common mistake

![Left, short wrinkles scattered over a sleeve with no starting point. Right, the same sleeve with folds running out from a dot at the elbow.](mistake)

Drawing wrinkles scattered over the cloth. Cloth only folds where the body holds or bends it, so every fold needs a dot to start from. Leonardo also warns against folds cut so deep they seem to break through the limb. Remove any fold with no origin.

## Checking your page

Look at the dressed mannequin:

1. Every group of folds starts at a marked tension point.
2. Folds get broader and softer the farther they run from their dot.
3. No fold looks deeper than the limb under it.
4. Folds curve round a limb instead of crossing it flat.
5. Each group carries one of the seven fold names.

Compare with the sleeve of a shirt you are wearing, bent at the elbow.
