# Clothing folds

Cloth falls into a small number of fold shapes, and each one comes from a place where the body holds, pushes or pulls it. In this lesson you'll dress a mannequin by finding those places first, then drawing the folds that run between them.

## Steps

1. **Pose.** Draw a mannequin standing with one arm raised and one knee bent, in about 5 minutes. Keep the forms simple.
2. **Wrap.** Draw the clothing as tubes wrapped round the limbs and torso, a little wider than the form underneath.
3. **Find the tension points.** A **tension point** is where the body holds or pulls the cloth: a shoulder, an elbow, a hip, a knee or a belt. Mark each with a dot.
4. **Draw the folds.** Run folds out from each dot in the direction the body pulls the cloth. Let the rest hang down with gravity. Change the fold shape where the body bends.
5. **Name them.** Label each group of folds with one of Proko's seven names: pipe, diaper, half lock, spiral, compression, flag or inert.

Marco Bucci teaches seven types of fold in his video, and Proko's lesson on the seven folds uses the same idea of tension points, gravity and movement.

## Common mistake

Drawing the folds as wrinkles scattered across the cloth. Folds begin at a tension point or a bend, so each line needs a starting dot. If a fold has no origin, remove it.

## Checking your page

Look at the dressed mannequin:

1. Every group of folds starts at a marked tension point.
2. Folds hang downward, away from the tension point.
3. The cloth is wider than the limb inside it.
4. Folds change direction where an elbow or a knee bends.
5. Each group carries one of the seven fold names.

Compare with the cloth on a person sitting beside you.
