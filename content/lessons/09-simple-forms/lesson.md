# Simple forms

Most objects can be broken down into spheres, boxes and cylinders. In this lesson you'll draw each of these forms, overlap them, and make the nearer ones larger so they sit in depth on the page. Seeing an object as a few simple forms makes it much easier to draw.

## Steps

1. **Choose three forms.** Draw a sphere, a box and a cylinder, each about as wide as your palm. Proko teaches that you can simplify almost any organic object into these three.

2. **Draw through.** Draw every form as if it were see-through, with the back edges of the box and the whole of every ellipse. Go round a sphere twice. Drawabox calls this **drawing through**.

3. **Overlap.** Draw a second form so it covers part of the first. The form you can see in full reads as nearer. Drawabox points out that drawings where nothing overlaps look flat.

4. **Make the nearer form larger.** Closer things look larger and farther things look smaller, so draw the nearer form of each pair clearly bigger than the one behind it.

5. **Fill the page.** Keep adding forms until the page is full, in one connected group rather than small clusters. Thicken the visible lines only where two forms cross, so you can tell which is in front.

Proko's Structure Basics shows how to place these forms, and Drawabox supplies the drawing-through and line-weight habits.

## Common mistake

The most common mistake is drawing each form alone, with gaps between them. Nothing is in front of anything, so the page reads flat. People do this when they're unsure of the forms. Let them cross, and draw each one through.

## Checking your page

Look at your page and check these:

1. Every sphere is a smooth, even circle.
2. Every box shows all twelve edges, including the back ones.
3. In each overlap, one form clearly sits in front.
4. Nearer forms are larger than the ones behind them.

An uneven circle won't read as a sphere, so redraw a sphere that wobbles on the next page.
