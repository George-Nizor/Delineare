## What a blockout is for

A blockout is a rough 3D scene made of primitives, used as reference for drawing. It answers questions that are hard to answer from imagination: what a box looks like from a very low camera, where a cast shadow falls from a lamp in a particular spot, how a pose reads from above. It does not replace construction drawing; you still build the forms on paper. Use it to explore viewpoints and to check your drawings, then put it away.

## The minimum in Blender

You need five things. Navigation: orbit, pan and zoom the view, and switch between perspective and orthographic. Primitives: a plane for the floor, then boxes, cylinders and spheres. Transforms: move, rotate and scale the primitives until they make a simple object or room. One light: a sun gives parallel shadows, while a point light gives shadows that spread out from the lamp. One camera: place it, look through it, and choose a focal length close to an ordinary phone photo rather than a very wide one, which stretches forms near the edges of the frame. Then take a screenshot to draw from.

Everything else in Blender can wait. Learn one step per session and draw at the end of every session, so the software never takes over your drawing time.

## Posable figures

For figures, a free posable figure tool or the app's mannequin does the same job: pose a simple body, then move the camera. A low camera makes the legs look long and the head small; a high camera does the opposite. Drawing one pose from two camera heights teaches more than one perfect render.

## How to use the reference

Draw first, check second. Predict what the forms will look like from a camera angle, sketch the prediction, then look and compare. When you draw from a screenshot, construct the boxes and cylinders as usual rather than tracing the outline, then overlay your drawing on the screenshot to check the edges. Keep the lighting simple: one main light gives a clear light side and shadow side, while several lights scatter the shadows and hide the form.

## When the app's tools are enough

The form viewer, the light tool and the mannequin cover most of what a blockout gives you, with the horizon, vanishing points and shading zones shown on screen. Reach for Blender when you need a specific scene, an unusual object, or a cut-out shape that the app's tools do not offer.
