## Big volumes first

Cars, boats, aircraft and robots are complicated on the surface and simple underneath. A car is a long box for the lower body with a smaller box or wedge for the cabin on top, wheels cut into the sides and the corners rounded off. A van is one tall box; a sports car is a low, wide wedge. Before any panel line, draw these big volumes in two-point perspective with their hidden edges, and check the proportions: wheelbase compared with overall length, height compared with width.

## Wheels and axles

Wheels are cylinders. Draw each axle line first, running across the car's width toward the vanishing point of the width edges; both wheels on one axle share that line. Each wheel's ellipse lies in the plane of the car's side, so its minor axis lies along the axle line. The front and rear wheels on the near side are ellipses of similar shape, with the farther one a little smaller and narrower as the car recedes. Cut the wheel arches as slightly larger ellipses around the wheels, and give the tyres thickness with a second, offset ellipse.

## Boats and aircraft

For a boat, start with a long box and carve the hull into it with three or four cross-sections from bow to stern: a sharp V at the bow, wider and flatter toward the stern. The deck is a plane laid over those sections, and the waterline is a contour wrapping the hull. For an aircraft, draw a side silhouette and a top view first, then a cylinder for the fuselage; the wings and tail surfaces are thin slabs attached along lines on it. Their position comes from the top view, so settle it there before drawing in perspective.

## Robots and machines

Build robots and machines from boxes, cylinders and spheres, and make every joint a real mechanism: a hinge is a cylinder that the two parts rotate around, a ball joint is a sphere in a socket. Ask whether each part could move without hitting another. A clear silhouette at thumbnail size matters more than detail.

## Surface detail

Panel lines, vents and seams are contour lines on the body. Each one follows the curve of the surface and stays consistent with the vanishing points. Add them last and sparingly, where they explain the form.
