## Clothing tells

Clothes and objects tell the viewer someone's job, wealth, era, climate and habits before they read the face. Design with that in mind: decide what the costume must say, then choose a few specific details that say it, such as a tool belt, a patched elbow, a badge, or boots worn down at the heel. Three telling details do more than ten decorative ones.

## Dress a body, not a shape

Draw the figure first, then the clothing over it. Cloth hangs from support points (shoulders, hips, a belt, a raised knee) and folds where it is pulled tight or squashed. Sleeves and trouser legs are tubes wrapped around limbs, so their hems and seams follow the limb's cross-section as ellipses. Thick or stiff materials such as leather, canvas and armour make few, large folds and hold their shape; thin, soft materials make many small folds and drape. Armour plates are curved shells over the body's forms, overlapping where the body bends.

## Props as families

A prop is an object designed for a character or a world. Design it in several versions before choosing one: the same lantern in brass, carved wood and scrap metal, or for a sailor, a miner and a priest. Build each version from primitives, then decide what makes it specific: material, wear, how it is held or carried, and how it attaches to the costume. Check every prop's size against the hand that holds it.

## Reference and history

Museums with open-access collections hold armour, costume, jewellery and tools from many periods, often photographed in enough detail to show how they were made: how a buckle fastens, how a sleeve is set into a garment, how a blade is fixed to a handle. Use them to learn construction, mix eras only on purpose, and record where each reference came from.

## Wear and story

Use, repair and damage carry story. A scuffed toe, a patched knee, a strap replaced with rope and the polished handle of an old tool each imply a history. Put wear where the object is touched or rubbed, not evenly all over, and give each mark a reason you could state in a few words.
