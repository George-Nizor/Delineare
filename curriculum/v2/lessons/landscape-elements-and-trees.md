## Shapes without outlines

Rocks, mountains, clouds, water, fire, smoke and trees have no fixed outline, so drawing them from memory needs rules. The same steps work for each: decide the light direction first, find the big masses, give each mass a light side and a shadow side, and keep detail for the area nearest the viewer.

## Rocks, mountains and clouds

A rock reads as solid when it has a few flat planes: a top plane catching the light, one or two side planes in half tone, a dark plane facing away, and a cast shadow on the ground. Draw those planes with straight or slightly curved strokes before any cracks. Mountains are the same planes at a huge scale, layered in overlapping ridges; distant ridges are lighter, lower in contrast and simpler. Clouds are lit volumes. A cumulus cloud has a fairly flat base and rounded, billowing tops, a bright side toward the sun and a grey shadow side. In perspective, clouds get smaller, flatter and closer together toward the horizon.

## Water

Calm water acts as a mirror. A reflection appears directly below the object and shows it as it would look from a point as far below the water's surface as your eye is above it, so the reflection shows more of the object's underside. Ripples break the reflection into horizontal strips and stretch it downward. Moving water shows its surface as shapes flowing around rocks, lighter where it breaks into white water.

## Fire and smoke

Fire and smoke follow rising air. Draw flames as a few tapering shapes that lean with the air, each made with one flowing line, brightest and most solid at the base and breaking into separate tongues at the top. Smoke rises as a column that widens and slows, then breaks into curling masses with softer edges; as it spreads, its edges get softer and its value moves closer to the sky behind it.

## Trees as masses

A tree is a trunk that forks and thins, carrying a canopy of foliage clumps. Draw the silhouette first, because species differ more in silhouette than in leaf detail: a conifer is a narrow cone, an oak a broad dome, a eucalypt an open, irregular crown. Divide the canopy into three to five clumps, each with a light top and a shadowed underside, and let some sky show through. In a forest, separate the foreground, middle and background trees into distinct value layers.
