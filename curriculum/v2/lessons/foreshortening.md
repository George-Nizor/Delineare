## The idea

Foreshortening is what happens to a form that points toward or away from you: its length on the page shrinks, its near end gets larger relative to its far end, and its cross-sections open up. It is ordinary perspective applied to a single form, so the same rules that make the far end of a road narrow make a pointing arm short and its hand large.

## Cylinders first

Draw a cylinder lying sideways, then turn it toward you in steps. Three things change together. Its drawn length gets shorter. The ellipses at its ends open up and get rounder, until a cylinder pointing straight at you shows almost a full circle with the far end hidden behind it. And the near end grows relative to the far end, more so the closer the object is to you. Draw the cylinder's centre line first; the ellipses stay square to it at every angle. Boxes behave the same way: faces turning toward you widen, faces turning away narrow, and the receding edges converge more steeply.

## Overlap does the work

On a strongly foreshortened form the outline alone cannot show length, so overlap carries it. Build a pointing arm as a chain of forms: the hand overlaps the forearm, which overlaps the upper arm, each drawn as its own volume, with the nearer one cutting into the silhouette of the farther one. The contour lines on each segment open up because you see more of their curve. If you draw the outline of the whole arm in one sweep, it reads as a short, flat arm rather than a long one coming toward you.

## Exaggerate a little

Foreshortening drawn exactly as measured often looks timid, because we expect to see the length we know is there. Push the near part a little larger and the far part a little smaller than you think, and keep the overlaps clear. Be careful with very close wide-angle photos, which already exaggerate foreshortening; copy them and you double the effect.

## Applying it to the figure

Treat the torso and pelvis as boxes and the limbs as cylinders. Pose the mannequin with a limb toward the camera and draw it from several camera heights. Photograph your own arm pointing at the lens and draw it as cylinders and a box before adding any detail. Keep one eye level for the whole figure, so that every part converges consistently.
