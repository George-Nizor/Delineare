## The idea

Once you can build a single object from primitives, three moves turn construction into invention: rotation, combination and redesign. Rotation means drawing an invented object from any view. Combination means fusing parts of two real objects into one that still makes sense. Redesign means changing an object's proportions and parts for a new user or purpose while keeping it buildable. All three work only because the drawing is made of forms you understand, not outlines you remember.

## Rotation

Draw the invented object's main form first as a box and decide its proportions as ratios: twice as long as it is tall, one and a half times as deep as it is wide. Those ratios travel between views; outlines do not. To draw the object turned, draw a new box with the same ratios at the new angle, then place every secondary form on the face it belongs to. A turnaround of four views (front, three-quarter, side and from above) shows quickly whether you really know the object, and light horizontal guide lines across the views keep the heights consistent.

## Combination and redesign

To fuse two objects, look for what they share. A kettle and a boot both have a main volume, an opening and something you hold; the fused object might have a boot-shaped body, a spout where the toe is, and a handle built from the heel. Join the forms with intersection lines that wrap both, so the parts look built rather than pasted together.

To redesign an object for a user, ask what that user needs and change the forms to answer it. A child's torch is shorter and fatter with a big switch; a diver's torch needs a sealed cap and a wrist loop. Write the reason beside each change, so the design stays a set of decisions rather than decoration.

## Check every invention

An invention you never check repeats your guesses. After drawing, find two photos of the nearest real thing, or build the main forms in the form viewer and look from your drawing's viewpoint. Mark what you got wrong in a second colour, then redraw from the same view. Extend box edges with a ruler, and lay a ruler across matching parts of your turnaround to compare proportions between views.

Studying old prints of interiors and machines for their structure, meaning the horizon, the boxes and how the parts join, adds to the library you invent from. Copy the structure, not the surface.
