## The idea

When you look at a familiar object you mostly recognise it, and recognition is fast and coarse: you know there is an eye, a handle, a lace. Drawing from that knowledge produces symbols, such as a tidy almond eye or a zigzag lace, that look like everyone's symbols and not like the object in front of you. Drawing from observation means following the actual edges and the shapes between them, one short stretch at a time, glancing between subject and paper often. The exercises in this module are ways to make that kind of looking happen on purpose.

## What each exercise is for

Contour drawing follows an edge slowly, with the pencil moving at the speed of your eye. In blind contour you do not look at the paper at all, so you cannot correct toward a symbol. The drawing will be distorted, which is expected: the purpose is the looking, not the result. Modified contour lets you glance down at junctions, where one edge meets another, so the drawing holds together while you still mostly watch the subject. Continuous line keeps the pencil on the paper across a whole group of shapes, which forces you to decide which edge connects to which.

The vase and faces puzzle and the upside-down copy work on the same habit from another side. In the puzzle the second profile has to mirror the first, so you end up steering by the shape of the space rather than by the idea of a nose. Turning a line image upside down makes its parts harder to name, so you copy angles and lengths instead. Treat both as looking games. Upside-down copying is sometimes sold as a way to draw more accurately, but studies that tested it found upside-down copies were no more accurate than upright ones, and the brain-hemisphere story often attached to these exercises is not supported by brain research. Accuracy comes from the measuring and checking modules.

## How to practise

Keep subjects simple and from life: your free hand, a mug, scissors, a shoe, a plant. Sit still, choose one starting point and begin at the edge nearest you. Let the pencil travel only as fast as your eye moves along the edge. Do several short drawings rather than one long one, and change the pose or the object each time.

## What to look for afterwards

Lay a set of blind drawings side by side. Do the angles come out too steep, the lengths too short, does the line drift off the edge of the page? These errors repeat across drawings and they are yours, so write the top two down. In modified contours, check that overlaps read in the right order: the nearer edge runs unbroken and cuts the one behind it.
