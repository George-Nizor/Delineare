## The idea

To draw an object you cannot see, you need a way to build it. Boxes and cylinders are that way: almost every manufactured object is a box or a cylinder, or a few of them joined, cut or rounded. If you can place a box and a cylinder in perspective, you can invent a toaster, a lamp or a radio by deciding which forms it is made of, where they sit, and how big each is compared with the others.

## How to build an invented object

Start with the largest form and draw it as a transparent box or cylinder, hidden edges included, with one horizon in mind. Decide its proportions on purpose: a radio is wider than it is tall, a torch is a long cylinder with a slightly larger cylinder at one end. Then add secondary forms by attaching them to faces of the first one. A handle is a bent cylinder whose ends meet a face; a spout is a cone growing from the side; a knob is a short cylinder on the front face, with its ellipse lying in that face's plane. Keep every new box converging toward the same vanishing points as the first. Only when the forms sit together do you round the corners or cut details.

Next, rotate it. Draw the same object turned about 45 degrees. Because you built it from forms, you know what it is made of and can rebuild it from the new view: the box turns, so its faces change shape; the cylinder's ellipses change degree; the attachments follow the faces they belong to.

## Study, hide, redraw

Invention improves as your library of real objects grows. Pick a real object with a simple build and draw it from two views as boxes and cylinders for ten minutes. Put it away. The next day draw it from a third view from memory, then compare with the object and mark the misses. Usually the proportions have drifted toward a generic version and the attachments have moved; those notes tell you what to look at next time.

## What to check

Extend the box edges with a ruler: each set should head toward one point. Check that every ellipse lies in the plane of its face, with its minor axis along the cylinder's axis. Look for missing hidden edges, which is where forms fail to meet. And check that the object reads as itself from both views. Do this every week rather than waiting until construction feels steady; drawing from imagination improves by being practised and checked, not by being postponed.
