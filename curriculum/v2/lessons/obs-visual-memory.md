## The idea

Memory drawing is a short loop: look at something with intent, hide it, draw it, then compare and mark what you missed. You can only recall what you actually noticed, so the loop shows you exactly what your looking skipped. It is also the bridge to drawing from imagination, because an invented drawing is assembled from structures you can recall, and this is the most direct way to store them.

## How to run the loop

Pick a simple subject: a mug, a shoe, a cube and a ball, the silhouette of a chair. Study it for about a minute with questions in mind. What is the overall shape? How tall is it compared with how wide? Which parts overlap? What angle does the main edge make? Then cover it or turn away and draw it in two minutes. Uncover it and mark every difference in a second colour: proportions, angles, overlaps, missing parts. Write one line on what you forgot.

Start with silhouettes and simple solids, where the answer is easy to check. Then add delay: draw the same object one minute after looking, ten minutes after, and the next day, and watch which features drop away first. Later, look at a scene for two minutes, such as a cafe table or a street corner, and draw it at home.

## What to look for

Most people forget the same kinds of thing. Angles come out wrong, especially shallow and steep ones; proportions slide toward a generic version of the object; overlaps vanish; small parts move or swap sides. Keep a short list of your own repeats, and next time you study a subject, check those features on purpose before you hide it. This is how the loop trains looking and not only memory.

Recall improves when you study structure rather than surface. A note like "the handle joins a third of the way down and sticks out half the cup's width" survives being hidden; "a nice curved handle" does not.

## How it fits the rest

After the gate, memory drawing becomes a few minutes in most sessions, and it is how revisits work in this app: draw the module's key idea cold, then check. Lecoq de Boisbaudran taught memory drawing in nineteenth-century Paris as a companion to drawing from nature, not as a replacement, and that is how to use it here. Every memory drawing ends with a check against the real thing; without the check it only rehearses errors.
