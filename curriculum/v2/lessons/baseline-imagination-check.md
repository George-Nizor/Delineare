## Why a baseline

Drawing from imagination is the goal, so it needs its own measure. Most checks in this curriculum compare a drawing with a reference; this one has no reference while you draw. The ten subjects touch every strand: a figure in motion, an animal, two objects (a chair and a car), a plant (a tree), two places (a room and a street corner), a hand, a head at an angle, and pure invention (a creature). At the start each one fails in a specific, readable way, and that is the point.

## How to run it

Find an hour without interruptions, or split it into two sittings of five drawings. Use the same paper size every time (half an A4 page per subject) and the same tool, a pencil or a fineliner, so later sets compare fairly. Set a timer for seven minutes per subject and stop at ten even if a drawing is unfinished. No reference and no erasing. If you do not know how something goes, guess and keep going: a guess shows you what to learn.

When the set is done, write one line under each drawing naming what you guessed. Be concrete: how the knee bends when the foot pushes off, where the wheel sits under the car body, what the underside of a chair looks like. Then photograph the whole set, dated, in even light.

## Reading the set later

At each phase benchmark you draw the same ten again under the same rules and lay the photos side by side. Look for three kinds of change, in this order. First, construction: are things built from boxes, cylinders, a mannequin or a head construction, or drawn as outlines? Second, consistency: does the room or the street keep one horizon, do the car's wheels match each other? Third, the number of guesses: are your notes getting shorter? Improvement usually shows first in construction and only later in finish, so do not judge the sets by how polished they look.

## Rules that keep it honest

Never redraw the baseline to make it look better; a record you edit stops being a record. Keep the prompts identical, in the same order and with the same time limits. Look at reference only after the photo is taken, and only to understand a mistake, not to fix the drawing. The baseline is not a test you pass or fail: the module is done when the set exists, is annotated and is stored where the benchmarks can find it.
