## Features are forms

Each feature sits on the curved front of the head and has its own volume. The eye is a ball set in a socket, and the lids are thick bands that wrap over it; in a relaxed face the upper lid covers the top of the iris and casts a small shadow onto the eyeball. The nose is a wedge growing out of the face: a narrow bridge, a rounder tip, and a wing on each side, with the nostrils on its underside. The mouth wraps around the curved front of the teeth, so its corners turn back into the face, and in profile the upper lip usually sits slightly forward of the lower one. The ear sits behind the middle of the side of the head, roughly between the brow line and the base of the nose, tilted back like the jaw; draw it as an outer C shape with an inner Y-shaped fold.

## Placing features on the construction

Build the head first and put the features on its guide lines. The eyes sit about halfway down the whole head; most beginners place them too high. The base of the nose sits about halfway between the brow line and the chin, and the line between the lips about a third of the way from the nose down to the chin. Draw each feature along the curved centre line and brow line, so that in a three-quarter view the far eye is narrower and the far half of the mouth shorter.

## Expression

Expressions come from muscles moving the brows, lids, cheeks and mouth together. Surprise lifts the brows and opens the lids wide. Anger pulls the brows down and together and tightens the lids. A real smile lifts the cheeks, which pushes the lower lids up and narrows the eyes, as well as raising the corners of the mouth. Sadness raises the inner ends of the brows. Draw six expressions on one head construction and change only these parts, then check each at thumbnail size: if it reads small, the big shapes are right. Faces are rarely symmetrical in expression, so let one side move more.

## Hair as volumes

Hair grows from the hairline and the crown and sits on the skull with thickness. Draw the skull first, then add hair as three to five large masses outside it. Show where it parts and which way it falls, give each mass a light side and a shadow side, and add single strands only at edges and partings. Straight, wavy, curly and coily hair differ in silhouette and in how the masses break up, not only in line texture.
