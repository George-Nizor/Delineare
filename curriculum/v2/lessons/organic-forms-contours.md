## The idea

Living things are not made of boxes. Stems, insect bodies, limbs, torsos and animal necks are soft, rounded volumes: sausages, eggs, teardrops and flattened balls. To draw them from imagination you need the organic equivalent of the box, a simple volume you can place in space and wrap with lines that describe its surface.

The basic unit is the sausage: two equal balls joined by a tube of the same width. Its silhouette is two parallel curves capped by round ends. Bend it and the two sides stay parallel around the bend; the width does not pinch or swell unless you decide it should.

## Contour lines

A contour line here is a curve drawn across the form, as if you had cut a thin slice through it and drawn the edge of the cut. On a sausage, each contour is part of an ellipse, and its degree tells the viewer which way the surface faces. Where the form points toward you the contours are open, close to circles; where it lies across your view they are thin, close to straight lines. Along a form that bends toward you and away again, the contours open and close to match.

Draw a central flow line first, then three to five contours where the form changes direction, and an ellipse cap at each end whose degree matches the nearest contour. Each contour must touch the silhouette on both sides; one that stops short or runs past it floats on the surface.

## Overlap and soft intersections

Overlap is the strongest depth cue for soft forms. Where one form passes in front of another, its silhouette runs unbroken and the form behind disappears behind it; the line of the back form stops where it meets the front one, making a T-shaped junction. Draw those junctions carefully.

When two organic forms join, they meet along an intersection line that wraps around both surfaces. Imagine pushing an egg into a ball: the line where they meet curves around the ball like a contour, and its shape changes as the ball turns. Draw it lightly first, then soften it where the forms blend into each other.

## How to practise

Fill pages with sausages in every direction, then add contours to some of them. Pile five to eight forms into overlapping heaps and check every junction. Then draw fruit and vegetables from life as organic forms before you commit to their outlines. These pages feed the plant, insect, animal and figure modules.
