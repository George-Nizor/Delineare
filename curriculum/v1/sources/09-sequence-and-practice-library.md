# Drawing instruction sequence, benchmarks and free practice library

Research date: 13 October 2026. Companion JSON: `09-sequence-and-practice-library.json` (12 syllabi, 8 consensus stages, 8 benchmarks, 54 practice resources, 3 weekly templates). Everything below was read on the page cited unless marked otherwise. Web search quota ran out partway through, so the later checks (museums, tools, communities) were done by opening URLs directly.

## How do universities and art schools sequence foundation and intermediate drawing?

### Takeaway
Across the syllabi I could open, a first drawing course runs the same ladder: contour and gesture, measuring and negative space, perspective, value, then texture, portrait or figure, and a final composed piece. Design-led schools (RISD, Edinburgh, RMIT) add idea generation and sketchbook work after or alongside the skills, and atelier schools (Florence Academy) go copy, then cast, then live model.

### Cited Findings
- Clark College's 8-week, 2-hour-a-week beginner class goes: contour and gesture and ellipses (wk 1), sighting (wk 2), negative space and first value (wk 3), more value and subtractive drawing (wk 4), one- and two-point perspective (wk 5), pen techniques (wk 6), portraits (wk 7), landscape (wk 8). No grading is described. — [Clark College syllabus, spring 2025](https://www.clark.edu/cce/class-resources/supply-lists/beginning-drawing-syllabus-spring2025.pdf)
- San Jose State's Drawing 1 (spring 2020) takes about 16 weeks: proportion and pictorial space first, composition and positive/negative space and contour in weeks 3-4, cross-contour and line weight in 5-6, gesture in 7, perspective in 8-9, value in 10-12, wet media in 15, head and figure in 16-17, final drawing and critique in 17-18. Grade is 75 percent class work including critiques and 25 percent homework. Meetings are Tue/Thu 12:00-2:50. — [SJSU Art 24 syllabus](https://www.sjsu.edu/art/docs/greensheets/syllabi_2202_spring2020/ART_024_04_Friedman_Erik_Spring%202020.pdf)
- Youngstown State's Foundation Drawing (fall 2026, the newest syllabus found) runs contour, gesture, positive/negative space, sighting and a linear still life, two weeks of perspective, value, drapery, texture, mixed media, portrait, then a final still life. Graded pieces are listed but weights are not. — [YSU ART 1521 syllabus](https://catalog.ysu.edu/syllabi/2026-fall/art-1521-01-40519/syllabi-202640-art-1521-01-40519.pdf)
- FUA's Foundation Drawing (15 lessons, beginner, no prerequisites) has outline, line and shape, negative space, composition and proportion with charcoal, hatching and tonal values, perspective, midterm, then a pencil and charcoal master copy, a personal still life and a final project. Weights: attendance 10, participation 10, midterm 20, digital album 20, final project 20, final exam 20 (percent). It asks for about 8 hours a week of preparation. — [FUA Foundation Drawing syllabus, hosted by CIS Australia](https://www.cisaustralia.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Foundation-Drawing.pdf)
- RMIT's vocational unit "Produce drawings to communicate ideas" (80 nominal hours, 21-week term, Certificate IV in Design) spends weeks 2-9 on seeing, line and shape, thumbnails, composition, hatching, pattern and texture, structure, perspective and stylised drawing, then weeks 11-21 on a self-directed drawing project. It is marked as competent or not yet competent, with a folio due in week 12 and a project in week 18. — [RMIT VART6406C course page, Term 2 2021](https://www1.rmit.edu.au/courses/C4382VART6406C2145)
- Monash Drawing 1 (2016 handbook) is 4 studio hours plus 8 independent hours a week and is graded only on a folio, 20 percent mid-semester and 80 percent at the end, with a journal inside the folio. It covers manufactured and natural forms and the figure, with proportion, structure, volume and space. — [Monash DWG1201, 2016 handbook](https://www3.monash.edu/pubs/2016handbooks/units/DWG1201.html)
- MIT's Student Arts Association class spends weeks 1-4 on small studies (mark-making, additive and subtractive drawing, line, value, shape), then larger drawings on volume, weight, gesture and composition. It is a paid 10-week class for the MIT community, not open courseware. — [Arts at MIT, SP26 Introduction to Drawing](https://arts.mit.edu/saa-classes/sp26-tuesday-introduction-to-drawing/)
- Edinburgh's Drawing Practices (SCQF 7, 30 contact hours) is concept-led: sketchbook, themed series, independent projects, group critique. It lists topics with no strict order and no marking weights; its learning outcomes cover research and ideas, technique, and selecting and presenting work. — [Edinburgh course descriptor LLLA07228](https://www.drps.ed.ac.uk/25_26_Archive_at_01-09-2025/dpt/cxllla07228.htm)
- RISD's Foundation Year drawing studio runs two semesters alongside Design and Spatial Dynamics and covers perceptual seeing, idea generation, abstraction, materials and performance, with group critique through each project. No weekly plan is published. — [RISD Foundation Year](https://risd.edu/academics/experimental-and-foundation-studies/first-year)
- Florence Academy of Art's one-year Foundation in Graphic Arts is figure drawing from the live model all year, Bargue copies by the sight-size method (pencil, charcoal, figure), cast drawing (feature study, then mask or head), etching, then copies and life-size portraits in trimester 3. To pass Bargue and cast work, students need accurate outlines, proportions and shadow shapes, plus a clear focal point and controlled values for casts. — [FAA curriculum 2024-25](https://florenceacademyofart.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Foundation-in-Graphic-Arts-Curriculum-2024-25.pdf); same three stages (foundations, figure and portrait, spring landscape) on the [programme page](https://www.florenceacademyofart.com/certificate-programs/foundation-in-graphic-art/)
- OpenLearn Create's free Drawing and Coloring Essentials is 24 study hours at Level 0: objects, figure, portraits, a bonus technique, still life, landscapes, then a quiz. A free Statement of Participation is issued on completion. — [OpenLearn Create course](https://www.open.edu/openlearncreate/course/view.php?id=4322)
- Proko's Figure Drawing Fundamentals orders its modules gesture, bean, structure, landmarks, robo bean, mannequinization, balance, exaggeration, proportions, measuring, shading. It costs $150 one-time, with 32 of 57 lessons free (about 5 of 15 hours). — [Proko course page](https://www.proko.com/course/figure-drawing-fundamentals)
- Grand Central Atelier's full-time core programme is listed as a Drawing Year, a Painting Year, then Advanced Study, with sculpture separate; length of each stage is not given. — [Grand Central Atelier](https://grandcentralatelier.org/)
- Thin pages, not entered in the JSON: New River CC's ART 121 lists line, perspective and proportion, gesture, value, composition, critique, live model (5 contact hours a week, no weeks given) — [NRCC ART 121](https://www2.nr.edu/art/pdf/art121.pdf); UGA's Drawing I is described only as building observation skills and visual vocabulary, 3 credits, 6 lab hours a week — [UGA ARST 1050](https://bulletin.uga.edu/Course/Details/5301); an atelier programme page (institution not named) lists cast drawing in year 1, still life in year 2, portraiture in year 3 — [Atelier Program page](https://www.fundtheatelier.org/the-atelier-program).

### Inferences
- The skills ladder is stable across US universities, a study-abroad provider, an Australian vocational unit and a community class, so the consensus stages in the JSON are well supported for stages 1-7.
- Stage 8 (ideas, sketchbook, invention) appears mainly in RMIT, RISD and Edinburgh and in the final-project weeks of FUA and YSU; it comes after the skills, not before.
- Perspective is the shortest block (1-2 weeks) in every skills-ladder syllabus; schools expect students to keep using it in later drawings rather than master it in isolation.

Animation Mentor's page lists 3D animation courses and states no drawing prerequisite — [Animation Mentor](https://www.animationmentor.com/). ## What pacing, consensus sequence and benchmarks follow?

### Takeaway
One foundation drawing unit is roughly 25 to 95 contact hours in the sources I opened, plus homework. Stages 1-7 below fit a 16-week semester. At 3, 6 and 10 hours a week, George would need roughly 30-50, 15-25 and 9-15 weeks per pass. The benchmarks are tasks with measurable checks, built from what the syllabi grade.

### Cited Findings
- Contact time per unit: Clark 16 hours (8 weeks x 2) — [Clark](https://www.clark.edu/cce/class-resources/supply-lists/beginning-drawing-syllabus-spring2025.pdf); MIT SAA 25 hours (10 x 2.5) — [Arts at MIT](https://arts.mit.edu/saa-classes/sp26-tuesday-introduction-to-drawing/); Edinburgh 30 hours — [Edinburgh](https://www.drps.ed.ac.uk/25_26_Archive_at_01-09-2025/dpt/cxllla07228.htm); RMIT 80 nominal hours — [RMIT](https://www1.rmit.edu.au/courses/C4382VART6406C2145); SJSU about 91 hours (5 h 40 min a week x 16, my arithmetic from the meeting times; the fetch summary said 5 h 50, which is wrong for 12:00-2:50 twice a week) — [SJSU](https://www.sjsu.edu/art/docs/greensheets/syllabi_2202_spring2020/ART_024_04_Friedman_Erik_Spring%202020.pdf); Monash 12 hours a week including independent work — [Monash](https://www3.monash.edu/pubs/2016handbooks/units/DWG1201.html).
- Block lengths in weeks, read from the syllabi above: contour and gesture 1-5; measuring and shape 2-4; perspective 1-2; value 1-3; texture, drapery and media 1-4; head, portrait, figure 1-3; landscape, space and final piece 1-4. The JSON's `typical_weeks` uses these ranges.
- Assessment criteria that recur: accurate proportion and outline, controlled value, a clear focal point, material handling (FAA), a folio and journal (Monash), participation in critique (SJSU, RISD), and reflection on your own work (Edinburgh). These shaped the pass signs.
- Atelier sequence for progression: copy flat images, then cast, then life, passing on proportion, shadow shapes and focal point — [FAA curriculum](https://florenceacademyofart.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Foundation-in-Graphic-Arts-Curriculum-2024-25.pdf).

### Inferences
- A part-time learner will take 2-5 times as long as a semester course for each pass. The benchmarks are what let George skip ahead or repeat, so run each one when a group is done rather than on a calendar.
- Benchmark tasks 3 and 4 (overlay accuracy test; posterised value check) give an objective answer without needing a teacher, which fits a learner who wants objective critique.
- The eight benchmarks are my design, not copied from any syllabus. The percent and thumb-width tolerances are rules of thumb, not published standards.

### Gaps
- No syllabus published a numeric accuracy standard, so the tolerance figures are untested.
- Hours per week for YSU, FUA, RISD and the FAA weeks per trimester are not stated; the JSON uses 0 for unknown.

## Which free timed-reference, photo and museum image sources exist, and under what licences?

### Takeaway
Plenty are usable for free: five timed-reference tools, a dozen photo banks and ten museum or archive sets. The clean-licence ones for master copies are the Met, Cleveland, Art Institute of Chicago, Getty and Smithsonian (all CC0) and the Rijksmuseum (CC0, public domain or CC BY 4.0 per item). NGV is the local option and is free for non-commercial use, not CC.

### Cited Findings
- Line of Action: the owner says the original practice tools stay free, free users see ads, paid tiers are Doodler $1.49, Apprentice $4.99 and Master $14.95 a month (USD), with a 33 percent student or teacher discount. Images belong to their photographers and no reuse licence is stated; some figure photos are nude. — [membership page](https://line-of-action.com/membership), [home page](https://line-of-action.com/)
- Quickposes offers timed practice with your own time limit, 20-image challenges and random poses; tips (3, 5, 10 euros) and a 15-euro desktop app are optional. It states no image licence. — [Quickposes](https://quickposes.com/en/)
- SketchDaily is a script-driven app that returned only a title to my fetch. A Proko thread lists its features (action or stationary poses, class mode, own-photo upload) and a pose-site roundup calls it free with no licence specified and advises practice use only. — [SketchDaily](https://www.sketchdaily.net/); [Proko thread](https://www.proko.com/community/topics/online-tools-for-practice); [roundup](https://doncorgi.com/blog/free-pose-reference-websites/)
- Magic Poser free tier is $0 forever: one model plus unlimited props, all editor features, 50 preset poses, one directional light, local saves and 2D image export. Not free: body morphs, more models, 3D import/export. Pro is $9.99 one-time; Master $14.99 a month; both have 7-day trials. — [Magic Poser pricing](https://magicposer.com/pricing-2.html)
- AdorkaStock's free pose gallery is under a Creative Commons licence (variant not named) that covers personal and professional art use; Sketch tool pricing is unstated; Patreon, Ko-fi packs and a pose book are paid. — [AdorkaStock](https://adorkastock.com/)

Flickr Commons has 100+ institution members — [Flickr Commons](https://www.flickr.com/commons). ## Which learning-science sources should shape the practice design?

### Takeaway
Five reputable items, each with a one-line drawing takeaway: spacing and self-testing beat rewatching, interleave subjects, feedback should be about the task and the next step.

### Cited Findings
- Dunlosky et al. (2013) rated practice testing and distributed practice as high utility and rereading and underlining as low. Drawing takeaway: redraw from memory and space sessions instead of rewatching lessons. — [APS summary page](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/publications/journals/pspi/learning-techniques.html)
- Roediger and Karpicke (2006): recalling material beat restudying on tests two days and a week later, even though restudying felt better and won on the 5-minute test. Drawing takeaway: close the lesson and draw its key idea before checking. — [Purdue lab PDF](https://learninglab.psych.purdue.edu/downloads/2006/2006_Roediger_Karpicke_PsychSci.pdf)
- Bjork and Bjork (2011): spacing, interleaving, varying conditions and testing make learning harder in the moment but more durable; a difficulty only helps if you have the background to meet it. Drawing takeaway: mix subjects in one session and keep difficulty manageable. — [UCLA Bjork Lab PDF](https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/13/2016/04/EBjork_RBjork_2011.pdf)
- Hattie and Timperley (2007): across 74 meta-analyses feedback averaged an effect size of 0.95; praise averaged 0.14 and corrective feedback 0.37; feedback on change from the last attempt scored 0.55 versus 0.28. Only the abstract and tables are free (the full text is restricted). Drawing takeaway: compare each attempt with your previous one and ask for task-level notes. — [SAGE page](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/003465430298487)
- The Learning Scientists offer free posters, slides and bookmarks for six strategies (spaced practice, retrieval, elaboration, interleaving, concrete examples, dual coding) under CC BY-NC-ND 4.0. Drawing takeaway: one poster on the wall is enough. — [Learning Scientists](https://www.learningscientists.org/downloadable-materials)

### Inferences
- The retrieval and spacing findings match George's preference for light retrieval over heavy notes: a 3-minute blank-page redraw is the retrieval step in all three weekly templates.

### Gaps
- Ericsson, Krampe and Tesch-Romer (1993) on deliberate practice is in the JSON as unverified: PsycNet and the mirror I tried blocked the fetch.
- I found no controlled studies specific to drawing skill in the pages I opened.

## What weekly templates fit a full-time job, jiu-jitsu on Tuesday and Thursday evenings, and learning by applying?

### Takeaway
Three plans (3, 6 and 10 hours) keep Tuesday and Thursday for jiu-jitsu, use Monday, Wednesday, Friday and the weekend for real sessions, and put one free "random crazy things" session in each week. Retrieval is a 3-minute blank-page redraw, not note-taking.

### Cited Findings
- The templates assume a full-time weekday job, jiu-jitsu on Tuesday and Thursday evenings (so those nights get little or nothing), and a normal bedtime. Missed sessions are dropped, not made up. This comes from the assignment brief; times of day should be checked against George's calendar before anything is scheduled.
- 3 hours: Mon 30 (lesson), Wed 30 (gestures plus memory redraw), Sat 75 (applied drawing plus comparison), Sun 45 (random crazy things).
- 6 hours: Mon 45, Wed 45, Thu 30 (optional, about 20:15), Fri 45 (sighting plus overlay check), Sat 120 (application plus master copy), Sun 75 (45 free play plus 30 recall and review).
- 10 hours: Mon 60, Tue 30, Wed 60, Thu 30, Fri 60, Sat 180, Sun 180 (90 free play, 30 review, 60 new medium). Tuesday and Thursday sessions are light and optional after training.
- Full session lists are in `weekly_templates` in the JSON.

### Inferences
- The 10-hour plan is demanding alongside full-time work and training; the JSON description says to drop to the 6-hour plan in heavy weeks.
- The master-copy blocks pair with the CC0 museum sets above; the overlay check pairs with Krita.

### Gaps
- Times of day are suggestions only; I did not check George's calendar.
