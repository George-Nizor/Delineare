Research date: 13 October 2026. All fetches were done on that date. The shared web-search budget ran out partway through, so the last third of the work used direct page fetches only (no new discovery searches). Anything I could not open is listed under "Gaps".

## Which free structured courses and teachers cover contour, negative space, measuring, block-in, sight-size, still life, memory, quick sketching and thumbnails?

### Takeaway
No single free course covers the whole group. The best free material is a set of single lessons and blog series: Proko's free Drawing Basics lessons on measuring (lessons 51 and 53), Liz Steel's free blog tutorials on sighting and quick sketching, Marc Taro Holmes's free workshop handouts, Urban Sketchers talks, John Muir Laws's free nature-journaling material, and the Sight-Size.com article library. Contour and still life have the weakest verified free coverage.

### Cited Findings
- Proko's "How to Draw Accurate Proportions" is lesson 51 of the 185-lesson Drawing Basics course, runs 12:04, is taught by Stan Prokopenko, and is free (a longer premium version exists). It covers flipping the reference to bypass assumptions, comparative measuring, pencil sighting, plumb lines, angles, using a unit such as head height, negative shapes, big-to-small layout, and checking by overlaying a photo of your drawing on the reference. — [Proko lesson 51](https://proko.com/course-lesson/how-to-draw-accurate-proportions)
- "Demo - Measure Proportions" is lesson 53 (7:57), a portrait demo that measures proportions and checks the drawing against the photo in Procreate. It is free. — [Proko lesson 53](https://proko.com/course-lesson/demo-measure-proportions)
- Lesson 52 ("Project - Measure Proportions") and lesson 55 ("critique measure proportions", 1:11:36) are premium only. The project asks for a portrait from a photo, block in the largest shapes first, and notes a careful layout may take about an hour. — [Proko lesson 52](https://proko.com/course-lesson/project-measure-proportions); [Proko lesson 55](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/critique-measure-proportions)
- Drawing Basics costs $159 one-time. The course page lists 185 lessons, 58 of them free. The visible Getting Started section shows 6 of 12 lessons free. The page does not show the lesson titles inside the Lines, Shapes, Values and Edges sections. — [Proko Drawing Basics](https://www.proko.com/course/drawing-basics)
- Proko's free "+Critique - Simplify Pear from Observation" is lesson 10 (9:30). It covers common mistakes in simplified pear drawings: shape design, proportion, asymmetry, shadow shapes. The related project and demos are not marked free. — [Proko lesson 10](https://proko.com/course-lesson/critique-simplify-pear-from-observation)
- Proko's "Project - Value Composition Thumbnails" is lesson 142 (7:07), free. The project: simplify supplied photos to three values in small thumbnails, at least five variations per photo, then pick one and make a larger sketch with up to five values. The submission deadline on the page (31 January 2025) has passed. — [Proko lesson 142](https://proko.com/course-lesson/project-value-composition-thumbnails)
- Proko's "Determining Proportions" (lesson 67 of The Art & Science of Figure Drawing, Brent Eviston, 19:58) covers pencil measuring, angle sighting and plumb lines but is premium only (course $119). — [Proko lesson 67](https://proko.com/course-lesson/determining-proportions)
- Liz Steel's index of free articles links to a video on sighting and measuring angles, a Foundations Friday lesson on measured setup, a post on continuous-line drawing, and a Quick Sketching Tips tag. Her paid products are the SketchingNow courses (Foundations, Edges, Buildings, Watercolour) and a Patreon. — [Liz Steel: how to improve your urban sketching](https://www.lizsteel.com/how-to-improve-your-urban-sketching/)
- Liz Steel's "Sighting and Measuring Angles (video)" appears free, embedded on the blog; the material was originally planned as Lesson 1 of her paid Buildings course. — [Liz Steel video post](https://www.lizsteel.com/video-sighting-and-measuring-angles/)
- Her Foundations Friday Lesson 5 ("Measured Setup") says measuring is slow but worth it when accuracy matters, suggests faint marks, and that you can measure only a few key edges and draw the rest by eye. In her words, "believable rather than precise is what to aim for". — [Liz Steel lesson 5](https://www.lizsteel.com/foundations-friday-2017-lesson-5-measured-setup/)
- "A Week of Continuous Line Drawings" treats continuous line as eye-hand training, suggests studying your blind contours for habitual angle and length errors, and gives loose practice rules (overlapping shapes, start at the focal point, leave some shapes undrawn). The post is free; the related Foundations course replay is for buyers only. — [Liz Steel continuous line post](https://www.lizsteel.com/a-week-of-continuous-line-drawings/)
- Her Quick Sketching Tips tag lists seven posts (including a 5-minute architecture series and a 30-minute scene post). Posts read as free with no paywall; the 5-minute post stresses finding the story first and getting big shapes down early. — [Liz Steel quick sketching tag](https://www.lizsteel.com/tag/quick-sketching-tips/); [5-minute: how to see](https://www.lizsteel.com/5-minute-sketching-architecture-how-to-see/); [30-minute scene](https://www.lizsteel.com/attempt-sketch-scene-30-minutes/)
- Her post on complex scenes recommends squinting to merge shapes, checking the large negative shapes (sky, foreground) before drawing, and using plumb lines on tricky foreground edges. — [Liz Steel complex scenes](https://www.lizsteel.com/tips-for-sketching-complex-scenes/)
- Foundations Friday Lesson 10 covers choosing a focal point and testing options with thumbnails (five labelled thumbnails of one rooftop view); Lesson 11 covers working outward from a focus and calls it a high-risk approach needing a strong base. Both read as free posts. — [Lesson 10](https://www.lizsteel.com/foundations-friday-2017-lesson-10-creating-a-focus-using-thumbnails/); [Lesson 11](https://www.lizsteel.com/?p=24267)
- Marc Taro Holmes's Citizen Sketcher site lists "Free PDF files" from past workshops, including The Broken Silhouette (2017 and 2023), Making Expressive Pen and Ink Drawings on Location, Drawing People in Motion, Actors on Stage and a Site Sketching Card Deck. His books and online classes are separate and priced elsewhere. — [Citizen Sketcher home](https://www.citizensketcher.com/); [Citizen Sketcher downloads](https://citizensketcher.com/downloads/)
- The Broken Silhouette notes describe a three-stage sequence: five 5-minute isolated-object sketches, three 10-minute sketches where the background is painted around the subject so it becomes a negative shape, and one 20-minute street view simplified by squinting and grouping buildings into fore, mid and back layers. — [Broken Silhouette notes (2017)](https://citizensketcher.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/the-broken-silhouette_chicago-2017_workshop-notes_marc-taro-holmes.pdf)
- A Holmes blog post compares painting a subject directly as a silhouette with painting the background around it; it is a public post with no paywall seen. — [Citizen Sketcher: positive vs negative shapes](https://citizensketcher.com/2018/05/22/comparing-positive-to-negative-shapes-in-direct-watercolor/)
- Urban Sketchers' USk Talks are described as a series of free lectures; Season 2 episodes include "Visual Storytelling: Practical Tips" and "How to Sketch a Country"; Season 1 includes a 5:11 clip "How to Improve Your Sketches". USk Prompts publishes a monthly list of daily prompts (archive back to 2022, latest October 2026) but the page does not state whether taking part is free. — [USk Talks](https://urbansketchers.org/usk-talks/); [USk Prompts](https://urbansketchers.org/usk-prompts/)
- Sight-Size.com (Darren R. Rousar, Velatura Press) describes sight-size as three elements: subject and artwork side by side, one fixed viewing spot you step back to, and recording what you perceive before departing from it. It contrasts sight-size (life-size) with comparative measuring (scaled to the source). The site says it has 100+ articles; the site also sells a cast-drawing book, ebook and video course. — [Sight-Size.com about](https://www.sightsize.com/about.html); [Sight-Size.com home](https://www.sightsize.com/)
- Wikipedia's summary: comparative measurement works at any scale and from any spot; sight-size needs a fixed setup; small errors can accumulate because each proportion builds on the last; sight-size is described as dating to at least the 17th century (Roger de Piles, 1708). — [Wikipedia: Comparative measurement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparative_measurement)
- Vitruvian Fine Art Studio's "Drawing Tutorials: Comparative Measurement, Part I" frames measuring as asking questions of your drawing, credits Deane G. Keller for the idea of comparing size and position (suggests adding angle), and gives the loop: pick two parts, judge the relation on the model, make the same comparison on the drawing, adjust. It says it is one of three planned tutorials; I could not find Parts 2 and 3. The author is not named and no price is stated. — [Vitruvian Studio](https://vitruvianstudio.com/blog/drawing-tutorials-comparative-measurement-part-1/)
- Betty Edwards's official site offers a Vase/Faces exercise page: trace a profile, draw the facing profile to complete a symmetrical vase, and notice where the verbal and visual modes conflict. Suggested ways round it include drawing from the bottom up, drawing a centre line, or turning the image upside down. The page does not state a time or price. Her books and workshops are sold on the same site. — [drawright.com exercise](https://www.drawright.com/try-an-exercise); [drawright.com home](https://www.drawright.com/)
- John Muir Laws's site lists a free Nature Journaling Curriculum download ("Opening the World through Journaling", 2nd edition, aimed at ages 8+ but usable by adults) and a workshop archive with titles such as "Shape vs. Structure: Integrating two ways of drawing", "Deep Observation and Nature Journaling" and "Gesture Sketching Birds". The archive page does not label individual items free or paid. — [Laws home](https://johnmuirlaws.com/); [Free curriculum](https://johnmuirlaws.com/journaling-curriculum/); [Workshop archive](https://johnmuirlaws.com/blog-archives/)

The page asks for donations and promotes a paid Stanford masterclass; it does not state the video's price. — [Laws: Fundamental Drawing Strategies](https://johnmuirlaws.com/fundamental-drawing-strategies-1-8-2026/)

- Sight-Size.com's "Memory Training for Painters" abridges Richard Lack's 1990 article: memory work supplements observation, suggested exercises are recalling geometric shapes, silhouettes and colour swatches, with form study getting three to four times the time of colour study. — [Sight-Size.com memory article](https://www.sightsize.com/memory-training-for-painters/)

### Inferences
- Because Proko lesson 51 touches measuring, plumb lines, angles, negative shapes, big-to-small and overlay checking in one 12-minute lesson, it works as core for several modules at once; the other modules lean on Liz Steel, Holmes and Sight-Size.com for depth.
- Liz Steel's own tag pages treat a rough early layout (big shapes, then measured edges) as the common thread between quick sketching and accuracy work, which supports sequencing thumbnails and block-in before location sketching.
- Proko's lesson 51 refers to a separate pencil-sighting lesson. I could not see its title because the course page collapses the Lines and Shapes sections, so I did not link it.
- What photos train versus life (obs-photo-vs-life) rests on my own reasoning (flat image, fixed viewpoint, lens distortion, compressed values, but a repeatable reference you can overlay). I did not find a primary source making these points, so the module's claims are worded as practice design, not as cited fact.

## Where are legitimate free copies of Bargue's Drawing Course plates and similar public-domain study material?

### Takeaway
The Rijksmuseum holds 9 Bargue print records and gives at least one verified Cours de dessin plate (Part I, Parthenon frieze) a Public Domain Mark with a download button; the other two records I opened are CC0. I could not confirm a complete free scan set of all 197 plates anywhere (Wikimedia Commons, Gallica and Internet Archive checks all failed or returned nothing). For wider master-copy practice, the Met, Art Institute of Chicago, Cleveland, National Gallery of Art and Smithsonian all publish free open-access images.

### Cited Findings
- Rijksmuseum object "Twee ruiters, deel van het fries van het Parthenon": Bargue as printmaker, Lemercier & Cie printer, Goupil & Cie publisher, 1866-1871, lithograph 443 x 588 mm. The page says it belongs to part one of the Bargue and Gerome Cours de dessin, is under the Creative Commons Public Domain Mark 1.0, and has a "Download image" option. — [Rijksmuseum plate](https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/collection/object/Twee-ruiters-deel-van-het-fries-van-het-Parthenon--500c28a74c24aeb1d55930863aa4f648)
- The Rijksmuseum data service returns 9 print records with creator Bargue (ids 200153108 to 200153113, 200622564, 200724204, 200724209). Two I opened are CC0 but their records do not say Cours de dessin: a Bargue print after Bouguereau (1855-56) and a Bargue print after a Holbein drawing (c. 1875-1900). The Holbein one fits Part II (master drawings) but the record does not confirm that. — [Rijksmuseum data search](https://data.rijksmuseum.nl/search/collection?creator=Bargue&type=print); [record 200153108](https://id.rijksmuseum.nl/200153108); [record 200622564](https://id.rijksmuseum.nl/200622564)
- Wikipedia describes the Drawing Course as 197 lithographs from Goupil & Cie with Jean-Leon Gerome: Part I casts (1868), Part II master drawings (1870), Part III living model (1873). It gives inconsistent dates (1860s, 1868-1873, 1866) and names a complete surviving set at the V&A National Art Library; it links no free digitised copy. — [Wikipedia: Charles Bargue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Bargue)
- A V&A API query for Bargue returned zero records, and an Internet Archive query for creator Bargue plus title "cours de dessin" returned zero results, so I found no free V&A or Internet Archive copy. A Wikimedia Commons category fetch and a Gallica query both failed to load. — [V&A API query](https://api.vam.ac.uk/v2/objects/search?q=Bargue%20cours%20de%20dessin&page_size=10); [Internet Archive query](https://archive.org/advancedsearch.php?q=title%3A%28cours+de+dessin%29+AND+creator%3Abargue&fl%5B%5D=identifier&rows=20&output=json)
- barguedrawing.wordpress.com (Indra Anderson) offers printable PDFs of Parts 1 to 3 and 300 DPI scans of Part 1, made by scanning Gerald Ackerman's book. The author says the plates may not be sold but can be used for atelier education. I did not link it: the scans come from a 20th-century book reproduction and the copyright position of those scans is unclear. — [Bargue's Drawing Course blog](https://barguedrawing.wordpress.com/)
- Art Renewal Center's Bargue Encore project reproduces 10 plates as lithographs sold at $149 each in 2021 (pre-order $115); buyers get access to two online courses. This is a paid product, not a free copy. — [ARC: Bargue Encore](https://www.artrenewal.org/articles/bargue-encore/166)
- The Met: public-domain images are free for unrestricted use under CC0; 406,000+ high-resolution images; filter search by Open Access; a few Open Access-tagged works may lack an image on the site and need a paid request via Art Resource. — [Met image resources](https://www.metmuseum.org/about-the-met/policies-and-documents/image-resources)
- Art Institute of Chicago: 50,000+ public-domain images under CC0 with a credit-line request (Artist, Title, Date, The Art Institute of Chicago); download button on public-domain artwork pages only. Its API returned three Bargue works marked public domain (Seated Arab c. 1850, Man in Eighteenth-Century Dress, Armed Arab Leaning Against a Wall); I did not confirm they are Cours de dessin plates. — [AIC open access images](https://www.artic.edu/open-access/open-access-images); [AIC API query](https://api.artic.edu/api/v1/artworks/search?q=Bargue&fields=id,title,artist_display,is_public_domain,date_display&limit=20)
- Cleveland Museum of Art: CC0 images and metadata, `cc0=1` search filter, download button on object pages, API with 61,000+ records, third-party copies on Wikimedia Commons and Internet Archive. — [Cleveland open access](https://www.clevelandart.org/open-access)
- National Gallery of Art: Open Access images free of charge for any use, "more than 60,000" images, download from object pages or through a downloadable-images search; the page does not mention drawings specifically and warns the Gallery may charge for extra imaging services. — [NGA open access](https://www.nga.gov/open-access-images.html)
- Smithsonian Open Access: 5.2 million+ 2D and 3D items, "Just browsing" link for CC0 media, content from museums, research centres, libraries, archives and the National Zoo. — [Smithsonian Open Access](https://www.si.edu/openaccess)
- Florence Academy of Art's one-year Foundation in Graphic Arts has students copy one simpler Bargue in pencil (about 10x14 in), one more advanced Bargue in charcoal (about 18x20 in) and a Bargue figure copy, to learn outline, proportion, shadow shapes and values; two cast drawings in charcoal follow. — [FAA Foundation curriculum 2024-25](https://florenceacademyofart.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Foundation-in-Graphic-Arts-Curriculum-2024-25.pdf)

### Inferences
- A practical way to copy a Rijksmuseum plate is to print it, or display it at the same size as your paper, and set it beside the drawing. That recreates a rough sight-size comparison.
- Because Bargue died in 1883 and Gerome in 1904, the original plates are old enough to be public domain, and the Rijksmuseum's marks agree. Modern book reproductions can carry their own claims, which is why I avoided the WordPress scans.
- The Rijksmuseum Bargue records outside the Parthenon plate may or may not be Cours de dessin plates. A user should treat them as "Bargue lithographs after masters" and use them as master-copy practice either way.

### Gaps
- No verified free complete set of the 197 plates. Not confirmed: Gallica (BnF), Musee Goupil (Bordeaux), V&A, Wikimedia Commons, Internet Archive.
- Rijksmuseum's own search page blocks fetch tools (robots), so I used the data-service query; the full list of titles was not read.
- I did not open NGA, Met or Cleveland object pages for specific drawings, only the terms pages.

## What do atelier and art-school syllabi assign in a first drawing term, and how many hours do they expect?

### Takeaway
Community-college Drawing I courses run a semester of roughly 90 contact hours and sequence contour, blind contour, shape and space, sighting and measuring, then value and perspective. A full-time atelier year puts about 35 hours a week on site and starts with Bargue copies and sight-size before cast drawing.

### Cited Findings
- Northwest Arkansas Community College ART 1303 outline: 3 credit hours, 45 lecture plus 45 lab contact hours, load hours 4.67. Topics include contour line, positive/negative shapes, sighting and measuring of linear dimensions and angles (including a "Mondrian tool"), gesture, master study, figure and landscape. Outcomes include applying sighting, measuring, proportion, space, shape, value, line variation, perspective, gesture and composition. Still life is not listed. — [NWACC ART 1303 outline](https://www.nwacc.edu/_documents/courseoutlines/art1303.pdf)
- Jamestown Community College (SUNY) ART 1510 Drawing I: 3 credit hours, lecture/lab, weekly contact hours and outside work not specified. Topics include contour line (contour, blind contour, shaded, gesture), positive and negative space, gesture, value, perspective, hatching, composition, sketchbook, portfolio and critique. Sighting, proportion and still life are not listed. — [JCC ART 1510](https://www.sunyjcc.edu/sites/default/files/ics/ART%201510.pdf)
- An ART 121 syllabus hosted on nr.edu (instructor Parks; the institution appears to be New River Community College, inferred from the domain) meets MWF 11:15am-1:15pm and runs 17 weeks. Week 1: gesture and contour, blind contour of objects then hands. Week 2: heavy contour of classroom objects, a foot in contour, cross-contours, line networks, intro to value. Later weeks cover pointillism, perspective, conte still lifes, a grid self-portrait, charcoal still life, locational sketching (Week 10), and copying a famous work with a grid at twice the size. Outside work is not quantified beyond sketchbooks done as homework. — [nr.edu ART 121 syllabus](https://www.nr.edu/art/pdf/art121_syllabus_PARKS.pdf)
- Florence Academy of Art Foundation in Graphic Arts (one year, three trimesters): classes 9am-4pm Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday and Friday (28 hours), plus Thursday 9am-4pm studio lab or museum visits (about 35 hours on site per week), Monday evening anatomy and Friday evening art history. Sight-size is introduced during the Bargue exercises as the accurate measuring method; in cast drawing it is a guide and accuracy depends more on the eye. Anatomy students should expect 1-2 hours a week of outside drawing. — [FAA Foundation curriculum 2024-25](https://florenceacademyofart.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Foundation-in-Graphic-Arts-Curriculum-2024-25.pdf)
- Royal Drawing School's Drawing Foundations is a self-paced online course, one class a week for eight weeks, with a year of access; total hours are not stated. — [Royal Drawing School FAQ](https://royaldrawingschool.org/here-to-help/faqs/drawing-foundations)
- The Getty/Royal Drawing School "Learning to Draw" class on 12 January 2026 was a live two-hour online session needing an Eventbrite ticket (price not stated) and is now past. — [Getty event page](https://www.getty.edu/calendar/learning-to-draw-royal-drawing-school)

### Inferences
- My module hour ranges add up to about 77 to 170 hours across the 14 modules. That is in line with one semester of Drawing I (90 contact hours at NWACC) plus homework, though a self-taught learner has no instructor feedback, so the upper end is safer.
- All three community-college outlines start with contour and shape, and the one that includes sighting puts it in the first half. Atelier training puts copy-and-measure work (Bargue, sight-size) before cast and life drawing. The module order (seeing, contour, negative space, measuring, block-in, sight-size, still life, then copies) matches both.

### Gaps
- Valencia College's Drawing 1 syllabus (McGrath, fall 2022) appeared in a search but failed to open, so it is not used.
- No syllabus I opened states outside-class hours per week as a number.
- Charles H. Cecil Studios, Grand Central Atelier and Watts Atelier syllabi were not checked.
