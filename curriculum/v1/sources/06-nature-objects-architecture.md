Companion file: 06-nature-objects-architecture.json (17 modules, group id "nature-objects-architecture", ids prefixed "nat-"). In the JSON, `verified: true` means I opened the page and what I saw supports the stated free/paid boundary. `verified: false` means the page exists (or appeared in search results) but I could not confirm contents or price, or the site blocked me.

## Which free structured courses cover these subjects, and what is free versus paid?

### Takeaway
John Muir Laws's site is the strongest free, structured source for plants, insects, birds, mammals, landscapes and water, with a clear lesson order on each page. For vehicles the best verified free course is the Petersen Automotive Museum x Yellowbrick auto design course, and for architecture and urban sketching it is Liz Steel's free nine-lesson Sketching Architecture section plus Marc Taro Holmes's free PDFs and videos. Reptiles/fish, rocks/mountains, fire/smoke, ships/aircraft/robots, food and ornament have no verified dedicated free course, so those modules lean on method transfer and reference banks.

### Inferences
- Laws's pages are the closest thing to a free, ordered course for nature subjects, because each page repeats the same pattern: basic shape, then anatomy or structure, then surface. The modules for mammals, birds, insects and plants use that pattern.
- For Melbourne (George's location) the most relevant free local source is Museums Victoria Collections for specimens, plus the Atlas of Living Australia for Australian species photos.
- Reptiles/amphibians/fish, rocks/mountains, fire/smoke, food, ships/aircraft/robots and ornament lack verified free structured teaching in my results, so the JSON marks them as method transfer and says so in the resource notes.

## Which free reference banks suit these subjects, and under what licence?

### Takeaway
CC0 banks from the Smithsonian and the Met cover animals, objects, ornament and still life; the Biodiversity Heritage Library, Haeckel and Muybridge cover historical plates and motion; Museums Victoria and the Atlas of Living Australia cover local species; the Library of Congress HABS/HAER survey is the best free source of measured architectural drawings. Pexels and Unsplash photo licences allow free use for drawing practice; the WMO Cloud Atlas and NASA have usage conditions.

### Cited Findings
- Smithsonian Open Access: more than 5.2 million 2D and 3D items, downloadable and reusable without asking, with a CC0 filter linked. — [Smithsonian Open Access](https://www.si.edu/openaccess)
- Smithsonian 3D exposes rotatable models (a Triceratops, ichthyosaur bone, mammoth fossil, hominin fossils) but states no licence on its landing page; terms are at si.edu/Termsofuse. — [Smithsonian 3D](https://3d.si.edu/)
- The Met Open Access: over 492,000 works with CC0 images and data, flagged with an OA icon. — [The Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/about-the-met/policies-and-documents/open-access)
- Rijksmuseum: freely available works can be downloaded in high resolution for personal and commercial use (CC0 not named on that page). — [Rijksmuseum](https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/about-collection-online)
- Museums Victoria Collections: data is CC0, curator text CC BY 4.0, images licensed separately, with over 100,000 images openly licensed or public domain; credit authors. — [Museums Victoria about](https://collections.museumsvictoria.com.au/about), [collections](https://collections.museumsvictoria.com.au/)
- Atlas of Living Australia: open-access biodiversity data hosted by CSIRO, with individually licensed photos (some CC BY, some CC BY-NC); site text is CC BY 3.0 Australia. — [ALA](https://www.ala.org.au/)
- BHL is a free-access biodiversity library supported by Smithsonian Libraries and Archives; the home page states no licence, and its Flickr stream shows 319,661 photos with no rights label visible on that page. — [BHL](https://www.biodiversitylibrary.org/), [BHL Flickr](https://www.flickr.com/photos/biodivlibrary)
- Haeckel's Kunstformen der Natur (100 plates, 1899 to 1904) has free scans listed on Wikipedia: BHL item 182319, Internet Archive KunstformenderN00Haec and a BHL Flickr album. I could not open the BHL item. — [Wikipedia: Kunstformen der Natur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunstformen_der_Natur)
- Muybridge's Animal Locomotion (1887) is grouped on Wikimedia Commons in 11 volumes (horses, domestic and wild animals, birds, humans); licence is shown per file. — [Commons: Animal Locomotion](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Animal_Locomotion)
- Owen Jones's The Grammar of Ornament (1856): Internet Archive scan from the Getty Research Institute with PDF, EPUB and full text, metadata "NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT". A Heidelberg digitisation exists but returned an access-denied bot page to me. — [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/gri_33125008700086), [Wikipedia: The Grammar of Ornament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Grammar_of_Ornament)
- Library of Congress HABS/HAER/HALS: more than 581,000 measured drawings, photographs and histories for over 43,000 US structures and sites; the "Rights And Restrictions" text was not shown to me. — [LoC HABS/HAER/HALS](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/collection/hh/)
- NASA media: images are generally not subject to US copyright when used non-commercially, but the insignia is excluded, third-party images need their owner's permission, and some uses need clearance. — [NASA media guidelines](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/)
- Unsplash licence: free for commercial and noncommercial use, no permission or attribution needed, but you cannot compile images into a competing service. — [Unsplash licence](https://unsplash.com/license)
- Pexels licence: free use and edits, no attribution required, but no selling unaltered copies, no redistribution on other stock sites, no implied endorsement. — [Pexels licence](https://www.pexels.com/license/)
- WMO International Cloud Atlas is the official cloud classification site; the footer reads 2017 WMO copyright and no open licence was visible. — [WMO Cloud Atlas](https://cloudatlas.wmo.int/en/home.html)
- Wikipedia's "List of roof shapes" names roof types in families (gable, hip, mansard/gambrel, arched, domed) — [Wikipedia: List of roof shapes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_roof_shapes)
- explore.org describes itself as a live nature cam network with YouTube channels for bears, oceans, eagles and birds and Africa; fees are not stated. — [explore.org](https://explore.org/livecams)

### Inferences
- For practice references, CC0 sources (Smithsonian, Met, Museums Victoria data) are the safest when you might later publish a drawing based on a photo; Pexels and Unsplash are fine for private study; BHL and Commons need a per-item check.
- The HABS/HAER drawings are the most useful lesser-known source for architecture because they show plans, elevations and sections at scale.
- Using Muybridge plates for gait study is low risk legally because the originals are 19th-century; still check each Commons file's licence tag.

### Gaps

- ColBase (Japanese national museums, JS-rendered), Gallica (BnF, French; free status not stated on the landing page) and Heidelberg (bot protection) could not be checked, so they are not in the JSON. — [Gallica](https://gallica.bnf.fr/)

## How do illustration and concept-art syllabi order these subjects?

### Takeaway
Every ordered sequence I could verify moves from simple natural structures to more complex animals, then to made objects and vehicles, and puts architecture after perspective: Drawabox goes plants, insects, animals, objects, vehicles; Liz Steel goes essentials, perspective, styles, elements, techniques, interiors; the Petersen course goes principles, case studies, then drawing. I found no verified university syllabus.

### Cited Findings
- Drawabox lesson order from public titles: 3 plants, 4 insects and arachnids, 5 animals, 6 everyday objects, 7 vehicles. — [L3](https://drawabox.com/community/submission/D5WFEQ2K), [L4](https://drawabox.com/community/submission/E74AS6L9), [L5](https://drawabox.com/community/submission/EF0NVKA), [L7](https://drawabox.com/community/submission/4MIZXWJ1/)
- Laws's plant series moves from introduction and symmetry to foreshortening, leaves and petals, then curling leaves; his mammal pages move from block-in, to anatomy, to fur texture. — [Laws: Plants](https://johnmuirlaws.com/drawing-plants/), [Laws: Mammals](https://johnmuirlaws.com/draw-mammals/)
- Liz Steel's architecture lessons: essentials, perspective, styles, elements and details, techniques, interiors, then inspiration, Palladio and design sketching. — [Liz Steel](https://www.lizsteel.com/sketching-architecture/)
- The Petersen/Yellowbrick course runs principles and history, case studies, then a drawing module that starts with perspective, circles and ellipses before cars. — [Yellowbrick](https://yellowbrick.co/autodesign)
- Kew's botanical illustration offering starts with a fundamentals course (measurement, perspective, then watercolour) and has separate beginner and intermediate pen-and-ink courses. — [Kew](https://www.kew.org/kew-gardens/whats-on/fundamentals-of-botanical-illustration)
- Cornell's Bird Academy groups its art offerings as a paid field-sketching course and a paid bird painting course, with free webinars alongside. — [Bird Academy](https://academy.allaboutbirds.org/)

### Inferences
- The ordering used in the JSON follows these patterns: field-sketching habit first, then plants, insects, mammals, birds, reptiles/fish, then gesture and texture, then creature design; landscape then trees in landscapes; everyday objects before hard-surface; architecture after perspective, then streets; food and ornament as lighter side modules.
- Suggested sequence for George: field-sketching habit, plants, insects, mammals, birds, everyday objects, architecture, landscape elements, then the rest. This mixes natural and built subjects to avoid long runs of one kind, in line with his stated wish for variety. This is my recommendation, not something a source says.
- Cross-group prerequisites in the JSON use real ids from the sibling files already on disk (01 foundations, 02 perspective, 03 observation, 04 light): found-line-control, found-basic-shapes, persp-box-basics, persp-ellipses-cylinders, persp-construct-primitives, persp-horizon-vps, persp-one-point, persp-two-point, persp-slopes, persp-measure-scale, obs-comparative-measuring, obs-quick-location-sketching, light-form-anatomy, light-basic-forms. Two ids are still placeholders because no figure-group file existed when I checked: `fig-anatomy-basics` (human anatomy basics, used by the mammal module) and `fig-gesture` (human gesture drawing, used by the animal gesture module). The merger should map these to the real figure-group ids.
- Overlaps with sibling files for the merger to reconcile: obs-nature-study-journaling and found-sketchbook-habits (overlap with nat-field-sketching-habit), obs-quick-location-sketching (overlap with nat-streets-cities-urban-clutter), obs-still-life-from-life and light-still-life-render (overlap with nat-food-organic-still-life), light-materials-textures (fur, hair, scales overlap with nat-texture-fur-feather-scale), persp-slopes (roofs, used as a prerequisite of nat-architecture-perspective).
- Fabric objects in the everyday-objects module overlap with drapery and clothing in a figure group; the merger may want to cross-link.

- No university or art-school syllabus for botany, zoology, architecture or vehicle drawing was verified; Feng Zhu School and Scott Robertson syllabi are paid or not visible.
