# Perspective, Construction and 3D-Thinking (module group: perspective-construction)

Research date: 13 October 2026. Companion file: `02-perspective-construction.json` (17 modules, ids prefixed `persp-`, 66 resource entries, 89 practice items). All practice items and summaries in the JSON are written in my own words and point to the provider for originals.

Module order used in the JSON: depth cues, box basics, orthographic/isometric bridge (extra), horizon and vanishing points, one-point, two-point, three-point, ellipses and cylinders, construction from primitives, measuring and scale, intuitive perspective, foreshortening, shadows, slopes (split out), curvilinear and fisheye, scene capstone (extra), imagination and visual library.

## Which free structured courses cover this, in what order, and what is free versus paid?

### Takeaway
There is no single free course that covers the whole group. The best free spine is a combination: Drawabox for box, ellipse and construction volume (free, but I could not load the site), a free open textbook and a university web series for the perspective theory (Lemoore College via LibreTexts and the University of Evansville's Studio Chalkboard), and Proko's Perspective Course as a free-tier supplement (32 free lessons out of 146). Everything else I found is either paid, partly paid, or too thinly described to rely on.

## What are the best free practice references and tools?

### Takeaway
The free tools worth recommending are Krita's perspective assistants, Blender, SketchUp Free (non-commercial only), and two grid makers (a browser one and a desktop one). Reference banks that are clearly free to use are the Met's CC0 Open Access collection and Unsplash photos.

### Cited Findings
- Krita describes itself as a free and open source painting program — [Krita about](https://krita.org/en/about/). The manual lists assistants named Vanishing Point, Perspective, 2 Point Perspective, Ruler and Parallel Ruler, Fish Eye Point, Perspective Ellipse, and Curvilinear Perspective (added in 5.3) — [Krita manual](https://docs.krita.org/Painting_With_Assistants).
- Blender is free and open source under the GPL and may be used for any purpose; Blender 5.2 LTS is listed with an initial release on 14 July 2026 and support until July 2028 — [Blender about](https://www.blender.org/about/), [Blender 5.2 LTS](https://www.blender.org/releases/5-2/). The 5.2 manual has pages titled Boolean Modifier, Cameras, Light Objects and Perspective/Orthographic, but my fetch returned only page titles and navigation — [Boolean](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/modeling/modifiers/generate/booleans.html), [Cameras](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/render/cameras.html), [Lights](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/render/lights/light_object.html), [Projections](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/editors/3dview/navigate/projections.html).
- SketchUp Free (help page updated 21 August 2026) includes SketchUp for Web, 10GB of Trimble Connect storage and the 3D Warehouse; it is not for commercial use and has no support; style editing, some Warehouse features and AI credits require a subscription — [SketchUp help](https://help.sketchup.com/en/admin/sketchup-free). The web-features page did not confirm shadows, field of view or orthographic mode — [SketchUp web features](https://help.sketchup.com/en/sketchup-web/web-features).
- imageonline.io's Perspective Grid Maker produces 1-, 2- and 3-point grids and a room mode, is free with no account or watermark, exports PNG up to 8000 px — [imageonline.io](https://imageonline.io/perspective-grid-maker/).
- haztro's Perspective Grid Maker on itch.io supports 1- to 5-point grids, 2D and 3D modes, transparent PNG export, Windows/macOS/Linux, name-your-own-price, made with Godot — [itch.io page](https://haztro.itch.io/perspective-grid-maker).
- The Met's Open Access programme releases public-domain works under CC0, with more than 492,000 works searchable and an API — [The Met Open Access](https://www.metmuseum.org/about-the-met/policies-and-documents/open-access).
- Unsplash licence: free to use, including commercially, no attribution required, but not for building a competing image service — [Unsplash licence](https://unsplash.com/license).
- The Virtual Instructor's cast-shadow lesson (Matt Fussell, with embedded video) teaches a box and sphere shadow construction from a point light — [The Virtual Instructor](https://thevirtualinstructor.com/how-to-draw-paint-cast-shadows.html). Studio Chalkboard's shadow page teaches shadow vanishing points for sun and artificial light — [Studio Chalkboard shadows](https://studiochalkboard.evansville.edu/lp-shadow.html).

"How to Draw Foreshortened Poses" (Steven Michael Hampton, 23:47) is free; "Foreshortening: Figures" (13:36) is premium only — [free lesson](https://proko.com/course-lesson/how-to-draw-foreshortened-poses), [premium lesson](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/foreshortening-figures).
- Oleander Studios defines a visual library as a memory of visual subjects and suggests collecting photos by subject, studying from life, testing recall without reference, and varying viewpoints — [Oleander Studios](https://www.oleanderstudios.com/what-is-a-visual-library/).
- A Journal of Engineering Education paper by Sorby and Baartmans describes a ten-week course (isometric and orthographic sketching with snap cubes, rotation, cross-sections, intersections of solids) after which mental-rotation test scores rose from about 50 percent to about 80 percent; the sample was small and single-institution — [paper copy on a Vanderbilt site](https://www.vanderbilt.edu/gised/wp-content/uploads/Sorby_DevelopmentAssessmentCourse-Enhancing3DSpatialVisualizationSkillsEngineering.pdf).

### Inferences
- Using Blender or SketchUp as a "check my drawing" tool (orbit the 3D model, compare with the sketch you drew from memory) is my own suggested use and is not claimed by any source above. The Sorby study supports the broader idea that spatial visualisation can be trained with isometric sketching and rotation exercises.
- SketchUp Free's non-commercial limit matters if the learner ever wants to use models commercially; Blender has no such limit, so Blender is the safer default for anything that might ship.
- The Sorby paper is hosted on a Vanderbilt site, and I could not confirm the host has publisher permission, so I used it only as a school reference, not a core resource.
- I did not find a free 3D "pose a box" web app that I could verify; Blender covers the need.

### Gaps

- The Blender manual body text was not captured; the claims that Blender offers Union/Difference/Intersect Boolean operations and point and sun lights come from my general knowledge, not from the fetched page.
- Whether SketchUp for Web has shadow and orthographic options is unconfirmed.

## How do atelier, animation-school and architecture-school syllabi sequence perspective?

### Takeaway
Architecture schools tend to teach projection and 3D form first and put perspective late, while drawing courses and ateliers put perspective early and build up from one-point to three-point. The most common drawing-course order is horizon and vanishing points, one-point, two-point, three-point, then shadows and atmosphere.

### Cited Findings
- Texas Tech University ARCH 1101 Architectural Representation I (Fall 2021): points and lines, then 2D figures, then plan, section and elevation (week 7), 3D object modelling (week 9), descriptive geometry with parallel projection (week 10), then perspective (week 11 of 13) — [TTU syllabus](https://www.depts.ttu.edu/architecture/documents/course-syllabi/2021-fall/ARCH1101_ArchitecturalRepresentationI_GaloCanizares_F21.pdf).
- MIT 4.105 Geometric Disciplines + Architecture Skills I (Fall 2024, instructor J. Roc Jih) uses orthographic drawing, Rhino and Grasshopper modelling, and surfaces; perspective is not listed — [MIT syllabus](https://architecture.mit.edu/sites/default/files/course-documents/24fa-4.105-syll-jih.pdf).
- Studio Chalkboard (University of Evansville) orders linear perspective: one-point exteriors and interiors, square, circles, grids and diminishing size, two-point exteriors and interiors, inclines, shadow vanishing points, three-point, atmospheric perspective last — [Studio Chalkboard](https://studiochalkboard.evansville.edu/draw.html).
- Lemoore College's textbook order is illusion of space (overlap, size, placement, atmosphere) before linear perspective, with one-point, interiors, two-point, three-point, then challenges — [LibreTexts chapters 3 and 4](https://human.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Art/Studio_Arts/Drawing_Perspectives_II_(Kennedy)/04%3A_Linear_Perspective).
- Storey (Royal Academy teacher of perspective, 1910) orders: theory, parallel perspective (squares, cubes, pavements, vanishing scale, figures at different heights, uneven ground), angular perspective (measuring points, circles and ellipses, columns, polygons, pyramids, roofs, arches, stairs), then shadow and reflection — [Gutenberg HTML](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/20165/20165-h/20165-h.htm).
- Proko's Perspective Course goes from why perspective is learned and depth tricks, to forms, orthos (no vanishing point) and one point, XYZ, grids, eye level, picture plane, then wide angles and proximity effects — [Proko lessons](https://www.proko.com/course/the-perspective-course/lessons).
- A UNAM workshop outline runs from definitions and light and shadow, through one-, two- and three-point, to fisheye and mixing with organic forms — [UNAM PDF](https://suayed.fad.unam.mx/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Desde-otro-punto-de-vista.pdf).
- Drawabox's course order is lines, ellipses and boxes first, then contour and construction, then plants, insects/animals, everyday objects, vehicles — [Concept Art Empire](https://conceptartempire.com/drawabox-lessons-review/), plus the lesson titles in the earlier section.

### Inferences
- Combining the two traditions suggests inserting an orthographic/isometric bridge module early (I added `persp-parallel-projection`), because architecture syllabi put parallel projection before perspective and the spatial-skills paper trains rotation through isometric sketching.
- Treating perspective as "box first, then horizon and vanishing points" (Drawabox, Proko) and as "theory first" (Storey, Kennedy) are alternative orders; I kept the box module first, then horizon, so beginners draw before they study theory.
- Atelier-style programmes (Watts, Gnomon, Schoolism) were not sourced, and I found no free animation-school syllabus for perspective. Pixar in a Box is the only animation-studio resource I found, and it is camera-focused.

### Gaps
- No atelier syllabus (for example Watts Atelier, Florence Academy, Gage Academy) with a publicly posted perspective sequence was found.
- No animation-school syllabus (CalArts, Gobelins, Sheridan) was found; search budget ran out before I could try.
- No Japanese or French-language resource was verified. The French search returned Skillshare (paid) and unclear university PDFs I did not open.

## Module-by-module coverage and what is thin

### Takeaway
All twelve requested topics are covered, with the one-, two- and three-point topics as separate modules, and slopes split from curvilinear. Five modules lean on resources whose free status or contents I could not confirm, mainly Drawabox-based ones; foreshortening and drawing-from-imagination have the thinnest free coverage.

### Cited Findings
- Depth cues: LibreTexts Chapter 3 section 3.6 covers overlap, size, placement and atmospheric effects and adds diminishing detail — [Chapter 3](https://human.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Art/Studio_Arts/Drawing_Perspectives_II_(Kennedy)/03%3A_Creating_the_Illusion_of_Space).
- Atmospheric perspective: lighter values, cooler and less vivid colour, reduced contrast and detail with distance — [Chapter 5](https://human.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Art/Studio_Arts/Drawing_Perspectives_II_(Kennedy)/05%3A_Atmospheric_Perspective).
- Shadows: the shadow vanishing point is found by dropping a vertical from the light to the receiving surface; for the sun it lies on the horizon directly below; different surfaces get different shadow points — [Studio Chalkboard](https://studiochalkboard.evansville.edu/lp-shadow.html). The Virtual Instructor adds that a light closer to the horizon makes longer shadows — [Virtual Instructor](https://thevirtualinstructor.com/how-to-draw-paint-cast-shadows.html).
- Curvilinear: five-point perspective uses four vanishing points on a circle and one at the centre, codified by Barre and Flocon in 1968; early approximations appear in van Eyck's Arnolfini Portrait (1434) and Parmigianino's convex-mirror self-portrait — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curvilinear_perspective).
- Orthographic/isometric: in axonometric projection size does not change with distance, and isometric uses equal foreshortening on all three axes at 120 degrees — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axonometric_projection).
- Slopes: Studio Chalkboard lesson 10 covers "Inclines: finding roof pitches and declines"; Storey Book II covers uneven ground and descending planes and Book III covers roofs and stairs — [Studio Chalkboard](https://studiochalkboard.evansville.edu/draw.html), [Gutenberg HTML](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/20165/20165-h/20165-h.htm).
- Measuring and scale: Studio Chalkboard lessons 6 and 7 (grids and tile floors, diminishing size); Storey Book II on the vanishing scale and figures at different heights — as cited above.

### Inferences
- The "inclined vanishing point on a vertical line through the flat-ground point" rule in the slopes module summary is standard perspective geometry I wrote from general knowledge; the cited sources confirm the lesson topic but my fetches did not capture the lesson text.
- The estimated hours (4 to 100 per module) are my own planning estimates, not provider figures. The intuitive-perspective and imagination modules are deliberately open-ended.

### Gaps
- Foreshortening of objects (not figures): no free structured lesson verified. The core entry is a free Proko figure lesson.
- Imagination module core is a Proko free-tier set; the strongest dedicated resource, Scott Robertson's book, is paid.
- Marco Bucci, Peter Han, Alphonso Dunn and other well-known YouTube teachers were not verified because the search budget ran out; add them in a later pass if wanted.
- Free fisheye or five-point video lesson: only Proko's Practical Perspective lesson (37:16) was found, free status unknown.
