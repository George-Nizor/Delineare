# Design, composition, characters, creatures, storytelling and imagination: free resources for a self-directed drawing curriculum

Research date: 13 October 2026. Companion JSON: `07-design-composition-characters.json` (16 modules, 62 resource entries, 79 practice items). The web search quota ran out partway through, so most checks after the first round were done by opening URLs directly. Sibling files 01, 02, 04, 06 and 09 were present in the folder when I finished, so I used their module ids for a few cross-group prerequisites (listed at the end).

## Which free structured courses cover composition, design, characters, creatures and storytelling, and what is free versus paid?

### Takeaway
The only provider I could confirm that is genuinely free and structured across this whole group is Ctrl+Paint (Matt Kohr), whose composition, thumbnail, reference, costume and idea-generation videos form a usable free spine. Almost every big-name teacher in the brief (Will Terry, Nathan Fowkes, Aaron Blaise, Marco Bucci's courses, Terryl Whitlatch, Gnomon, Proko's Marvel course) sells the structured version; what is free is mostly their YouTube channels, blogs, or a small set of sample lessons.

### Cited Findings

**Ctrl+Paint (free, best fit)**
- The library page calls its videos "short, free" and says a new free video appears every Tuesday and Thursday; a separate Premium Series ($10 videos, e.g. Playbook 16 Machine Design) is sold in the store — [Ctrl+Paint library](https://www.ctrlpaint.com/library)
- The library has a Composition Basics section (Principles of Design series, visual simplification, tangents, framing elements, shadow shape, critique), a Using Reference Materials section (What is Design?, Designing with reference, Costume Sketching, film studies of foreground/middle/background), thumbnail videos (Modular Thumbnail Sketches, Thumbnail Iteration, Chop and Warp, Imaginary Landscape Thumbnails) and a Study Habits section (Generating Ideas, What Should I Draw Today?, Give Yourself Assignments) — [Ctrl+Paint library](https://www.ctrlpaint.com/library)
- Film Studies: Foreground, Middleground, Background reduces movie stills to three graphic shapes (posted May 2012) — [Ctrl+Paint](https://www.ctrlpaint.com/videos/film-studies-foreground-middleground-background)
- Modular Thumbnail Sketches builds reusable parts (demo subject: robots) and recombines them to make many designs fast — [Ctrl+Paint](https://www.ctrlpaint.com/videos/modular-thumbnail-sketching)
- Thumbnail Iteration refines one character-silhouette thumbnail with masks instead of redrawing, crediting the book The Skillful Huntsman — [Ctrl+Paint](https://www.ctrlpaint.com/videos/thumbnail-iteration)
- Generating Ideas (Sept 2012) suggests narrow self-made assignments and links donjon and Chaotic Shiny generators — [Ctrl+Paint](https://www.ctrlpaint.com/videos/generating-ideas)
- Designing with Reference (Aug 2012) dresses mannequin drawings as real professions to build a visual library; Costume Sketching (Mar 2012) uses fashion photos from The Sartorialist — [Designing with Reference](https://www.ctrlpaint.com/videos/designing-with-reference), [Costume Sketching](https://www.ctrlpaint.com/videos/costume-sketching)
- Expressiveness in Your Drawing (Nov 2012) argues character drawing needs body language and facial expression, and points to animator Glen Keane — [Ctrl+Paint](https://www.ctrlpaint.com/videos/expressiveness-in-drawing)
- Caveat: individual lesson pages do not repeat the word "free"; the free claim rests on the library page. No Ctrl+Paint dedicated section for creatures, storytelling or shape language exists — [Ctrl+Paint library](https://www.ctrlpaint.com/library)

## Which free ideation and practice resources exist (prompt lists, challenges, generators, reference banks), and what are their licences?

### Takeaway
Inktober's official 2026 prompt list is free and optional, but the prompt words are in an image I could not read, so the learner must open the page. Random generators (donjon, Chaotic Shiny, Seventh Sanctum) are free to use but none states ownership terms for their output, so results should stay private prompts. Museum open-access banks (Met, Cleveland: CC0; Smithsonian: CC0 subset) are the safest free reference.

### Cited Findings
- The SVSLearn article publishes the official Inktober 2026 list, says it is optional, credits Jake Parker as founder, and lists the rules: ink drawing, share it, tag #inktober and #inktober2026 — [SVSLearn](https://www.svslearn.com/news/2026/9/1/the-official-inktober-2026-prompt-list)
- The official site's rules page says to make an ink drawing, post and hashtag it; official prompt lists have existed since 2016; the Inktober name for selling products has separate trademark guidelines — [Inktober rules](https://inktober.com/rules)
- Aaron Blaise's site hosts an Unofficial Inktober Prompt List 2026 (posted 1 Oct 2026 by Nick Burch); prompts sit in a header image; the footer says content is copyright Aaron Blaise; earlier years exist — [Unofficial list](https://creatureartteacher.com/ink-prompts-2026/)
- donjon is a collection of random generators for fantasy, sci-fi and horror (NPCs, hybrid animals, monsters, towns, inns, spaceships); the home page shows donation links, "code Copyright 2009-2026 drow" and some Open Game License content, and no output terms — [donjon](https://donjon.bin.sh/)
- Chaotic Shiny (running since 2010) has generators for characters (Fantasy Character, Class Mashup), creatures (Monsters, Zoomorph animal-human mixes, Familiar) and places (Tavern, Arena, Map, Planet); many are free and some are sold as packs; no terms-of-use section — [Chaotic Shiny](https://www.chaoticshiny.com/)
- Seventh Sanctum (Steve Savage, 1999) has Beings, Characters, Settings and Places, and Darkness/Evil generators; no longer updated; code free to use with credit; no guarantees — [Seventh Sanctum](https://www.seventhsanctum.com/)
- Surrealist chance methods are documented free by Tate (exquisite corpse invented in Paris in 1925; automatism; frottage developed by Max Ernst from 1925), Centre Pompidou's teacher glossary (ten techniques including decalcomania, fumage, grattage, collage), a LACMA exhibition wall-text PDF, and National Galleries of Scotland — [Tate exquisite corpse](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/c/cadavre-exquis-exquisite-corpse), [Tate automatism](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/a/automatism), [Tate frottage](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/f/frottage), [Centre Pompidou](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/en/offre-aux-professionnels/enseignants/dossiers-ressources-sur-lart/le-surrealisme/glossaire-des-techniques-surrealistes), [LACMA PDF](https://www.lacma.org/sites/default/files/Drawing%20Surrealism%20didactics%2010.22.12.pdf), [NGS frottage](https://www.nationalgalleries.org/art-and-artists/glossary-terms/frottage)
- The Met Open Access: images of public-domain works and data on all accessioned works are CC0; more than 492,000 works; API and CSV on GitHub — [The Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/about-the-met/policies-and-documents/open-access)
- Cleveland Museum of Art: CC0 high-res images for more than half the collection; free API with no key; 61,000+ CC0 records — [Cleveland](https://www.clevelandart.org/open-access)
- Smithsonian Open Access: 5.2 million+ items; reuse without asking; CC0 reached via a CC0-filtered search, so check each record — [Smithsonian](https://www.si.edu/openaccess)
- Art Institute of Chicago: its page says it provides open access to images, data and source code but does not state the licence on that page — [AIC](https://www.artic.edu/open-access)
- Biodiversity Heritage Library: free access to biodiversity literature; licences are not stated on its home page — [BHL](https://www.biodiversitylibrary.org/)
- Unsplash licence: free for commercial and non-commercial use, no credit required, cannot be compiled into a competing service — [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com/license)
- Line of Action has reference tools for figures, animals, faces and expressions, hands, and scenes/environments plus forums, but the page does not say which tools are free; it also sells subscriptions — [Line of Action](https://line-of-action.com/)
- Digital Comic Museum offers public-domain Golden Age comics free with registration, excludes post-1959 books, and notes that Warner Bros. flagged some Fawcett titles as not public domain — [Digital Comic Museum](https://digitalcomicmuseum.com/)
- Pepper&Carrot is a free(libre) webcomic with Making-of, Resources and Wiki sections; the home page does not name its licence and a blog post mentions a licence update prohibiting AI derivation — [Pepper&Carrot](https://www.peppercarrot.com/)
- Every Frame a Painting is a YouTube channel of video essays on film form (2014 to 2016, with a limited return in late 2024) — [Channel](https://www.youtube.com/@everyframeapainting)
- PureRef is a reference board app for Windows, Mac and Linux; the pages I opened did not state pricing — [PureRef](https://www.pureref.com/)

### Inferences
- Because no generator states terms for generated text, using results as private starting prompts is the safe reading. Any published work should rest on your own invention.
- Inktober should be framed as optional prompts rather than a streak, which matches the SVSLearn article and the user's earlier difficulty with streaks.
- For someone in Australia, "public domain" on a US-hosted comic site may not equal public domain locally; I recommend private study use only. This is my own caution, not a finding from a source.

### Gaps
- The actual 31 official Inktober 2026 prompts: they sit in an image on the page and I cannot read images.
- Character Design Challenge (characterdesignchallenge.com): my fetch was blocked by robots rules, so I did not include it. I could not check its current schedule or licence.
- Other 30-day lists (Drawtober, Mermay and similar) and the Reddit and Discord critique communities: no verifiable pages.
- Rijksmuseum Rijksstudio: the page I opened did not describe it, so I left it out. Sketch Daily and Quickposes pages returned only a title or nothing and are not listed in the JSON.

- Trove (National Library of Australia): a bot-check page blocked me; I included it in one module as unverified.

## How do concept-art, animation and illustration school curricula sequence these topics, and what do students produce?

### Takeaway
The programmes I could open start with drawing, colour/design and storytelling basics, then split into specialisms (concept/visual development versus illustration versus animation), and end in a portfolio or a team major project. Character and environment design are introduced in first or second year; acting for animation comes after advanced character design.

### Cited Findings
- ArtCenter Entertainment Design (BS) has three tracks. The Concept Track covers drawing, rendering, model-building, sculpting and 3D digital tools, with topics such as animal anatomy, costume design, light and colour, digital painting, visual structure and world building; the page names no individual courses; graduates may become storyboarders, concept artists or production designers; the portfolio is presented as the main asset — [ArtCenter Entertainment Design](https://www.artcenter.edu/academics/undergraduate-degrees/entertainment-design/overview.html)
- ArtCenter Illustration (BS) lists an Entertainment Arts track (character design, storyboarding, action development, backgrounds), fine-art courses on composition and conceptual processes, and outputs including character designs, storyboards and backgrounds shown at a Grad Show — [ArtCenter Illustration](https://www.artcenter.edu/academics/undergraduate-degrees/illustration/overview.html)
- Ringling Illustration (BFA) begins with visual storytelling, colour theory, composition and anatomy; in third year students choose Illustration or Visual Development (concept art; 2D props, characters, environments; art test; high volume, quick turnarounds); outputs include a portfolio, a graphic novel (Meanwhile) and a visual development book (Design Unscripted) — [Ringling Illustration](https://www.ringling.edu/academics/majors/illustration/)
- Ringling 2D Character Animation (BFA) combines traditional draftsmanship with production tools; students pitch TV series, features and web series; the gallery shows character design sheets, environment design, expressions, poses and props; the page names no courses — [Ringling 2D](https://www.ringling.edu/academics/majors/2d-character-animation/)
- CalArts Character Animation (BFA) is four years: year 1 life drawing, colour and design, storytelling and character animation; year 2 adds dialogue and sound; year 3 advanced work; year 4 customised; one short film per year and a graduation review — [CalArts](https://calarts.edu/academics/programs-and-degrees/bfa-character-animation)
- Swinburne Bachelor of Animation (Melbourne, 3 years): year 1 semester 2 Introduction to Character and Environment Design and Action Analysis and Locomotion; year 2 Advanced Character Design for Animation, Writing and Directing for Animation, then Acting for Animation; year 3 a two-semester team Major Project aimed at festivals or public exhibition — [Swinburne](https://www.swinburne.edu.au/course/undergraduate/bachelor-of-animation)
- FZD School of Design (Singapore, one year): term 1 fundamentals (perspective, lighting, anatomy, materials, forms); term 2 expanding the visual library (history, culture, nature, generating ideas from reference); term 3 a story-driven portfolio — [FZD](https://www.fzdschool.com/)
- Gnomon School's home page lists a 2-year Certificate in Digital Production for Entertainment, a 4-year BFA and 10-week standalone courses, but no concept-design curriculum detail — [Gnomon](https://www.gnomon.edu/)

### Inferences
- The shared sequence is: drawing and design fundamentals, then idea generation from reference, then character/environment design, then performance and storytelling, then a portfolio or major project. My module order follows it (ideation, composition, thumbnails, reference, shape language, depth, environment, character, costume, creature, expression, storytelling, stylisation, surreal ideation, visual development).
- Composition appears as a first-year topic at Ringling and ArtCenter, which supports putting it before character work.
- The Swinburne order (character design, then acting) supports teaching expression after shape language and character design.

### Gaps
- ArtCenter, Ringling and Swinburne course-by-course pages (the course-of-study pages failed to load), so I could not give named courses with term numbers for ArtCenter or Ringling.
- Gobelins, Sheridan, RMIT, VFS and CalArts catalogues: pages did not load or did not describe the programmes.
- Course descriptions rarely say what students produce at week level; only outcomes at programme level are known.
