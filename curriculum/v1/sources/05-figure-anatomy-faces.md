# Figure, anatomy, faces, hands/feet and clothing: free learning resources (module group 05, researched 2026-10-13)

Scope: gesture, figure construction, proportion, anatomy, hands, feet, head, features and expressions, portraits, hair, drapery, foreshortening, dynamic poses, drawing from imagination. The matching module file is `05-figure-anatomy-faces.json` (17 modules, ids prefixed `fig-`; 15 requested plus two added: `fig-master-studies` and `fig-life-drawing-session-routine`).

Method note: my shared web-search budget ran out partway through, so everything below comes from pages I opened with WebFetch plus the early search results. Anything I could not open is listed under Gaps. Prices are as displayed on 2026-10-13 and change.

## Which free structured courses cover this group, and what exactly is free versus paid?

### Takeaway
Nobody offers a complete free figure course. The most usable free material is a set of free lessons inside Proko's paid courses (gesture, Loomis head method, hair, 115 anatomy lessons, Hampton's drapery start, hands and feet), the Friday live sessions and previews at New Masters Academy (NMA), and public-domain books. Hampton's Gumroad listing states no free content and Vilppu Academy's page lists no free videos; Hampton's free lessons are the ones hosted on Proko.

## What are the best free practice tools and reference banks, and what exactly is free?

### Takeaway
Line of Action, Quickposes, Posemaniacs and Proko Timer cover timed poses, hands, faces and 3D references at no cost for at least part of their content; Magic Poser's free plan is limited to one model per scene. Z-Anatomy and BodyParts3D are free 3D anatomy viewers. Nude references are common in photo tools, so check each.

### Cited Findings
- Line of Action (line-of-action.com): free-menu tools for Figure Drawing, Animals, Hands and Feet, Faces and Expression, Basic Shapes (test), Scenes, and 360 degree views; store has subscriptions, pay-as-you-go, photo bundles; free-tier limits not shown — [home](https://line-of-action.com)
- Line of Action FAQ: memberships called Full and Doodler; images licensed for educational use inside the tools; you may post your drawings but not sell them — [FAQ](https://line-of-action.com/faq)
- Line of Action also has a roughly 15-minute free gesture tutorial (line of action, three movable masses, limb placement) — [learn to draw](https://www.line-of-action.com/learn-to-draw)
- A Proko forum post describes Line of Action's options as nude or clothed, male or female, child/teen/adult, class mode; Sketch Daily with nudity, gender, class mode, stationary or action poses, hands/feet, portraits, animals, plants, own-photo upload; Quickposes as a favourite — [Proko community](https://www.proko.com/community/topics/online-tools-for-practice)
- Quickposes: free image library and gesture tool; timed practice, random gestures, 20-image Challenges; optional payments of EUR 3 to 15 including a desktop app; account optional (offered); nudity not described — [Quickposes](https://quickposes.com)
- Proko Timer: free sample packs (300 Best of 2025 = 300 images; Classical Art Poses sample = 439; Grafit Reference Sampler = 81); paid packs $5-$229; 1-999 images per session; some packs tagged mature or NUDE; NoAI robots tag; terms not shown — [Proko Timer](https://www.proko.com/timer)
- Posemaniacs: royalty-free 3D pose reference; sections for 3D figure, muscle references, hand poses, face references, tutorials; sign-up for bookmarks; pricing and nudity policy not stated; terms page not read — [Posemaniacs](https://www.posemaniacs.com)
- Magic Poser: free plan $0 (body/hand/foot posing, hair and clothing, 2D export; 1 model per scene, 1 light, 1 character, 50 poses, 10 props, 4 hair/clothing options; no body morphs or 3D export); Pro $9.99 one-time; Master $14.99 per month — [Magic Poser pricing](https://magicposer.com/pricing-2.html)
- Sketch Daily home page loads only a title in a fetch (JavaScript app); free versus paid not confirmed — [SketchDaily](https://www.sketchdaily.net)
- Z-Anatomy: Blender template and navigable atlas, CC BY-SA 4.0, derived from BodyParts3D, male model, Terminologia Anatomica labels; a 2022 article says the Blender file and an Android app are free; the web address loads an in-browser viewer — [GitHub](https://github.com/Z-Anatomy/Models-of-human-anatomy), [CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/2022/05/check-out-amazing-free-3d-anatomy-reference-z-anatomy/2/), [z-anatomy.com](https://www.z-anatomy.com)

- Anatomy Atlases (Michael P. D'Alessandro and Ronald A. Bergman): Atlas of Human Anatomy, Cross Section, Illustrated Encyclopedia of Human Anatomic Variation and more; CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; no ads; medical images — [Anatomy Atlases](https://www.anatomyatlases.org)
- TeachMeAnatomy: free version exists, Pro subscription adds quizzes and 3D models; sections for head, neck, thorax, upper limb, lower limb — [TeachMeAnatomy](https://www.teachmeanatomy.info)
- OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e: free to read online or download — [OpenStax](https://openstax.org/details/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e)
- MakeHuman is a figure-creation tool (1.2.0 alpha page); its free status and export licence were not stated on the pages I read — [MakeHuman](https://www.makehumancommunity.org), [GitHub](https://github.com/makehumancommunity/makehuman)

## Which public-domain and open-access books and image banks have legitimate free copies?

### Takeaway
DrawingBooks.org, Archive.org and Project Gutenberg host public-domain drawing, anatomy and expression books; the Met, Rijksmuseum and National Gallery of Art give free high-resolution historic drawings and photographs. Loomis's books are not safe to link: a teacher's page says the family renewed the copyrights.

### Cited Findings
- DrawingBooks.org lists 23 public-domain books, including Bridgman's Constructive Anatomy and Book of a Hundred Hands, Vanderpoel's The Human Figure, Hatton's Figure Drawing, Henry Warren's Artistic Anatomy, Lutz's Practical Art Anatomy, Rimmer's Drawing Children, Burns and Colenso's Living Anatomy; it states they are public domain in the US. The reader is JPEG pages and it was launched by the manager of the former Saveloomis.org — [DrawingBooks.org](https://www.drawingbooks.org/), [3DVF article](https://3dvf.com/actualite-2684-drawingbooks-ressources-domaine-public-dessin-html/)
- Bridgman's Constructive Anatomy (1920): contents include General Anatomy, Masses and Movements of the Body, Hand, Arm, Neck, Head, Eye, Nose, Ear, Mouth, Trunk, Pelvis, Lower limbs, Foot; reader runs about 220 pages — [DrawingBooks reader](https://www.drawingbooks.org/bridgman1/index.html), [Archive.org](https://archive.org/details/constructiveanat00brid)
- Bridgman's Book of a Hundred Hands: reader to about page 175, bones of the wrist, fingers, thumb, muscles — [reader](https://www.drawingbooks.org/bridgman2/index.html)
- Vanderpoel's The Human Figure: 17 chapters from eyes and head to The Figure as a Whole, about 168 pages — [reader](https://www.drawingbooks.org/vanderpoel1/index.html)
- Hatton's Figure Drawing chapters: Method and Proportion, The Head and Neck, The Trunk, The Upper Limb, The Lower Limb, Drapery — [reader](https://www.drawingbooks.org/fdhatton/index.html)

- Loomis: a teacher's page first said his books were out of copyright, then corrected that the family renewed them and removed the links; I found no legitimate free copy — [Scott Eaton page](https://www.scott-eaton.com/?p=49)
- Gray's Anatomy 1918: Archive.org offers free download; no rights statement on the item — [Archive.org](https://archive.org/details/anatomyofhumanbo1918gray)

- National Gallery of Art: Muybridge's Plate 7, Walking (1887) is free and in the public domain with a download link — [NGA](https://www.nga.gov/artworks/166511-plate-number-7-walking)
- Stanford's copy of Muybridge's The Human Figure in Motion (1901) is open for non-commercial use with attribution but is missing many pages; Wellcome Collection's copy is in closed stores — [Stanford](https://purl.stanford.edu/jf437nt9771), [Wellcome](https://wellcomecollection.org/works/aytcfdp8)
- NLM Historical Anatomies on the Web: rights listed as Public Domain; titles not checked — [NLM](https://www.nlm.nih.gov/exhibition/historicalanatomies/home.html)

### Inferences
- Pre-1931 US publications (Bridgman 1920, Gray 1918, Darwin 1872) are the safest free books. The DrawingBooks.org public-domain claim is the site's own, so treat later titles with care.
- I left Stanchfield out of the JSON because I could not confirm the notes are hosted with permission; the published Drawn to Life books are the legitimate route.

## How do art-school life-drawing syllabi sequence gesture, structure and anatomy?

### Takeaway
Three syllabi (UF, Redwoods, UVU) start with gesture and contour in weeks 1-2; the FLCC syllabus lists topics without weeks. Anatomy is taught in short blocks or weekly lectures alongside drawing, not as a separate first phase. Hands and feet come early to mid-term, heads and features mid-term, and long poses last. The animation-minded UVU syllabus adds stylisation near the end.

### Cited Findings
- UF ART2330C Figure Drawing I, Spring 2026: Week 1 blind contour; Week 2 gesture, line quality, contour, proportion and a hands-and-feet homework; Week 3 short poses and musculature; Weeks 4-5 skeleton, sight measuring, foreshortening, eyes/ears/features; Weeks 6-7 volume and cross-hatching; Weeks 8-9 light, value, motion; Week 10 manipulating the figure; Weeks 11-14 long poses including a double-model pose; three self-portraits — [syllabus](https://digitalworlds.ufl.edu/syllabi/2026/Spring/2026-Spring-Art%202330%20-%20Figure%20Drawing%201-10497.pdf)
- College of the Redwoods ART 19, Fall 2021: week 1 gesture, contour, cross-contour; weeks 2-4 media and value; week 5 foreshortening; week 6 contrapposto and head studies; week 7 hands and feet; weeks 9-10 anatomy (skeleton, musculature); week 11 costume study — [syllabus](https://www.redwoods.edu/fs/syllabi/art/2021F-ART19-E1803-Hooper.pdf)
- UVU ART 4700R Figure Drawing, Spring 2025: week 1 gesture and motion; week 2 anatomy essentials and proportion; week 3 dynamic movement; weeks 5-8 character, idealization, elongation, stylization, abstraction; week 9 hands, feet, heads; week 10 light; week 11 textures; week 13 movement and animation; week 14 live model — [syllabus](https://uvu.edu/syllabi-repository/docs/art-470r-001-202520.pdf)
- ART 200 Figure Drawing I (probably Finger Lakes Community College): proportional systems, weekly skeletal and muscular lectures, drawings from the skeleton and master copies, 30-second gestures, 5-20 minute short poses, 2-6 hour long poses, portfolio reviews — [syllabus](https://cyber.flcc.edu/pdf/syllabi/ART200.pdf)
- NMA's studio-school order for Constructive Figure Drawing I: gesture and structure, form and position, head and torso, shoulders and limbs, proportions and whole figure, light and shadow, rendering — [NMA](https://www.nma.art/courses/constructive-figure-drawing-i/)

### Inferences
- A sensible home order: gesture -> construction -> proportion -> anatomy by region in short blocks -> hands/feet -> head -> foreshortening -> long poses -> stylisation/imagination. This is the order I used for module prerequisites.
- UF puts hands and feet in week 2 as homework, which suggests treating hands and feet as ongoing practice, not a late module.

### Gaps
- UF and FLCC names are inferred from domains; the PDFs do not name the institution fully.
- No university open-courseware figure course (MIT, Open University) was found; the search budget ran out before I could look.
