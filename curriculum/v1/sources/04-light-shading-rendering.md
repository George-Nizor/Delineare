# Light, shading, values, edges, texture and rendering: free resources for a self-directed drawing curriculum

Research date: 13 October 2026. The structured data for this group is in `04-light-shading-rendering.json` (14 modules, ids prefixed `light-`). This file records what I found, what is free versus paid, and what I could not verify.

Module order I recommend: light-values-squint, light-form-anatomy, light-basic-forms, then in parallel light-media, light-techniques and light-edges; then light-value-composition, light-direction-types, light-cast-shadows-ao, light-materials-textures, light-line-weight-inking, light-bargue-cast (optional atelier detour), light-invented-light and light-still-life-render as the capstone. The 12 modules you listed are all present. I added two: `light-bargue-cast` (atelier-style Bargue and cast training) and `light-invented-light` (shading invented subjects, which matters for surreal and invented work). Prerequisites in the JSON only point to modules inside this group. When the files are merged, add cross-group prerequisites for line control and basic form construction on the first modules (`light-values-squint`, `light-basic-forms`) and perspective of boxes before `light-cast-shadows-ao`.

## Which free structured courses cover this group?

### Takeaway
No single free course covers the whole group. The best free spine is a patchwork: Proko's free lessons (Practical Guide to Realistic Shading, Shading Light and Form, Intro to Edges), Ctrl+Paint's Free Video Library, Pixar's lighting course on Khan Academy, and a handful of well-regarded individual teachers. The deepest structured value course (Dorian Iten's Shading Course) is mostly paid.

### Cited Findings
- Proko's Shading Course is taught by Dorian Iten (former program director at Barcelona Academy of Art) and has 68 lessons in 10 modules, from "How to Separate Light & Shadow" to "Bringing it all Together"; it costs $250 — [Proko, The Shading Course](https://www.proko.com/course/the-shading-course)
- "Practical Guide to Realistic Shading" (15:44, Stan Prokopenko) is free and walks one sphere from layin to final adjustments, naming terminator, core shadow, reflected light, halftones, occlusion, umbra and penumbra — [Proko](https://proko.com/880)
- "Shading Light and Form - Basics" (7:16) is free, lesson 43 of 57 in Figure Drawing Fundamentals; it covers zones, terminator, form versus cast shadow, reflected light, halftone and highlight, and sets an egg-shading assignment — [Proko](https://www.proko.com/lesson/shading-light-and-form-basics)
- Proko's Drawing Basics has sections called Values and Edges. "Intro to Edges" (11:01) is free, and so are the projects "Shadow Mapping" and "Lost and Found Edges" — [Proko, Intro to Edges](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/intro-to-edges)
- Ctrl+Paint (Matt Kohr) lists free videos including "Form, Value, and Light", "Sketching with Values", "Hard and Soft Shadow Edges", "Lost Edges", "Shadow Properties", "Shadow Shape", "Material Spheres", "Studying Glossiness" pts 1 to 3 and the "Basic Rendering" series — [Ctrl+Paint library](https://www.ctrlpaint.com/library)
- The Ctrl+Paint videos I opened (Hard and Soft Shadow Edges, Lost Edges, Shadow Properties, Sketching: Value, Shadow Shapes, Form Value and Light, Material Spheres, Studying Glossiness) show no paywall on the page — [example: Lost Edges](https://www.ctrlpaint.com/videos/lost-edges)
- Marco Bucci's free YouTube videos include "Essential Values for Painting, Lighting and Design", "Learn to see like a painter ... with edges!" and "The Shading Lesson That Made My Art Career Possible" (channel confirmed via YouTube oEmbed data; I could not open the watch pages) — [Essential Values](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BTYGWfiZnMA); [Edges](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nnhj5efzN_w); [Shading lesson](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DQ5QF0q4QqQ)
- Pixar in a Box, made with Khan Academy, is described as a free online curriculum and includes "The Art of Lighting" as one of fifteen topics. Search results list a "Light roles" video and three activities (seeing light and color; lighting an orange physically; lighting an orange virtually) — [Computer Graphics World](https://www.cgw.com/Press-Center/Covid-19/2020/Pixar-in-a-Box-Helps-Animators-Sharpen-Their-Ski.aspx); [Light roles](https://www.khanacademy.org/computing/pixar/art-of-lighting/introduction-to-virtual-lighting/v/lighting-roles)
- The Drawing Source (Marina Fridman) has a free article on seven elements of light and shadow, with a free reference sheet — [The Drawing Source](https://www.thedrawingsource.com/light-and-form.html)
- Notan and value planning: Will Kemp's free article and Dan Scott's (Draw Paint Academy, Australia) free guide with a free notan tool — [Will Kemp](https://willkempartschool.com/how-to-use-notan-design-to-create-compelling-compositions-in-your-paintings/); [Draw Paint Academy](https://drawpaintacademy.com/notan/)
- Pen and ink: Alphonso Dunn has "hundreds of free instructional videos" on YouTube and a paid shading course; Lizzie Harper (scientific illustrator) has a free article covering line pressure and stippling — [Dunn's courses page](https://courses.alphonsodunn.com/); [Lizzie Harper](https://lizzieharper.co.uk/2013/04/scientific-illustration-pen-and-ink-techniques/)
- Will Kemp Art School's own site lists a paid Light and Shadow Drawing Course (charcoal, chalk, chiaroscuro, toned paper) and says its free videos page covers acrylic painting, not drawing — [Will Kemp, course page](https://willkempartschool.com/product/light-shadow-drawing-course/)
- Circle Line Art School has hundreds of free YouTube lessons, but its page describes them as focused on perspective, buildings and structured observation; shading appears only as an image caption in the paid "How to Draw from Scratch" course — [Circle Line Art School](https://www.circlelineartschool.com/)

### Inferences
- Because the free Proko lessons are tied to paid courses, a learner can follow the same teacher for vocabulary and sphere shading, then switch to Ctrl+Paint and YouTube for edges, materials and composition without paying.
- Pixar's lighting course suits character, scene and invented work better than still-life drills, since it teaches light roles in a scene.
- Scientific illustration (Lizzie Harper) and Australian landscape painting (Dan Scott) add variety beyond the usual drawing channels.

### Gaps
- Drawabox: its pages returned a loading interstitial to my fetch tool. Search results confirmed "Lesson 2: Contour Lines, Texture and Construction" and a "Dissections" exercise, but I could not confirm any form-shading or cast-shadow sections. I did not invent a lesson list. It is included as an unverified supplementary resource in `light-materials-textures`.
- OpenLearn: the main site disallowed my fetch tool and search results showed no drawing-tone course (only physics and teacher training). No OpenLearn drawing module is included.
- Non-English sources: my Spanish and French searches returned only Skillshare, Platzi and Elephorm listings (mostly paid), so no non-English free source is included. A follow-up search in Japanese, Korean, Russian or German would likely turn something up.
- Circle Line Art School: I found no free shading lesson I could confirm; it is not in the JSON.
- Gurney Journey (James Gurney) was not used: my search budget ran out before I could find specific post URLs.
- The search tool's per-turn budget ran out, so I could not follow up on Drawabox, atelier cast-drawing videos or free charcoal tutorials.

## What free practice material exists?

### Takeaway
The best free practice material is physical and cheap (a lamp, an egg, an orange, white mugs), plus Blender for 3D light tests, the Met's CC0 collection for master references, and a few downloadable tools.

### Cited Findings
- Pixar in a Box has a physical lighting activity ("Activity 2: Lighting an orange") and a virtual one ("Activity 3") — [Khan Academy, Activity 2](https://www.khanacademy.org/computing/pixar/art-of-lighting/introduction-to-virtual-lighting/a/exercise-2-light-roles); [Activity 3](https://en.khanacademy.org/computing/pixar/art-of-lighting/introduction-to-virtual-lighting/a/exercise-3-lighting-an-orange). ## How do atelier and art-school courses sequence value study?

### Takeaway
Atelier training goes from copying toward observation: Bargue plates in pencil, then in charcoal, then plaster casts in charcoal, then portraiture and figure from life. Online value courses follow a similar logic in a shorter form: separate light and shadow, learn the effects of light, control and organise values, then render, then invent.

### Cited Findings
- Florence Academy of Art's Foundation in Graphic Arts has students do one pencil Bargue copy (normally 10 x 14 inches) and one charcoal copy (normally 18 x 20 inches) over many weeks, then two charcoal casts (feature, then mask or head), then portraits in the third trimester — [FAA curriculum PDF](https://florenceacademyofart.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Foundation-in-Graphic-Arts-Curriculum-2024-25.pdf). The document does not mention grisaille.
- Its 6-week elective runs Bargue and Old Master copies with sight-size, then casts, portraits and still life in charcoal and oil, then figure from the model in three-hour sessions — [FAA 6-week elective](https://www.florenceacademyofart.edu/?p=33788)
- Charles Bargue (1825 to 1883) devised the Drawing Course with help from Jean-Leon Gerome; Goupil & Cie published 197 lithographs in three parts (casts, master drawings, the living model). Sources disagree on dates (infobox: 1866; text: probably 1868 to 1873) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Bargue)
- Proko's Shading Course module order: separate light and shadow, see light effects, control values, organise values, create realistic shading, light probes, show form, invent shading, maquettes, bring it all together — [Proko](https://www.proko.com/course/the-shading-course)
- Proko's Drawing Basics runs Lines, Shapes, Perspective, Values, then Edges, then bonus content — [Proko](https://www.proko.com/course/drawing-basics)
- Princeton's art-making session goes from the value scale to a shaded circle becoming a sphere to a still life — [Princeton University Art Museum](https://artmuseum.princeton.edu/art/stories-perspectives/video/art-making-l-drawing-light-values-and-shading)
- Dorian Iten's "Inventing Light" assignments (egg still life, light guide, relighting study) place invented light after observation — [Proko](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/inventing-light)

### Inferences
- The shared pattern is: tone scale, then simple forms, then edges, then complex objects, then invented subjects. The JSON module order follows it.
- Grisaille (monochrome underpainting) did not appear in any source I could open, so no grisaille module is included. The nearest free stand-in is a graphite or charcoal value study from a Met open-access painting.
- Bargue copying is slow and optional. I marked it intermediate with a wide hour range (15 to 60) so George can decide whether it serves his goal of drawing invented subjects.

### Gaps
- No free video of sight-size cast drawing was verified.
- I did not see a durable source for Grand Central Atelier, Watts Atelier or Angel Academy sequences, so only the Florence Academy is cited.
