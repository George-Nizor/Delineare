# Colour, traditional media, digital tools and finishing: free resources for a self-directed drawing curriculum (research date 2026-10-13)

Scope: 18 modules with the id prefix `col-` (full detail is in `08-color-media-digital.json`). The 15 modules requested (seven of them single-medium primers) became 16 because I split the digital primer into `col-digital-primer` and `col-digital-brushes-layers`. I added two more: `col-color-studies-master-copies` and `col-color-script-mood`.

How to read the `verified` flag in the JSON: true means I opened the URL (or saw it in search results) and the free claim was either stated on the page or the content was openly readable with no login or paywall. False means the page loaded but freeness, contents or terms were not clear, or the fetch was limited. Where freeness was not stated and I relied on an open page, `free_notes` says so.

Method note: the web search tool hit its shared budget part-way through. I did everything after that by opening URLs directly. That is why several names I would normally try to confirm (see Gaps) are missing rather than guessed.

## Which free structured courses cover each topic, and what is free versus paid?

### Takeaway
There is no single free course that spans colour, media and digital. The strongest free structured spine is Ctrl+Paint (digital painting and colour, hundreds of short videos, no ads), supported by Handprint (colour mixing), an Open University OpenLearn Create colour theory course (about 1 hour), and the manufacturers' own lessons for markers and pastels. The big-name teachers (Marco Bucci, Will Kemp, James Gurney) mostly give a free taste and sell the full course.

### Cited Findings
- Ctrl+Paint is "Learn digital painting, for free": a library of hundreds of streaming videos, about 5 minutes each, ordered like a school curriculum, with no ads or subscription; $10 videos in the store support it — [Ctrl+Paint home](https://www.ctrlpaint.com/)
- Ctrl+Paint is run by Matt Kohr (SCAD comic books graduate, former console-game concept artist); the site states no licence or reuse terms for its videos — [About](https://www.ctrlpaint.com/about-contact)
- Ctrl+Paint library sections relevant here: 1 Digital Painting 101 (8 videos), 8 The Brush Tool (27), 9 Basic Painting Techniques (32, includes Basic Rendering 1-4 and edge and shadow lessons), 11 Painting With Color (18: Color Relativity, Intro to Color Schemes, Guess That Color, Colored Lineart, Imaginary Landscape Thumbnails and others), 15 Uniquely Digital Techniques (Gradient Map, Color Correction pt. 1-5) — [Library](https://www.ctrlpaint.com/library)
- Ctrl+Paint also hosts a separate free 30-episode video series, the Concept Art Playbook (design challenges such as enemies, props, buildings, vehicles). It belongs more to a design/imagination group, so I did not use it here — [Concept Art Playbook](https://www.ctrlpaint.com/concept-art-playbook)
- Ctrl+Paint's FAQ says a stylus is needed, recommends Wacom and says even the cheapest Wacom is enough for basic painting; it does not mention Krita, GIMP or Photoshop, so I could not confirm which software the demos use — [FAQ](https://www.ctrlpaint.com/faq)
- Marco Bucci, Proko: "Color Theory Basics for Digital Painters" is a free 6 min 41 s lesson covering hue, saturation and value, with a premium version that has extra content — [Proko lesson](https://proko.com/course-lesson/color-theory-basics-for-digital-painters)
- Marco Bucci's full colour course, The Color Survival Guide, costs $79, has 11 lessons (4 h 23 min) and one free lesson — [Proko course](https://proko.com/course/the-color-survival-guide/comments); its final lesson, Light & Color Integration (22:11), is premium only — [Proko lesson](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/chapter-6b-light-and-color-integration)
- Bucci's "Warm vs. Cool Colors" (25 min) is premium only and sits inside a $59 course, Illustrating Children's Books (and beyond!) — [Proko lesson](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/warm-vs-cool-colors/notes)
- Bucci's YouTube channel offers free painting fundamentals and demos, and says more lessons are sold at his store. I could not list playlists — [Channel](https://www.youtube.com/@MarcoBucci)
- Will Kemp Art School: free items are a 50-page acrylics PDF, newsletter extras, free acrylic videos and blog articles on drawing and acrylic painting. Paid courses cover drawing, acrylic, oil and water-mixable oil. Watercolour was not mentioned on the pages I read, so Kemp is a weak fit for the watercolour module — [Guide](https://willkempartschool.com/guide-to-will-kemp-art-school/) and [home page](https://www.willkempartschool.com/)
- Will Kemp's free article On the Wisdom of Colour Schemes (about 12 minutes) walks through seven restricted palettes for repeated still-life studies — [Article](https://willkempartschool.com/on-the-wisdom-of-colour-schemes/)
- James Gurney: Gurney Journey is a free daily blog with large labels (Watercolor Painting 355 posts, Color 248, Gouache 229, Plein Air Painting 220); he also has a Substack, Paint Here, with optional paid posts, and sells books and videos including Color and Light — [Gurney Journey](https://gurneyjourney.blogspot.com/) and [Substack](https://jamesgurney.substack.com/)
- Gurney's gouache video Gouache in the Wild is a paid product (sold via Sellfy and Gumroad); a free third-party review describes its six filmed plein air sessions — [Brad Teare review](https://bradteare.com/240-painting-with-gouache/)
- Gurney's gamut masking method is summarised for free in a Lines and Colors post by Charley Parker, which links to Gurney's three-part series — [Lines and Colors](https://linesandcolors.com/2011/09/19/james-gurney-on-gamut-masking/)
- Krita's official manual is free and covers Getting Started, a User Manual, Tutorials (Inking, Flat Coloring, Common Workflows, brush tips), a Reference Manual and General Concepts including colour pages (Mixing Colors, Color Models, Color Managed Workflow and others); this edition covers Krita 5.3 — [Krita manual](https://docs.krita.org/en/), [Tutorials](https://docs.krita.org/en/tutorials.html), [Colors section](https://docs.krita.org/en/general_concepts/colors.html)
- Krita Artists forum member Tamas Molnar posted a 7-video Krita beginners series (installation, brushes, layers, selections, saving) — [Forum thread](https://krita-artists.org/t/krita-beginners-tutorial-series/18521)
- David Revoy's Krita A-to-Z illustration tutorial is a free 1 h 22 min video published 15 August 2022 under CC BY 4.0 (his art carries an AI-derivation prohibition) — [Tutorial page](https://www.davidrevoy.com/article924/tutorial-an-illustration-from-a-to-z-with-krita)
- Sinix Design is a YouTube channel started in 2006 (tutorials and speedpaintings, digital painting). I could not confirm any structured course, playlist or what is paid — [Channel](https://www.youtube.com/@SinixDesign)
- Jazza's channel (drawing, animation, how-to-draw content per its keywords) exists, but I could not confirm any colour, Krita or structured-course playlist, or what he sells — [Channel](https://www.youtube.com/@Jazza)
- Open University, OpenLearn Create: A Colour Theory General Ideas is a beginner (Level 0) course of about 1 hour, free with a free account, free Statement of Participation and badge; it belongs to a 14-course collection called An Introduction to Plastic Arts — [Course](https://www.open.edu/openlearncreate/course/view.php?id=5330)
- Open University, OpenLearn Create: Drawing and Coloring Essentials is a beginner course of 24 study hours (coloured pencil and pastel exercises, perspective, objects, figure, portraits, still life, landscapes, quiz) — [Course](https://www.open.edu/openlearncreate/course/view.php?id=4322)
- The main OpenLearn Design Hub listing showed only two free design-process courses (Design, 28 hours; Design thinking, 10 hours) on its first page and none on colour or drawing — [Design Hub](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/design-hub)
- RMIT Open Press (Melbourne): Colour Theory: Understanding and Working with Colour by Lisa Cianci, 2023, CC BY-NC 4.0, covering history of colour theory, how colour is perceived, colour systems for mixing and relationships, with quizzes and activities — [Book](https://rmit.pressbooks.pub/colourtheory1/)
- Handprint (Bruce MacEvoy) has a long colour section: Color wheels, Basic mixing method, Mixing with a color wheel, An artist's color wheel, Tonal value, Color temperature, Color harmony and color design, Learning color through paints (27 study topics), plus watercolor mixing complements — [Handprint colour index](https://www.handprint.com/HP/WCL/wcolor.html)
- Datacolor offers a free 5-part colour course (about 72 minutes in total, sessions of 11-17 minutes) that is colour-science for industry (colour coordinates, tolerances, instruments); the instructor is not named and signup is via a form — [Datacolor course](https://datacolor.com/?p=182075). I did not use it in the JSON because it is aimed at colour measurement, not drawing.
- Traditional media: Copic's official how-to page (nibs, layering, gradation, colourless blender) and an 11-video official YouTube playlist — [Copic page](https://copic.jp/en/howto/marker/), [Playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PL0PpH6TSR87vxaiCXqQThtmvbUUVgnKSq)
- Unison Colour's Soft Pastels for Beginners, by Rebecca de Mendonça, is a free PDF (name and email required) — [Guide](https://www.unisoncolour.com/op/introduction-to-soft-pastels/)
- The Virtual Instructor's charcoal lesson (author Matt Fussell) is free; other items on the site need membership — [Lesson](https://thevirtualinstructor.com/charcoal.html)
- Alphonso Dunn's channel gives free pen and ink, watercolour, urban sketching and figure drawing tutorials; his books are sold separately — [Channel](https://www.youtube.com/@AlphonsoDunn)
- The Mind of Watercolor is a free YouTube channel by Steve Mitchell (designer and illustrator, 30-plus years) — [Channel](https://www.youtube.com/@mindofwatercolor)
- Ann Kullberg's coloured pencil site gives a free sample issue and a free colours workbook help session video, but most items are paid (magazine issue $5.40, webinars $25, classes $45-$75) — [annkullberg.com](https://annkullberg.com/)
- Museum colour/materials resources: Tate glossary pages on charcoal, ink and pastel are free to read — [Charcoal](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/c/charcoal), [Ink](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/i/ink), [Pastel](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/art-terms/p/pastel). ColourLex (paintings, pigment lexicon and timelines) and WebExhibits Pigments through the Ages exist but their pages did not state cost — [ColourLex](https://www.colourlex.com/), [Pigments](http://webexhibits.org/pigments/)
- CLIP STUDIO TIPS carries artist-written tutorials in several languages and official Clip Studio tutorials; terms of reading were not stated — [TIPS](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us)
- Muddy Colors is a free resource run by a collective of more than 20 artists with a new article daily — [Muddy Colors](https://www.muddycolors.com/)

### Inferences
- For a beginner on a zero budget, the least-paid path is: OpenLearn colour theory (1 hour) → Handprint mixing pages → Ctrl+Paint sections 11 and 9 → Gurney Journey for light and colour. The paid layer (Bucci's Color Survival Guide at $79, Gurney's Color and Light) is optional.
- Free traditional-media lessons are mostly manufacturer-made (Copic, Unison, Derwent) or one-teacher YouTube channels (Dunn, Mitchell, Gurney). That is useful but brand-biased; tell the learner that technique transfers across brands.
- The Open University material is generic and introductory; it suits a learner who wants an academic-style quiz and badge more than a deep technical course.
- Will Kemp is better treated as a source for limited palettes and still-life habits than as a general free art school, since his free content is mainly acrylic and drawing.

### Gaps
- Sinix Design and Jazza: I could confirm the channels exist but not any named series or what is paid.
- Marco Bucci's YouTube playlist names: the channel page returned no playlist list.
- Gurney Journey label pages (Color, Gouache) refuse automated fetches, so I cited the blog home, which lists the label counts, and told the learner to use the sidebar.
- Handprint's watercolor library index (wcl.html) failed to load; I used only the colour index page I could open.
- Other courses in the OpenLearn Create An Introduction to Plastic Arts collection: not listed on the pages I opened.
- Pixar in a Box (Khan Academy) and any animation-studio colour-script material: the pages returned only a title, so I excluded them.
- No scientific-illustration course found beyond the Biodiversity Heritage Library as a reference source.
- No non-English teaching source was fully confirmed; CLIP STUDIO TIPS (multi-language articles), the bilingual Copic playlist and the Japanese-origin ibisPaint and MediBang apps are the nearest.

## What is the free software landscape in October 2026?

### Takeaway
Krita is the best all-round free painting program (current 5.3.4.1, GPL v3, Windows/macOS/Linux/Android, no iPad). GIMP, Blender and FireAlpaca are free and current; MediBang Paint and ibisPaint are free-to-start apps with premium tiers and cover iPad and iPhone; Photopea is a free browser editor. Sketchbook's status is unclear.

### Cited Findings
- Krita 5.3.4.1 was released on 7 October 2026 per the download page, which also lists a 6.0.4 source tarball. Licence GNU GPL v3. Platforms: Windows 8.1+, macOS 10.15+, 64-bit Linux AppImage plus community Flatpak and snap, Android via Google Play and APK. iPad and iPhone are explicitly unsupported. Paid copies exist on Microsoft Store, Steam, Epic and the Mac App Store, with part of the proceeds funding development — [Krita download](https://krita.org/en/download/)
- Krita FAQ: not available for iOS/iPadOS due to GPL v3 and App Store licensing; Android "in beta" (this conflicts with the download page, which does not call it beta, so check the current state); minimum OpenGL 3.2, 2 GB RAM (4 GB preferred); a pressure-sensitive tablet is strongly advised; the Steam and Windows Store builds are the same binaries as the free download — [Krita FAQ](https://docs.krita.org/en/KritaFAQ.html)
- Krita's Artistic Color Selector docker includes gamut masks (managed in a Gamut Masks docker) — [Manual](https://docs.krita.org/en/reference_manual/dockers/artistic_color_selector.html)
- GIMP: the home page shows version 3.2.6 (news item dated 2026-09-10) and describes it as free software; the About page says it is developed under the GNU General Public License — [GIMP home](https://www.gimp.org/), [About](https://www.gimp.org/about/). Conflict: the downloads page I opened still said 2.10.38 (2024-05-02), probably a stale or cached page; prefer the home page — [Downloads](https://www.gimp.org/downloads/). Documentation for GIMP 3.2 is in 34 languages at [docs.gimp.org](https://docs.gimp.org/)
- Blender 5.2.2 LTS (15 September 2026), "free and open source"; macOS Apple Silicon only (macOS 13+), Windows and Linux builds — [Blender download](https://www.blender.org/download/). Licence: GPL (source GPL v2 or later; binaries GPL v3 or later), and what you create is yours — [Licence page](https://www.blender.org/about/license/)
- FireAlpaca: version 2.16.3 (news 10 September 2026) and a new Grain Pen brush announced 9 October 2026; "a free paint tool" for Windows and Mac; a separate FireAlpaca SE is promoted — [FireAlpaca](https://firealpaca.com/)
- MediBang Paint: page titles call it "the free digital painting and manga creation software"; the site lists Windows/Mac, iPad, iPhone and Android; a MediBang Premium service offers unlimited exclusive brushes and materials. Prices, storage and ads were not stated on the pages I read — [MediBang](https://medibangpaint.com/en/), [PC page](https://medibangpaint.com/en/pc/)
- ibisPaint: the site says it works on Windows, Mac, iPhone/iPad and Android and lists a Premium Membership; free features, ads and brush counts were not stated on the pages I read — [ibisPaint](https://ibispaint.com/product.jsp)
- Photopea: a free browser photo editor with layers and masks that opens and saves PSD files — [Photopea](https://www.photopea.com/)
- Sketchbook: the site footer reads Sketchbook, Inc. (2021-2026); Sketchbook Pro for Windows/Mac is labelled "Purchase" and the mobile apps "Download", with no prices or free tier stated — [Sketchbook](https://www.sketchbook.com/)
- Tablets: Ctrl+Paint's FAQ says a stylus (not a mouse) is essential and recommends Wacom — [FAQ](https://www.ctrlpaint.com/faq); Krita's FAQ covers WinTab drivers or Windows Ink setup — [Krita FAQ](https://docs.krita.org/en/KritaFAQ.html)
- Krita file formats: working formats with layers (kra, psd, ora, exr) are heavier; png and jpg are the shareable options — [File formats](https://docs.krita.org/en/general_concepts/file_formats.html)

### Inferences
- Default recommendation: Krita on a desktop or laptop. If the learner uses an iPad, MediBang Paint or ibisPaint are the free-to-start options, because Krita is not available there.
- Blender is useful here only as a reference tool (posing a light and a model to see colour and shadow behaviour), not as a drawing program.
- The Android and GIMP version conflicts mean the writer should say "check the current download page" instead of quoting a version as permanent.

### Gaps
- MyPaint: the page returned only metadata, so I did not include it.
- Free versus ad-supported details for MediBang and ibisPaint were not stated on the pages I could read; I set those resources aside from the main module resources and noted them here.
- I did not check Krita's Android listing separately from its download page, so its beta status is unresolved.
- Autodesk Sketchbook's current ownership/pricing is unclear from the site; I would not recommend it as a free option without checking.

## What free practice material exists (palettes, colour studies, public-domain paintings)?

### Takeaway
There is plenty: four major museums release public-domain paintings as CC0 or free-for-any-use downloads, large palette libraries are free, and Copic and Inktober offer free line art and a daily-drawing structure. A free gallery in Melbourne makes in-person colour study possible.

### Cited Findings
- The Met: images of public-domain works are CC0 (more than 375,000 images announced in 2017); you can filter the collection to public-domain works — [Met Open Access](https://www.metmuseum.org/perspectives/open-access-at-the-met)
- National Gallery of Art, Washington: images are free for any use, commercial or not; the open data set of 130,000+ artworks is CC0; more than 60,000 images can be downloaded — [NGA Open Access](https://www.nga.gov/open-access-images.html)
- Cleveland Museum of Art: high-resolution images and data are CC0, searchable with a CC0 filter, with a no-key API — [CMA Open Access](https://www.clevelandart.org/open-access)
- Smithsonian Open Access: more than 5.2 million 2D and 3D items, with a "Just browsing" link that filters to CC0 media; its art museums include the Smithsonian American Art Museum — [Smithsonian Open Access](https://www.si.edu/openaccess)
- Rijksmuseum: freely available works can be downloaded in high resolution for personal and commercial use; the page I could read did not mention CC0 — [Rijksmuseum](https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/about-collection-online)
- Biodiversity Heritage Library: free access to biodiversity literature (illustrated natural-history books), hosted with major support from Smithsonian Libraries and Archives; image licensing was not stated on the page I read, so check each item — [BHL](https://www.biodiversitylibrary.org/)
- Lospec Palette List: over 2,500 pixel-art palettes free to download (the listing showed 4,530 results), with filters by colour count and formats including PNG, PAL, ASE, Paint.NET TXT, GIMP GPL and HEX — [Lospec](https://lospec.com/palette-list)
- Coolors: free tier allows 10 saved palettes, 1 project, 5 favourite colours with ads; Pro (a pop-up mentioned $3 a month) adds picking colours from images and contrast checks — [Coolors](https://coolors.co/)
- Paletton: free colour scheme designer based on the classic red-yellow-blue wheel, funded by ads and donations — [Paletton](https://paletton.com/)
- Adobe Color: palette creation and extraction from images is offered inside Adobe Express; the page says automatically applying palettes to designs needs a paid plan, and did not say if saving requires an account — [Adobe Color](https://color.adobe.com/)
- Copic Line Art: roughly 100 free line drawings for colouring practice, personal use only, plus colour charts; the page advises laser printing because inkjet lines may disappear under marker — [Copic line art](https://copic.jp/en/coloring/)
- Inktober: Jake Parker's 31 days, 31 drawings challenge, founded 2009, with Classic and Inktober52 formats; cost and 2026 prompts were not stated on the page I read — [Inktober](https://inktober.com/)
- NGV International in Melbourne lists free entry, open daily 10am-5pm, with entry fees possible for special exhibitions; sketching permission was not stated — [NGV International](https://www.ngv.vic.gov.au/visit/ngv-international/)
- Urban Sketchers lists teaching, learning and prompts pages and local chapters — [Urban Sketchers](https://www.urbansketchers.org/)
- Gurney's gamut mask method has a free interactive tool mentioned in a reader comment on LivePaintingLessons.com; I did not open it and have not used it — [Lines and Colors comment thread](https://linesandcolors.com/2011/09/19/james-gurney-on-gamut-masking/)

### Inferences
- Colour studies from CC0 museum images are legally safe to download, copy and share as practice work. The Met, NGA, Cleveland and Smithsonian all state CC0 or free-for-any-use terms; the Rijksmuseum statement is looser.
- Museum screen images vary in colour accuracy, so the learner should treat them as references for relationships, not as true colour (included as a common mistake in the colour-studies module).

### Gaps
- Art Institute of Chicago: the public-domain page failed to load and its open-access page did not mention CC0, so I excluded it.
- Wikimedia Commons Google Art Project: I did not open it.
- No dedicated free "colour-study challenge" list (like a daily palette challenge) was confirmed; the practice items describe the exercise types instead.
